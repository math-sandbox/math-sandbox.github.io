8.5 Practical applications of connected rates of change

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 8 Further differentiation, printed pp.230–234. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook use of formulae for area and volume with the chain rule to connect rates in practical problems.

Key words: connected rates of change, rate of change

Connected rates of change

In practical problems two quantities are often linked by a formula, such as the area and radius of a circle. When one changes with time the other does too, and their rates are called connected rates of change. The chain rule links them, for example

\[\frac{dV}{dt}=\frac{dV}{dr}\times\frac{dr}{dt}.\]

A method

  • Write down the formula connecting the two quantities.
  • Differentiate it to get the derivative that links them.
  • Write the chain rule with the given rate of change and the one you want.
  • Substitute the values at the instant in question, and include units.

Useful formulae: circle \(A=\pi r^2\); sphere \(V=\tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3\) and surface area \(4\pi r^2\); cube \(V=x^3\).

Worked example 1: A spreading ripple

Coursebook, 8.5, area of a circle. New numbers.

The radius of a circular ripple increases at 2 cm per second. Find the rate of increase of its area when the radius is 5 cm.

  1. \(A=\pi r^2\), \(\dfrac{dA}{dr}=2\pi r=10\pi\) B1
  2. \(\dfrac{dA}{dt}=10\pi\times2\) M1
  3. \(=20\pi=62.8\) cm\(^2\) per second A1

Answer: \(20\pi\approx62.8\) cm\(^2\) per second

Worked example 2: A growing sphere

Coursebook, 8.5, volume of a sphere. New numbers.

The volume of a sphere increases at 100 cm\(^3\) per second. Find the rate of increase of the radius when the radius is 5 cm.

  1. \(\dfrac{dV}{dr}=4\pi r^2=100\pi\) B1
  2. \(\dfrac{dr}{dt}=\dfrac{100}{100\pi}\) M1
  3. \(=\dfrac{1}{\pi}=0.318\) cm per second A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{1}{\pi}\approx0.318\) cm per second

Common mistakes

  • Using the volume formula's derivative upside down: \(\dfrac{dr}{dt}=\dfrac{dV}{dt}\div\dfrac{dV}{dr}\).
  • Substituting the value of r into the formula before differentiating.
  • Leaving out the units of a rate.

Past paper

9709/11/O/N/20 Q3, official QP and official MS.

Air is being pumped into a balloon in the shape of a sphere so that its volume is increasing at a constant rate of 50 cm\(^3\) s\(^{-1}\).

Find the rate at which the radius of the balloon is increasing when the radius is 10 cm. [3]

Mark scheme
  1. \(\dfrac{dV}{dr}=4\pi r^2=400\pi\) B1 Official MS: B1, 4πr² (400π), SOI; award for dr/dV.
  2. \(\dfrac{dr}{dt}=\dfrac{50}{400\pi}\) M1 Official MS: M1, 50 ÷ their derivative.
  3. \(=\dfrac{1}{8\pi}=0.0398\) cm s\(^{-1}\) A1 Official MS: A1, 1/(8π) or 0.0398 AWRT.

Past paper

9709/11/O/N/12 Q3, official QP and official MS.

An oil pipeline under the sea is leaking oil and a circular patch of oil has formed on the surface of the sea. At midday the radius of the patch of oil is 50 m and is increasing at a rate of 3 metres per hour. Find the rate at which the area of the oil is increasing at midday. [4]

Mark scheme
  1. \(A=\pi r^2\), \(\dfrac{dA}{dr}=2\pi r\) B1 Official MS: B1, dA/dr = 2πr.
  2. \(\dfrac{dA}{dt}=\dfrac{dA}{dr}\times\dfrac{dr}{dt}\) M1 Official MS: M1, chain rule used.
  3. \(\dfrac{dr}{dt}=3\) B1 Official MS: B1, 3 soi.
  4. \(\dfrac{dA}{dt}=2\pi\times50\times3=300\pi\) (942) m\(^2\) per hour A1 Official MS: A1, 300π (or 942).

Quick check 1

The volume of a spherical balloon is increasing at a constant rate of 50 cm\(^3\) per second. Find the rate at which the radius is increasing when the radius is 5 cm.

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{dV}{dr}=4\pi\times25=100\pi\)
  2. \(\dfrac{dr}{dt}=\dfrac{50}{100\pi}\)

Answer: \(0.159\) cm per second (3 s.f.)

Quick check 2

The radius of a circular ripple increases at a constant rate of 3 cm per second. Find the rate at which the area of the circle is increasing when the radius is 10 cm.

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{dA}{dr}=20\pi\)
  2. \(\dfrac{dA}{dt}=20\pi\times3=60\pi\)

Answer: \(188\) cm\(^2\) per second (3 s.f.)

Quick check 3

The edges of a cube are increasing at a constant rate of 0.2 cm per second. Find the rate at which the volume is increasing when the edge length is 4 cm.

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{dV}{dx}=3\times16=48\)
  2. \(48\times0.2\)

Answer: \(9.6\) cm\(^3\) per second
