9.1 Displacement or translation vectors

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 9 Vectors, printed pp.211–219. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook notation for vectors in two and three dimensions.

Key words: vector, magnitude, parallel vectors

Vectors and their components

A vector has both magnitude (size) and direction. A displacement in three dimensions can be written as a column vector or with the unit vectors \(\mathbf{i}\), \(\mathbf{j}\), \(\mathbf{k}\) along the \(x\)-, \(y\)- and \(z\)-axes:

\[\mathbf{v}=\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\\c\end{pmatrix}=a\mathbf{i}+b\mathbf{j}+c\mathbf{k}.\]

Vectors are added and subtracted component by component, and multiplied by a scalar by multiplying every component.

Magnitude

By Pythagoras in three dimensions, the magnitude of \(\mathbf{v}\) is \(\lvert \mathbf{v}\rvert =\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}\).

Parallel vectors

Two vectors are parallel when one is a scalar multiple of the other: \(\mathbf{u}=k\mathbf{v}\) for some number \(k\). If \(k\gt 0\) they point the same way; if \(k\lt 0\) they point in opposite directions.

A unit vector has magnitude 1. The unit vector in the direction of \(\mathbf{v}\) is \(\dfrac{\mathbf{v}}{\lvert \mathbf{v}\rvert }\).

Worked example 1: Combining vectors

Coursebook, 9.1, operations on vectors. New numbers.

\(\mathbf{a}=2\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}+3\mathbf{k}\) and \(\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{i}+4\mathbf{j}-\mathbf{k}\). Find \(2\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\) and its magnitude.

  1. \(2\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}=4\mathbf{i}-2\mathbf{j}+6\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{i}-4\mathbf{j}+\mathbf{k}=3\mathbf{i}-6\mathbf{j}+7\mathbf{k}\) B1
  2. \(\lvert 2\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\rvert =\sqrt{9+36+49}=\sqrt{94}\) M1
  3. \(=9.70\) (3 s.f.) A1

Answer: \(3\mathbf{i}-6\mathbf{j}+7\mathbf{k}\), \(\sqrt{94}=9.70\)

Worked example 2: A unit vector

Coursebook, 9.1, unit vectors. New numbers.

Find the unit vector in the direction of \(\mathbf{v}=4\mathbf{i}+4\mathbf{j}-7\mathbf{k}\).

  1. \(\lvert \mathbf{v}\rvert =\sqrt{16+16+49}=9\) M1
  2. Unit vector \(=\dfrac{1}{9}(4\mathbf{i}+4\mathbf{j}-7\mathbf{k})\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{1}{9}(4\mathbf{i}+4\mathbf{j}-7\mathbf{k})\)

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to square negative components as positive numbers when finding a magnitude.
  • Using different multiples for different components when checking whether vectors are parallel.
  • Writing a vector without its direction, for example giving only its magnitude.

Past paper practice for these skills is in subsections 9.2 to 9.5.

Quick check 1

Find the magnitude of \(\begin{pmatrix}2\\-3\\6\end{pmatrix}\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\sqrt{4+9+36}=\sqrt{49}\)

Answer: \(7\)

Quick check 2

The vectors \(\begin{pmatrix}1\\3\\2\end{pmatrix}\) and \(\begin{pmatrix}3\\p\\6\end{pmatrix}\) are parallel. Find \(p\).

Worked solution
  1. The second vector is 3 times the first.
  2. \(p=3\times3\)

Answer: \(p=9\)

Quick check 3

The vector \(\begin{pmatrix}t\\2\\4\end{pmatrix}\), with \(t\gt 0\), has magnitude \(\sqrt{36}\). Find \(t\).

Worked solution
  1. \(t^2+4+16=36\), so \(t^2=16\).

Answer: \(t=4\)
