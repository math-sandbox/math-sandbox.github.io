1.2 Representation of discrete data: stem-and-leaf diagrams

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 1 Coursebook (Dean Chalmers), Chapter 1 Representation of data, printed pp.3–5 (Key point 1.2, Worked example 1.1, Exercise 1A). Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: stem-and-leaf diagram, stem, leaf, key, back-to-back stem-and-leaf diagram

Building a stem-and-leaf diagram

A stem-and-leaf diagram is a table that is well suited to small amounts of discrete data. The last digit of each data value appears as a leaf, attached to all the other digits, which appear in a stem. The stem digits are ordered vertically. The leaf digits are ordered horizontally, with the smallest digit placed nearest to the stem.

Each row is a class of values. The rows should have intervals of equal width, so that two sets of data can be compared by eye. A key, with the appropriate unit, must be included to explain what the values represent. The raw data can still be read from the diagram. If two related sets of data are to be compared, they can be shown in a back-to-back stem-and-leaf diagram.

Key point. Data in a stem-and-leaf diagram are ordered in rows of equal widths. A key must be given.

The finished diagram should have a bar-chart-like shape, which is obtained by lining the leaves up in columns. It is wise to work in pencil, or to redraw the diagram if a leaf is out of place.

Worked example 1: A single stem-and-leaf diagram

Book: page 3 (Physics scores construction); new list

The percentage scores of 12 students in a Chemistry test are 47, 51, 48, 63, 55, 71, 66, 52, 74, 59, 60 and 45. Construct a stem-and-leaf diagram for these scores, and include a key.

  1. Group the scores into equal-width classes 40–49, 50–59, 60–69 and 70–79, so the stem is 4, 5, 6, 7. B1
  2. Write the leaves in order, nearest to the stem first, and give a key. B1

The ordered diagram is $$\begin{array}{r|l} 4 & 5\ 7\ 8\\ 5 & 1\ 2\ 5\ 9\\ 6 & 0\ 3\ 6\\ 7 & 1\ 4 \end{array} \qquad\text{Key: }4\mid 5\text{ represents a score of }45\%.$$

Answer: the diagram above, with the key

Worked example 2: A back-to-back diagram

Book: Worked example 1.1, p.4; new numbers (two clubs, not calendar years)

The numbers of matches won by two clubs, North and South, in each of 10 seasons are as follows.

North: 17, 20, 13, 12, 10, 8, 1, 5, 11, 16.

South: 9, 13, 11, 8, 6, 3, 1, 2, 4, 7.

Display the data in a back-to-back stem-and-leaf diagram, and compare the numbers of wins.

  1. Use the same equal-width classes 0–9, 10–19 and 20–29 for both clubs. On the left of the stem the leaves increase towards the stem; on the right they increase away from the stem. Include a key. B1
  2. North has 113 wins in total and South has 64 wins, so North won more matches in total. No information is given about the margins of victory, so it would be a mistake to say that North played better in every other sense. B1

The diagram is $$\begin{array}{r|c|l} \text{North (10)} & & \text{South (10)}\\ \hline 8\ 5\ 1 & 0 & 1\ 2\ 3\ 4\ 6\ 7\ 8\ 9\\ 7\ 6\ 3\ 2\ 1\ 0 & 1 & 1\ 3\\ 0 & 2 & \end{array} \qquad\text{Key: }5\mid 0\mid 6\text{ represents }5\text{ wins for North and }6\text{ wins for South.}$$

Answer: the diagram above; North won more matches in total (113 compared with 64)

In a back-to-back diagram, leaves to the right of the stem ascend from left to right, and leaves to the left of the stem ascend from right to left (so the smallest leaf is always nearest to the stem). If a row would be very long, the stem values may be repeated to split a class into narrower intervals such as 0–4 and 5–9, but that is not needed for the numbers above.

Worked example 3: Reading a stem-and-leaf diagram

Book: Exercise 1A Q3 style, p.4–5; new diagram

The stem-and-leaf diagram shows the number of employees at 16 companies. $$\begin{array}{r|l} 1 & 2\ 4\ 4\ 7\\ 2 & 0\ 1\ 5\ 5\ 8\\ 3 & 1\ 3\ 3\ 6\\ 4 & 0\ 2\ 9 \end{array} \qquad\text{Key: }1\mid 2\text{ represents }12\text{ employees.}$$ How many of the companies have fewer than 25 employees, and what percentage of the companies have more than 30 employees?

  1. The values less than 25 are 12, 14, 14, 17, 20 and 21, so 6 companies have fewer than 25 employees. B1
  2. The values greater than 30 are 31, 33, 33, 36, 40, 42 and 49, so 7 of the 16 companies, which is \(\dfrac{7}{16}=43.75\%\), have more than 30 employees. M1 A1

Answer: 6 companies; \(43.75\%\)

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the leaves unordered, or forgetting the key and its unit.
  • On the left of a back-to-back diagram, writing the leaves so that they increase away from the stem instead of towards it.
  • Using rows of unequal width, which makes visual comparison unreliable.
  • Reading a comparison of counts (for example, days on which an event occurred) as if it were a comparison of a different quantity that was never recorded.

Quick checks

Quick check 1

The scores of 9 players, already written in a stem-and-leaf diagram, are 23, 25, 27, 31, 32, 34, 36, 41 and 45. How many of these scores are less than 35?

Worked solution
  1. The scores less than 35 are 23, 25, 27, 31, 32 and 34, so there are 6 such scores.

Answer: \(6\)

Quick check 2

Using the companies diagram in Worked example 3, what is the most common number of employees? (If two values appear equally often, give the smaller one.)

Worked solution
  1. The values 14, 25 and 33 each appear twice, and no value appears more often. The smallest of these is 14.

Answer: \(14\)

Quick check 3

Using the same companies diagram, how many employees work at the company with the greatest number of employees?

Worked solution
  1. The greatest value in the diagram is 49.

Answer: \(49\)

Quick check 4

A stem-and-leaf diagram for 20 values has four leaves in the first row, seven in the second and nine in the third. How many values are represented in total?

Worked solution
  1. \(4+7+9=20\).

Answer: \(20\)

Marks: M = method, A = accurate answer (depends on M), B = independent mark. Marks on worked examples are illustrative, not official. Give exact integers or exact percentages unless a question says otherwise. Leaves must be ordered, rows must have equal width, and a key with units is required.
