8.6 Integration by parts

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 8 Further calculus, printed pp.197–200. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook formula for integration by parts and its choice of the part to differentiate.

Key words: integration by parts, product rule

The formula

Integrating the product rule \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(uv)=u\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}+v\dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}\) and rearranging gives integration by parts:

\[\int u\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}\,\mathrm{d}x=uv-\int v\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}\,\mathrm{d}x.\]

Choosing u

Choose \(u\) so that \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}\) is simpler than \(u\), and so that \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}\) can be integrated.

  • For \(x\mathrm{e}^x\), \(x\sin x\) or \(x\cos x\), take \(u=x\).
  • When there is a logarithm, as in \(x\ln x\), take \(u=\ln x\), because \(\ln x\) is hard to integrate but easy to differentiate.
  • For \(\ln x\) or \(\tan^{-1}x\) on its own, write it as \(1\times\ln x\) and take \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}=1\).
  • For \(x^2\mathrm{e}^x\), use integration by parts twice.

For a definite integral: \(\displaystyle\int_a^bu\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}\,\mathrm{d}x=\bigl[uv\bigr]_a^b-\int_a^bv\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}\,\mathrm{d}x\).

Worked example 1: A trigonometric product

Coursebook, 8.6, integration by parts. New numbers.

Find \(\displaystyle\int x\cos x\,\mathrm{d}x\).

  1. \(u=x\), \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}=\cos x\), so \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}=1\) and \(v=\sin x\). M1
  2. \(x\sin x-\displaystyle\int\sin x\,\mathrm{d}x\) A1
  3. \(=x\sin x+\cos x+c\) A1

Answer: \(x\sin x+\cos x+c\)

Worked example 2: A logarithm

Coursebook, 8.6, integration by parts with ln x. New numbers.

Find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_1^{\mathrm{e}}x^2\ln x\,\mathrm{d}x\).

  1. \(u=\ln x\), \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}=x^2\): \(\dfrac{1}{3}x^3\ln x-\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{3}x^2\,\mathrm{d}x\) M1
  2. \(=\dfrac{1}{3}x^3\ln x-\dfrac{1}{9}x^3\) A1
  3. \(\Bigl[\dfrac{1}{3}x^3\ln x-\dfrac{1}{9}x^3\Bigr]_1^{\mathrm{e}}=\dfrac{\mathrm{e}^3}{3}-\dfrac{\mathrm{e}^3}{9}+\dfrac{1}{9}\) M1
  4. \(=\dfrac{2\mathrm{e}^3+1}{9}\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{2\mathrm{e}^3+1}{9}\)

Common mistakes

  • Choosing \(u=\mathrm{e}^x\) or \(u=\cos x\) in \(x\mathrm{e}^x\) or \(x\cos x\); the new integral is then harder.
  • Sign errors when \(v\) is negative, as in \(\displaystyle\int\sin x\,\mathrm{d}x=-\cos x\).
  • Using limits on \(uv\) but forgetting them on the second integral.

Mark scheme

Quick check 1

Use integration by parts to find the value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^1x\mathrm{e}^x\,\mathrm{d}x\), correct to 3 significant figures.

Worked solution
  1. \((x-1)\mathrm{e}^x\)
  2. \(0-(-1)\)

Answer: \(1\)

Quick check 2

Use integration by parts to find the value of \(\displaystyle\int_1^2x\ln x\,\mathrm{d}x\), correct to 3 significant figures.

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{1}{2}x^2\ln x-\dfrac{1}{4}x^2\)
  2. \(2\ln2-1+\dfrac{1}{4}\)

Answer: \(0.636\)

Quick check 3

Use integration by parts to find the value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^1x\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\,\mathrm{d}x\), correct to 3 significant figures.

Worked solution
  1. \(-\dfrac{x}{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2x}-\dfrac{1}{4}\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\)
  2. \(-\dfrac{3}{4}\mathrm{e}^{-2}+\dfrac{1}{4}\)

Answer: \(0.148\)
