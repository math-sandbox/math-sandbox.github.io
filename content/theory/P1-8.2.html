8.2 Stationary points

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 8 Further differentiation, printed pp.216–221. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook conditions for stationary points and the first and second derivative tests for their nature.

Key words: stationary point, maximum point, minimum point, second derivative

Finding stationary points

A stationary point is a point on a curve where the gradient is zero. To find stationary points, solve \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=0\) and substitute each \(x\) value into the equation of the curve to find \(y\).

Maximum and minimum points

At a maximum point the gradient changes from positive to zero to negative. At a minimum point it changes from negative to zero to positive. You can decide the nature by checking the sign of \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) just either side of the point.

A quicker method uses the second derivative \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\):

  • if \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\lt 0\) at the point, it is a maximum point;
  • if \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\gt 0\) at the point, it is a minimum point;
  • if \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=0\), this test fails, so use the gradient either side.

Worked example 1: Two stationary points

Coursebook, 8.2, second derivative test. New numbers.

Find the stationary points of \(y=x^3-6x^2+9x+1\) and determine their nature.

  1. \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=3x^2-12x+9=3(x-1)(x-3)=0\), so \(x=1\) or \(x=3\) M1
  2. Points \((1, 5)\) and \((3, 1)\) A1
  3. \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=6x-12\) M1
  4. At \(x=1\): \(-6\lt 0\), maximum; at \(x=3\): \(6\gt 0\), minimum A1

Answer: Maximum point \((1, 5)\), minimum point \((3, 1)\)

Worked example 2: A curve with a negative power

Coursebook, 8.2, stationary points. New numbers.

Find the stationary point of \(y=x+\dfrac{4}{x}\) for \(x\gt 0\) and determine its nature.

  1. \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=1-\dfrac{4}{x^2}=0\), so \(x^2=4\) and \(x=2\) M1
  2. \(y=2+2=4\) A1
  3. \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\dfrac{8}{x^3}=1\gt 0\) at \(x=2\), so minimum B1

Answer: Minimum point \((2, 4)\)

Common mistakes

  • Stopping at the \(x\)-coordinate when the question asks for the point.
  • Concluding that \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=0\) means a point of inflexion. It only means the test fails.
  • Losing the negative sign when differentiating \(\dfrac{4}{x}\).

Past paper

9709/11/O/N/12 Q5, official QP and official MS.

A curve has equation \(y=2x+\dfrac{1}{(x-1)^2}\). Verify that the curve has a stationary point at \(x=2\) and determine its nature. [5]

Mark scheme
  1. \(\dfrac{d}{dx}(x-1)^{-2}=-2(x-1)^{-3}\) B1 Official MS: B2,1,0 for dy/dx = 2 − 2(x − 1)^(−3), −1 each error in 2, −2, (x − 1)^(−3); recorded here as B1 + B1.
  2. \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2-2(x-1)^{-3}\) B1 Official MS: second mark of B2,1,0, fully correct derivative.
  3. \(x=2\): \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2-2\times1=0\), so there is a stationary point at \(x=2\) B1 Official MS: B1 AG, sub x = 2 into dy/dx giving 0, so stationary value at x = 2.
  4. \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=6(x-1)^{-4}\), and substitute \(x=2\) M1 Official MS: M1, reasonable attempt to differentiate the form (x − 1)^(−3) (and sub x = 2).
  5. At \(x=2\), \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=6>0\), so minimum A1 Official MS: A1, correct d²y/dx² and ‘minimum’ is required; or other valid method for the last 2 marks.

9709/11/O/N/22 Q3, official QP and official MS.

A curve has equation \(y=ax^{\frac{1}{2}}-2x\), where \(x>0\) and \(a\) is a constant. The curve has a stationary point at the point \(P\), which has \(x\)-coordinate 9.

Find the \(y\)-coordinate of \(P\). [5]

Mark scheme
  1. \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\bigl(ax^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)=\tfrac{1}{2}ax^{-\frac{1}{2}}\) B1 Official MS: B2,1,0 for dy/dx = ½ax^(−½) − 2; recorded here as B1 + B1.
  2. \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\tfrac{1}{2}ax^{-\frac{1}{2}}-2\) B1 Official MS: second mark of B2,1,0, fully correct derivative.
  3. \(\tfrac{1}{2}a\times9^{-\frac{1}{2}}-2=0\), i.e. \(\dfrac{a}{6}=2\) M1 Official MS: M1, set their dy/dx = 0 with x = 9 and solve a linear equation for a.
  4. \(a=12\) A1 Official MS: A1, a = 12.
  5. \(y=12\times9^{\frac{1}{2}}-18=18\) A1 Official MS: A1 FT, FT on their a; y = 18.

Quick check 1

The curve \(y=x^2-6x+5\) has one stationary point. Find the \(y\)-coordinate of this stationary point.

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2x-6=0\), \(x=3\)
  2. \(y=9-18+5\)

Answer: \(-4\)

Quick check 2

The curve \(y=x^3-12x+3\) has a maximum point and a minimum point. Find the \(y\)-coordinate of the maximum point.

Worked solution
  1. \(3x^2-12=0\), \(x=\pm2\); \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=6x\lt 0\) at \(x=-2\)
  2. \(y=-8+24+3\)

Answer: \(19\)

Quick check 3

The curve \(y=2x+\dfrac{18}{x}\), for \(x\gt 0\), has a minimum point. Find the \(y\)-coordinate of the minimum point.

Worked solution
  1. \(2-\dfrac{18}{x^2}=0\), \(x=3\)
  2. \(y=6+6\)

Answer: \(12\)
