8.1 Derivative of \(\tan^{-1}x\)

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 8 Further calculus, printed p.186. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook derivation of the derivative of the inverse tangent function.

Key words: inverse tangent, chain rule

The result

Let \(y=\tan^{-1}x\). Then \(x=\tan y\), so \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}y}=\sec^2y=1+\tan^2y=1+x^2\). Therefore

\[\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigl(\tan^{-1}x\bigr)=\frac{1}{1+x^2}.\]

Using the chain rule

For a function of \(x\) inside the inverse tangent, use the chain rule:

\[\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\Bigl(\tan^{-1}f(x)\Bigr)=\frac{f'(x)}{1+\bigl(f(x)\bigr)^2}.\]

For example, \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{x}{a}\right)=\dfrac{a}{a^2+x^2}\). The product and quotient rules work with \(\tan^{-1}x\) in the usual way. Angles are in radians, so \(\tan^{-1}1=\dfrac{\pi}{4}\).

Worked example 1: The chain rule

Coursebook, 8.1, derivative of the inverse tangent. New numbers.

Differentiate \(y=\tan^{-1}(3x)\).

  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{3}{1+(3x)^2}\) M1
  2. \(=\dfrac{3}{1+9x^2}\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{3}{1+9x^2}\)

Worked example 2: The product rule

Coursebook, 8.1, products with the inverse tangent. New numbers.

Find the exact gradient of the curve \(y=x^2\tan^{-1}x\) at the point where \(x=1\).

  1. Product rule: \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=2x\tan^{-1}x+x^2\times\dfrac{1}{1+x^2}\) M1
  2. \(=2x\tan^{-1}x+\dfrac{x^2}{1+x^2}\) A1
  3. At \(x=1\): \(2\times\dfrac{\pi}{4}+\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{\pi}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{\pi}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\)

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to multiply by the derivative of the inside function, for example writing \(\dfrac{1}{1+9x^2}\) for the derivative of \(\tan^{-1}(3x)\).
  • Writing \(1+3x^2\) instead of \(1+(3x)^2=1+9x^2\).
  • Using degrees: \(\tan^{-1}1\) is \(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\), not 45, in calculus.

Quick check 1

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=\tan^{-1}(2x)\) at the point where \(x=1\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{2}{1+4x^2}\)
  2. At \(x=1\): \(\dfrac{2}{5}\)

Answer: \(0.4\)

Quick check 2

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{x}{3}\right)\) at the point where \(x=4\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{3}{9+x^2}\)
  2. At \(x=4\): \(\dfrac{3}{25}\)

Answer: \(0.12\)

Quick check 3

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=\tan^{-1}(2x^2)\) at the point where \(x=1\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{4x}{1+4x^4}\)
  2. At \(x=1\): \(\dfrac{4}{5}\)

Answer: \(0.8\)
