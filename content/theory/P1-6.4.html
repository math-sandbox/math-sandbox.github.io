6.4 Geometric progressions

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 6 Series, printed pp.171–175. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook formulae for the \(n\)th term and the sum of the first \(n\) terms of a geometric progression.

Key words: geometric progression, common ratio

The \(n\)th term

A geometric progression is a sequence in which each term is found by multiplying the term before by the same number. That number is the common ratio, \(r\). With first term \(a\), the terms are \(a,\ ar,\ ar^2,\ \ldots\), so

\[u_n=ar^{n-1}.\]

The common ratio can be found by dividing any term by the term before it: \(r=\dfrac{u_{n+1}}{u_n}\).

The sum of the first \(n\) terms

Subtracting \(rS_n\) from \(S_n\) cancels all but two terms, which gives

\[S_n=\dfrac{a(1-r^n)}{1-r}=\dfrac{a(r^n-1)}{r-1},\qquad r\ne1.\]

The first form is convenient when \(\lvert r\rvert<1\) and the second when \(r>1\).

Worked example 1: A term and a sum

Coursebook, 6.4, \(n\)th term and sum formulae. New numbers.

The geometric progression \(3, 6, 12, \ldots\) is given. Find the 8th term and the sum of the first 8 terms.

  1. \(a=3\), \(r=2\), so \(u_8=3\times2^7=384\). B1
  2. \(S_8=\dfrac{3(2^8-1)}{2-1}\) M1
  3. \(=765\) A1

Answer: \(u_8=384\), \(S_8=765\)

Worked example 2: A ratio less than 1

Coursebook, 6.4, the sum formula with \(\lvert r\rvert<1\). New numbers.

A geometric progression has first term 64 and common ratio \(\dfrac{1}{2}\). Find the sum of the first 6 terms.

  1. \(S_6=\dfrac{64\left(1-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^6\right)}{1-\frac{1}{2}}\) M1
  2. \(=128\times\dfrac{63}{64}=126\) A1

Answer: \(126\)

Common mistakes

  • Using \(ar^n\) for the \(n\)th term; the correct formula is \(ar^{n-1}\).
  • Mixing up an arithmetic progression and a geometric progression: in an arithmetic progression a fixed amount is added, in a geometric progression the terms are multiplied by a fixed ratio.
  • Rounding the common ratio too early, which can change the answer to a later term a lot.

Quick check 1

A geometric progression has first term 4 and common ratio 3. Find the 5th term.

Worked solution
  1. \(u_5=4\times3^4=324\)

Answer: \(324\)

Quick check 2

A geometric progression has first term 2 and common ratio 3. Find the sum of the first 5 terms.

Worked solution
  1. \(S_5=\dfrac{2(3^5-1)}{3-1}=\dfrac{2\times242}{2}\)

Answer: \(242\)

Quick check 3

The second term of a geometric progression is 10 and the fifth term is 80. Find the first term.

Worked solution
  1. \(ar=10\) and \(ar^4=80\), so \(r^3=8\) and \(r=2\).
  2. \(a=\dfrac{10}{2}=5\)

Answer: \(5\)
