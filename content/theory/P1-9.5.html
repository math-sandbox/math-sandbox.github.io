9.5 Definite integration

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 9 Integration, printed pp.250–252. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook notation for definite integrals, square-bracket working and substituting the upper and lower limits.

Key words: definite integral, limits, integration

Definite integrals

If \(\mathrm{F}(x)\) is an integral of \(\mathrm{f}(x)\), the definite integral from \(a\) to \(b\) is

\[\int_a^b\mathrm{f}(x)\,dx=\Bigl[\mathrm{F}(x)\Bigr]_a^b=\mathrm{F}(b)-\mathrm{F}(a).\]

The numbers \(a\) and \(b\) are the lower and upper limits. The constant of integration cancels, so it is not written. A definite integral is a number.

Setting out

  • Integrate and write the result in square brackets with the limits.
  • Substitute the upper limit, then subtract the value at the lower limit. Use brackets: \(\mathrm{F}(b)-\bigl(\mathrm{F}(a)\bigr)\).
  • Show the working: many questions say 'showing all necessary working', so a calculator value alone scores no marks.

Worked example 1: A polynomial

Coursebook, 9.5, evaluating a definite integral. New numbers.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_1^3(x^2+2)\,dx\).

  1. \(\Bigl[\dfrac{x^3}{3}+2x\Bigr]_1^3\) B1
  2. \(=(9+6)-\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{3}+2\Bigr)\) M1
  3. \(=\dfrac{38}{3}\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{38}{3}\)

Worked example 2: A root

Coursebook, 9.5, a definite integral of (ax + b)^n. New numbers.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^4\sqrt{2x+1}\,dx\).

  1. \(\Bigl[\dfrac{(2x+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\frac{3}{2}\times2}\Bigr]_0^4=\Bigl[\dfrac{(2x+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{3}\Bigr]_0^4\) B1
  2. \(=\dfrac{27}{3}-\dfrac{1}{3}\) M1
  3. \(=\dfrac{26}{3}\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{26}{3}\)

Common mistakes

  • Subtracting the wrong way round: it is upper limit minus lower limit.
  • Assuming the value at a lower limit of 0 is 0: for \((2x+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}\) it is 1.
  • Substituting the limits into \(\mathrm{f}(x)\) instead of into the integral \(\mathrm{F}(x)\).
