11.2 Complex numbers

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 11 Complex numbers, printed pp.269–272. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook rules for adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing complex numbers, complex conjugates and equal complex numbers.

Key words: complex number, real part, imaginary part, complex conjugate

The form x + iy

A complex number is \(z=x+\mathrm{i}y\), where \(x\) and \(y\) are real. \(x\) is the real part, \(\mathrm{Re}\,z\), and \(y\) is the imaginary part, \(\mathrm{Im}\,z\). Note that the imaginary part is the real number \(y\), not \(\mathrm{i}y\).

Adding, subtracting and multiplying

Add or subtract the real parts and the imaginary parts separately. Multiply out brackets as in algebra and replace \(\mathrm{i}^2\) by \(-1\):

\[(a+b\mathrm{i})(c+d\mathrm{i})=(ac-bd)+(ad+bc)\mathrm{i}.\]

The complex conjugate

The complex conjugate of \(z=x+\mathrm{i}y\) is \(z^*=x-\mathrm{i}y\). The product \(zz^*=x^2+y^2\) is real. To divide by a complex number, multiply the numerator and the denominator by the conjugate of the denominator.

Equal complex numbers

Two complex numbers are equal only if their real parts are equal and their imaginary parts are equal. So one equation between complex numbers gives two equations between real numbers. This is how we solve equations such as \(z+2z^*=3+2\mathrm{i}\): substitute \(z=x+\mathrm{i}y\) and equate parts.

Worked example 1: Division

Coursebook, 11.2, dividing by multiplying by the conjugate. New numbers.

Express \(\dfrac{5+\mathrm{i}}{2-3\mathrm{i}}\) in the form \(x+\mathrm{i}y\).

  1. \(\dfrac{(5+\mathrm{i})(2+3\mathrm{i})}{(2-3\mathrm{i})(2+3\mathrm{i})}\) M1
  2. \(=\dfrac{10+15\mathrm{i}+2\mathrm{i}+3\mathrm{i}^2}{4+9}=\dfrac{7+17\mathrm{i}}{13}\) A1

Answer: \(\tfrac{7}{13}+\tfrac{17}{13}\mathrm{i}\)

Worked example 2: Equating parts

Coursebook, 11.2, equal complex numbers. New numbers.

Find the complex number \(z\) such that \(3z-2\mathrm{i}z^*=4+\mathrm{i}\).

  1. \(3(x+\mathrm{i}y)-2\mathrm{i}(x-\mathrm{i}y)=(3x-2y)+(3y-2x)\mathrm{i}\) B1
  2. \(3x-2y=4\) and \(-2x+3y=1\) M1
  3. \(x=\tfrac{14}{5}\), \(y=\tfrac{11}{5}\) A1

Answer: \(z=\tfrac{14}{5}+\tfrac{11}{5}\mathrm{i}\)

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting that \(\mathrm{i}^2=-1\) when multiplying out.
  • Multiplying only the denominator by the conjugate when dividing.
  • Writing \(z^*=-x+\mathrm{i}y\): only the sign of the imaginary part changes.

(a) Given the complex numbers \(u=a+\mathrm{i}b\) and \(w=c+\mathrm{i}d\), where \(a\), \(b\), \(c\) and \(d\) are real, prove that \((u+w)^*=u^*+w^*\). [2]

(b) Solve the equation \((z+2+\mathrm{i})^*+(2+\mathrm{i})z=0\), giving your answer in the form \(x+\mathrm{i}y\) where \(x\) and \(y\) are real. [4]

Mark scheme
  1. (a) \((u+w)^*=\bigl((a+c)+\mathrm{i}(b+d)\bigr)^*=(a+c)-\mathrm{i}(b+d)\) B1 Official MS: B1, substitute for u and w and state correct conjugate of one side.
  2. (a) \(u^*+w^*=(a-\mathrm{i}b)+(c-\mathrm{i}d)=(a+c)-\mathrm{i}(b+d)\), so \((u+w)^*=u^*+w^*\) B1 Official MS: B1, express the other side without conjugates and confirm (u + w)* = u* + w*; given answer, needs explicit reference to conjugate of both sides.
  3. (b) \(x+2-(y+1)\mathrm{i}+(2+\mathrm{i})(x+\mathrm{i}y)=0\) B1 Official MS: B1, substitute and remove conjugates to obtain a correct equation in x and y.
  4. (b) Use \(\mathrm{i}^2=-1\) and equate real and imaginary parts to zero M1 Official MS: M1.
  5. (b) \(3x-y+2=0\) and \(x+y-1=0\) A1 Official MS: A1, obtain two correct equations in x and y.
  6. (b) \(z=-\tfrac{1}{4}+\tfrac{5}{4}\mathrm{i}\) A1 Official MS: A1, solve and obtain answer z = −1/4 + 5/4 i; allow for real and imaginary parts stated separately.

Find the real part of \(\dfrac{1+3\mathrm{i}}{1+2\mathrm{i}}\).

Answer: \(\dfrac{7}{5}=1.40\)
