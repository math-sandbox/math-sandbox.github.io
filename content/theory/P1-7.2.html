7.2 The chain rule

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 7 Differentiation, printed pp.198–201. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook statement of the chain rule for composite functions.

Key words: chain rule, composite function, derivative

The chain rule

To differentiate a function of a function, such as \(y=(3x-1)^5\), let \(u=3x-1\), so that \(y=u^5\). Then

\[\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}u}\times\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}.\]

Here \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}u}=5u^4\) and \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}=3\), so \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=15(3x-1)^4\).

A quick form for linear inside functions

If \(y=(ax+b)^n\), then \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=an(ax+b)^{n-1}\). The same idea works for roots and reciprocals once they are written as powers: \(\sqrt{ax+b}=(ax+b)^{\frac{1}{2}}\) and \(\dfrac{1}{ax+b}=(ax+b)^{-1}\).

Worked example 1: A power of a quadratic

Coursebook, 7.2, chain rule. New numbers.

Differentiate \(y=(x^2+1)^4\).

  1. \(u=x^2+1\), \(y=u^4\) B1
  2. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=4u^3\times2x\) M1
  3. \(=8x(x^2+1)^3\) A1

Answer: \(8x(x^2+1)^3\)

Worked example 2: A reciprocal of a root

Coursebook, 7.2, chain rule with negative powers. New numbers.

Find the gradient of \(y=\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{2x+5}}\) at \(x=2\).

  1. \(y=4(2x+5)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\) B1
  2. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=4\times\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)(2x+5)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\times2=-4(2x+5)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\) M1
  3. At \(x=2\): \(-4\times9^{-\frac{3}{2}}=-\dfrac{4}{27}\) A1

Answer: \(-\dfrac{4}{27}\)

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to multiply by the derivative of the inside function.
  • Reducing the power wrongly for negative or fractional powers, for example \(-\frac{1}{2}-1=-\frac{3}{2}\).
  • Expanding a high power instead of using the chain rule.

Quick check 1

Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) for \(y=(2x+1)^3\) when \(x=1\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=6(2x+1)^2\)
  2. \(6\times3^2\)

Answer: \(54\)

Quick check 2

Find the gradient of \(y=\sqrt{2x+5}\) at \(x=2\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=(2x+5)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\)
  2. \(9^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\dfrac{1}{3}\)

Answer: \(\dfrac{1}{3}=0.333\) (3 s.f.)

Quick check 3

Find the gradient of \(y=\dfrac{6}{2x+1}\) at \(x=1\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=-12(2x+1)^{-2}\)
  2. \(-\dfrac{12}{9}\)

Answer: \(-\dfrac{4}{3}=-1.33\) (3 s.f.)
