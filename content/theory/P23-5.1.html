5.1 Integration of exponential functions

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, Julianne Hughes), Chapter 5 Integration, p.112 (Key point 5.1, Worked example 5.1) and Exercise 5A Q1, p.113. Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: reverse differentiation, exponential function, reverse chain rule, indefinite integral, constant of integration

Reverse differentiation

Integration is reverse differentiation. For the exponential function we know \(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(\mathrm{e}^{x})=\mathrm{e}^{x}\) and, by the chain rule, \(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(\mathrm{e}^{ax+b})=a\mathrm{e}^{ax+b}\). Reversing these gives:

$$\int \mathrm{e}^{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=\mathrm{e}^{x}+c,\qquad \int \mathrm{e}^{ax+b}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{a}\mathrm{e}^{ax+b}+c\quad(a\neq0)$$

This is the reverse chain rule: keep the exponential exactly as it is and divide by \(a\), the coefficient of \(x\) in the power. The rule needs \(a\neq0\): if \(a=0\) the integrand is just a constant. A constant multiplier stays in front: \(\int k\mathrm{e}^{ax+b}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{k}{a}\mathrm{e}^{ax+b}+c\).

The constant of integration

An indefinite integral is a whole family of functions, because any constant differentiates to zero. So we always add a constant of integration \(c\). If we also know one point on the curve (or one value of the function), we can find \(c\).

Always check an answer by differentiating it: you should get back the function you integrated.

Common mistakes

  • Multiplying by \(a\) instead of dividing: \(\int \mathrm{e}^{4x}\,\mathrm{d}x\neq4\mathrm{e}^{4x}+c\).
  • Changing the power: the power stays \(ax+b\); do not add 1 to it.
  • Losing the sign when \(a\) is negative: \(\int \mathrm{e}^{-2x}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\frac12\mathrm{e}^{-2x}+c\).
  • Leaving out \(+c\).

Worked example 1: Using the rule

Book: Worked example 5.1, p.112; Exercise 5A Q1 p.113

Find (a) \(\int \mathrm{e}^{4x}\,\mathrm{d}x\) (b) \(\int \mathrm{e}^{-3x}\,\mathrm{d}x\) (c) \(\int \mathrm{e}^{2x+5}\,\mathrm{d}x\).

  1. (a) \(a=4\): \(\frac14\mathrm{e}^{4x}+c\) B1
  2. (b) \(a=-3\): \(\frac{1}{-3}\mathrm{e}^{-3x}+c=-\frac13\mathrm{e}^{-3x}+c\) B1
  3. (c) \(a=2\): \(\frac12\mathrm{e}^{2x+5}+c\) B1

Answer: (a) \(\frac14\mathrm{e}^{4x}+c\) (b) \(-\frac13\mathrm{e}^{-3x}+c\) (c) \(\frac12\mathrm{e}^{2x+5}+c\)

Worked example 2: A constant multiplier, and checking

Book: Key point 5.1, p.112; Exercise 5A Q1h p.113

Find \(\int 6\mathrm{e}^{1-2x}\,\mathrm{d}x\) and check your answer by differentiating.

  1. Form \(k\mathrm{e}^{1-2x}\) with \(k=\frac{6}{-2}\) M1
  2. \(=-3\mathrm{e}^{1-2x}+c\) A1
  3. Check: \(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(-3\mathrm{e}^{1-2x})=-3\times(-2)\mathrm{e}^{1-2x}=6\mathrm{e}^{1-2x}\) (correct)

Answer: \(-3\mathrm{e}^{1-2x}+c\)

Worked example 3: Finding the constant of integration

Book: Key point 5.1, p.112 (constant of integration)

A curve has gradient \(\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=4\mathrm{e}^{2x}\) and passes through the point \((0,5)\). Find the equation of the curve.

  1. \(y=\int4\mathrm{e}^{2x}\,\mathrm{d}x=2\mathrm{e}^{2x}+c\) B1
  2. At \((0,5)\): \(5=2\mathrm{e}^{0}+c=2+c\) M1
  3. \(c=3\), so \(y=2\mathrm{e}^{2x}+3\) A1

Answer: \(y=2\mathrm{e}^{2x}+3\)

Quick checks

Quick check 1

Find \(\int 8\mathrm{e}^{4x-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\frac{8}{4}\mathrm{e}^{4x-1}+c\)
  2. \(=2\mathrm{e}^{4x-1}+c\)

Answer: \(2\mathrm{e}^{4x-1}+c\)

Quick check 2

A curve has gradient \(\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=6\mathrm{e}^{3x}\) and passes through \((0,7)\). Find the constant of integration \(c\) in \(y=2\mathrm{e}^{3x}+c\).

Worked solution
  1. \(7=2\mathrm{e}^{0}+c=2+c\)
  2. \(c=5\)

Answer: \(c=5\)

Quick check 3

For the curve \(y=2\mathrm{e}^{3x}+5\) in Quick check 2, find \(y\) when \(x=0.4\), correct to 3 significant figures.

Worked solution
  1. \(y=2\mathrm{e}^{1.2}+5=2\times3.3201\ldots+5\)
  2. \(=11.640\ldots=11.6\) (3 s.f.)

Answer: \(11.6\)

Quick check 4

Find \(\int 5\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}x}\,\mathrm{d}x\).

Worked solution
  1. \(a=-\frac12\), so divide by \(-\frac12\) (multiply by \(-2\))
  2. \(5\times(-2)\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}x}+c=-10\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}x}+c\)

Answer: \(-10\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}x}+c\)

Marks: M = method, A = accurate answer (depends on M), B = independent mark. Indefinite integrals are exact expressions: include \(+c\). Keep full calculator values until the end; give decimal answers to 3 s.f.
