9.1 Integration as the reverse of differentiation

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 9 Integration, printed pp.239–243. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook idea of integration as the reverse of differentiation, the rule for integrating powers of x and the constant of integration.

Key words: integration, indefinite integral, constant of integration

Reversing differentiation

If \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=3x^2\), then \(y\) could be \(x^3\), \(x^3+5\) or \(x^3-2\): differentiating any constant gives 0. Finding \(y\) from \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) is called integration, and we write

\[\int 3x^2\,dx=x^3+c,\]

where \(c\) is the constant of integration. This is an indefinite integral: it is a family of functions, not a single one.

The rule for powers of x

\[\int x^n\,dx=\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+c,\qquad n\ne-1.\]

Add one to the power, then divide by the new power. Constant multiples stay in front, and sums are integrated term by term: \(\int(\mathrm{f}(x)+\mathrm{g}(x))\,dx=\int\mathrm{f}(x)\,dx+\int\mathrm{g}(x)\,dx\). Before integrating, write roots and fractions as powers of \(x\), and expand brackets.

Worked example 1: A polynomial

Coursebook, 9.1, integrating term by term. New numbers.

Find \(\displaystyle\int(4x^3-6x+2)\,dx\).

  1. \(\dfrac{4x^4}{4}-\dfrac{6x^2}{2}+2x\) M1
  2. \(=x^4-3x^2+2x+c\) A1

Answer: \(x^4-3x^2+2x+c\)

Worked example 2: Rewriting first

Coursebook, 9.1, dividing before integrating. New numbers.

Find \(\displaystyle\int\frac{x^2+3}{x^2}\,dx\).

  1. \(\dfrac{x^2+3}{x^2}=1+3x^{-2}\) M1
  2. \(\displaystyle\int(1+3x^{-2})\,dx=x+\dfrac{3x^{-1}}{-1}\) M1
  3. \(=x-\dfrac{3}{x}+c\) A1

Answer: \(x-\dfrac{3}{x}+c\)

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the constant of integration \(c\).
  • Dividing by the old power instead of the new power.
  • Integrating a fraction or product without first writing it as a sum of powers of \(x\).

Past paper

There is no whole past-paper question on this subsection alone. The first past papers on integration use a point on the curve, so they are in 9.2.

Quick check 1

Find \(\displaystyle\int 6x^2\,dx\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{6x^3}{3}\)

Answer: \(2x^3+c\)

Quick check 2

Find \(\displaystyle\int\frac{4}{x^3}\,dx\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\displaystyle\int4x^{-3}\,dx=\dfrac{4x^{-2}}{-2}\)

Answer: \(-2x^{-2}+c\)

Quick check 3

Find \(\displaystyle\int6\sqrt{x}\,dx\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{6x^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\frac{3}{2}}\)

Answer: \(4x^{\frac{3}{2}}+c\)
