8.1 Continuous random variables

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 1 Coursebook (Dean Chalmers), Chapter 8 The normal distribution, printed pp.188–192. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook link between relative frequency histograms and a probability density graph, with probability as area and total area 1.

Key words: continuous random variable, probability density

Discrete and continuous

A discrete random variable takes separate values, such as a number of goals. A continuous random variable can take any value in an interval, such as a length, a mass or a time. Measurements are rounded, but the quantity itself is continuous.

From histograms to a probability density graph

If the relative frequencies of a large set of measurements are shown in a histogram, the area of each bar is the proportion of values in that class, and the total area is 1. As the data set grows and the classes get narrower, the tops of the bars approach a smooth curve. This curve is the graph of the probability density.

  • The probability that \(X\) lies between two values is the area under the graph between them.
  • The total area under the graph is 1, and the graph is never below the \(x\)-axis.
  • For a continuous random variable \(P(X=a)=0\), so \(P(X\lt a)=P(X\le a)\).

Worked example 1: A constant density

Coursebook, 8.1, probability as area. New numbers.

\(X\) takes values from 0 to 12 and its probability density is the same for every value in this interval. Find \(P(3\lt X\lt 7)\).

  1. Height of the rectangle \(=\dfrac{1}{12}\), because the total area is 1. M1
  2. \(P(3\lt X\lt 7)=4\times\dfrac{1}{12}=\dfrac{1}{3}\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{1}{3}\)

Worked example 2: A triangular density

Coursebook, 8.1, total area 1. New numbers.

The probability density of \(X\) is a straight line from \((0, 0)\) to \((2, h)\) and is zero outside \(0\le x\le 2\). Find \(h\) and \(P(X\lt 1)\).

  1. \(\tfrac{1}{2}\times2\times h=1\), so \(h=1\) B1
  2. At \(x=1\) the height is \(\tfrac{1}{2}\), so \(P(X\lt 1)=\tfrac{1}{2}\times1\times\tfrac{1}{2}\) M1
  3. \(=\tfrac{1}{4}\) A1

Answer: \(h=1\), \(P(X\lt 1)=\tfrac{1}{4}\)

Common mistakes

  • Reading the height of the graph as a probability. Only areas are probabilities.
  • Forgetting that the total area is 1 when a height is unknown.
  • Giving a non-zero answer for \(P(X=a)\) when \(X\) is continuous.

Quick check 1

\(X\) takes values from 0 to 10, and its probability density is the same for every value in this interval. Find \(P(X\lt 3)\).

Worked solution
  1. Height \(=\dfrac{1}{10}\)
  2. \(P(X\lt 3)=3\times\dfrac{1}{10}\)

Answer: \(0.3\)

Quick check 2

The probability density of \(X\) is a straight line from \((0, 0)\) to \((4, h)\) and zero outside \(0\le x\le 4\). Find \(P(X\lt 2)\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\tfrac{1}{2}\times4\times h=1\), so \(h=0.5\)
  2. Height at 2 is \(0.25\), so \(P(X\lt 2)=\tfrac{1}{2}\times2\times0.25\)

Answer: \(0.25\)

Quick check 3

The probability density of \(X\) is constant and equal to 0.04 for \(20\le x\le 25\). Find \(P(20\lt X\lt 25)\).

Worked solution
  1. Area \(=5\times0.04\)

Answer: \(0.2\)
