23.3 Vectors

Based on Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics Core and Extended Coursebook (Karen Morrison and Nick Hamshaw), Chapter 23 Transformations and vectors, printed pp.809–829. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook notation for vectors, adding and multiplying column vectors, magnitude and position vectors.

Key words: vector, column vector, magnitude, position vector

Writing vectors

A vector has both size and direction. A translation is a vector. We write a vector as \(\overrightarrow{AB}\), as a bold letter such as \(\mathbf{a}\), or as a column vector \(\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}\), meaning \(x\) units right and \(y\) units up. Equal vectors have the same size and the same direction.

Adding, subtracting and multiplying by a number

Work component by component: \(\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}c\\d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a+c\\b+d\end{pmatrix}\) and \(k\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}ka\\kb\end{pmatrix}\). The vector \(k\mathbf{a}\) is parallel to \(\mathbf{a}\); if \(k\) is negative it points the opposite way. Going from \(A\) to \(C\) via \(B\) gives \(\overrightarrow{AC}=\overrightarrow{AB}+\overrightarrow{BC}\).

Magnitude

The magnitude of a vector is its length. By Pythagoras, \(\left|\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}\right|=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\).

Position vectors

The position vector of a point \(A\) is \(\overrightarrow{OA}\), the vector from the origin to \(A\). If \(A\) and \(B\) have position vectors \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\), then \(\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}\).

Worked example 1: Combining vectors

Coursebook, 23.3, column vectors and magnitude. New numbers.

\(\mathbf{a}=\begin{pmatrix}3\\-1\end{pmatrix}\) and \(\mathbf{b}=\begin{pmatrix}-2\\4\end{pmatrix}\). Find \(3\mathbf{a}-2\mathbf{b}\) and \(|\mathbf{b}|\).

  1. \(3\mathbf{a}=\begin{pmatrix}9\\-3\end{pmatrix}\), \(2\mathbf{b}=\begin{pmatrix}-4\\8\end{pmatrix}\) M1
  2. \(3\mathbf{a}-2\mathbf{b}=\begin{pmatrix}13\\-11\end{pmatrix}\) A1
  3. \(|\mathbf{b}|=\sqrt{(-2)^2+4^2}=\sqrt{20}=4.47\) (3 s.f.) B1

Answer: \(\begin{pmatrix}13\\-11\end{pmatrix}\), \(4.47\)

Worked example 2: A vector between two points

Coursebook, 23.3, position vectors. New numbers.

\(A\) is the point \((2, 5)\) and \(B\) is the point \((8, -3)\). Find \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) and the distance \(AB\).

  1. \(\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}=\begin{pmatrix}8-2\\-3-5\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}6\\-8\end{pmatrix}\) B1
  2. \(AB=\sqrt{6^2+(-8)^2}\) M1
  3. \(=10\) A1

Answer: \(\overrightarrow{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}6\\-8\end{pmatrix}\), \(AB=10\)

Common mistakes

  • Finding \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) as \(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\) instead of \(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}\).
  • Squaring a negative component wrongly: \((-8)^2=64\), not \(-64\).
  • Giving a vector answer with a fraction line between the components.
