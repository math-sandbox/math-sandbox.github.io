9.4 Further indefinite integration

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 9 Integration, printed pp.249–250. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook idea of using a given derivative to integrate a more complicated expression.

Key words: integration, chain rule, indefinite integral

Using a derivative to integrate

Integration is the reverse of differentiation, so every result \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\mathrm{F}(x)=\mathrm{f}(x)\) gives an integral:

\[\text{if }\ \frac{d}{dx}\mathrm{F}(x)=\mathrm{f}(x),\ \text{ then }\ \int\mathrm{f}(x)\,dx=\mathrm{F}(x)+c.\]

Questions often say 'differentiate ... hence find ...'. Use the chain rule for the first part, then compare the expression to be integrated with the derivative: it is usually the derivative multiplied by a constant. Divide or multiply by that constant.

Worked example 1: Differentiate, hence integrate

Coursebook, 9.4, using a derivative to integrate. New numbers.

(a) Differentiate \((x^2+4)^5\). (b) Hence find \(\displaystyle\int x(x^2+4)^4\,dx\).

  1. (a) \(5(x^2+4)^4\times2x=10x(x^2+4)^4\) M1 A1
  2. (b) \(\displaystyle\int x(x^2+4)^4\,dx=\dfrac{1}{10}\int10x(x^2+4)^4\,dx\) M1
  3. \(=\dfrac{1}{10}(x^2+4)^5+c\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{1}{10}(x^2+4)^5+c\)

Worked example 2: A square root

Coursebook, 9.4, a derivative with a root. New numbers.

Given that \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\sqrt{x^2+9}=\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+9}}\), find \(\displaystyle\int\frac{3x}{\sqrt{x^2+9}}\,dx\).

  1. \(\dfrac{3x}{\sqrt{x^2+9}}=3\times\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+9}}\) M1
  2. \(\displaystyle\int\dfrac{3x}{\sqrt{x^2+9}}\,dx=3\sqrt{x^2+9}+c\) A1

Answer: \(3\sqrt{x^2+9}+c\)

Common mistakes

  • Using the rule for \((ax+b)^n\) when the bracket is not linear, such as \((x^2+4)^4\).
  • Multiplying by the constant instead of dividing when matching the derivative.
  • Forgetting \(+c\) in the 'hence' part.

Past paper

There is no whole past-paper question on this subsection alone. The past papers that use this method are parts of longer questions on other topics.

Quick check 1

Given \(\dfrac{d}{dx}(2x^2+1)^3=kx(2x^2+1)^2\), find \(k\).

Worked solution
  1. \(3(2x^2+1)^2\times4x\)

Answer: \(k=12\)

Quick check 2

Given \(\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^2+3)^4=8x(x^2+3)^3\), find \(\displaystyle\int16x(x^2+3)^3\,dx\).

Worked solution
  1. \(16x(x^2+3)^3=2\times8x(x^2+3)^3\)

Answer: \(2(x^2+3)^4+c\)

Quick check 3

Given \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\sqrt{2x^2+5}=\dfrac{2x}{\sqrt{2x^2+5}}\), find \(\displaystyle\int\frac{6x}{\sqrt{2x^2+5}}\,dx\).

Worked solution
  1. \(6x=3\times2x\)

Answer: \(3\sqrt{2x^2+5}+c\)
