6.2 Hypothesis testing of the population mean

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 2 Coursebook (Jayne Kranat), Chapter 6 Estimation, printed pp.127–130. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook test of a population mean using the normal distribution of the sample mean, with known or large-sample standard deviation.

Key words: hypothesis test, null hypothesis, alternative hypothesis, significance level, test statistic, critical value

Setting up the test

A hypothesis test about a population mean starts from a claimed value \(\mu_0\). The null hypothesis is \(\mathrm{H}_0\colon\mu=\mu_0\). The alternative hypothesis is \(\mathrm{H}_1\colon\mu\gt \mu_0\) or \(\mathrm{H}_1\colon\mu\lt \mu_0\) for a one-tailed test, or \(\mathrm{H}_1\colon\mu\ne\mu_0\) for a two-tailed test. The significance level is the probability of rejecting \(\mathrm{H}_0\) when it is true.

The test statistic

If \(\mathrm{H}_0\) is true, the mean of a random sample of size \(n\) has \(\bar{X}\sim\mathrm{N}\bigl(\mu_0, \tfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\bigr)\). This is exact when the population is normal; for a large sample it is approximately true by the Central Limit theorem, and an unbiased estimate from the sample may be used for \(\sigma^2\). The test statistic is

\[z=\dfrac{\bar{x}-\mu_0}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}}.\]

Comparing and concluding

Compare \(z\) with the critical value from the table, for example 1.645 for a 5% one-tailed test, 1.96 for a 2.5% one-tailed test or a 5% two-tailed test, and 2.326 for a 1% one-tailed test. Equivalently, compare the tail probability with the significance level. State the conclusion in context and not definitely: there is, or is not, evidence that the mean has changed.

Worked example 1: A one-tailed test

Coursebook, 6.2, test of a population mean. New numbers.

Bags of flour have masses with mean 500 g and standard deviation 8 g. After a machine change, a random sample of 50 bags has mean 497.6 g. Test at the 5% significance level whether the mean mass has decreased.

  1. \(\mathrm{H}_0\colon\mu=500\), \(\mathrm{H}_1\colon\mu\lt 500\) B1
  2. \(z=\dfrac{497.6-500}{8/\sqrt{50}}\) M1
  3. \(=-2.121\) A1
  4. \(-2.121\lt -1.645\) M1
  5. Reject \(\mathrm{H}_0\). There is evidence that the mean mass has decreased. A1

Answer: There is evidence at the 5% level that the mean mass has decreased.

Worked example 2: A two-tailed test

Coursebook, 6.2, two-tailed test. New numbers.

A population has standard deviation 3. A random sample of 36 values has mean 20.8. Test \(\mathrm{H}_0\colon\mu=20\) against \(\mathrm{H}_1\colon\mu\ne20\) at the 5% significance level.

  1. \(z=\dfrac{20.8-20}{3/6}=1.6\) M1
  2. Two-tailed 5%: critical value 1.96, and \(1.6\lt 1.96\) M1
  3. Do not reject \(\mathrm{H}_0\). There is no evidence that the mean is not 20. A1

Answer: No evidence at the 5% level that the mean differs from 20.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing by \(\sigma\) instead of \(\sigma/\sqrt{n}\).
  • Writing the hypotheses in terms of \(\bar{x}\) instead of the population mean \(\mu\).
  • Giving a definite conclusion, such as 'the mean has increased'. A test gives evidence, not proof.

Past paper

9709/62/M/J/22 Q2, official QP and official MS.

In the past, the mean height of plants of a particular species has been 2.3 m. A random sample of 60 plants of this species was treated with fertiliser and the mean height of these 60 plants was found to be 2.4 m. Assume that the standard deviation of the heights of plants treated with fertiliser is 0.4 m.

Carry out a test at the 2.5% significance level of whether the mean height of plants treated with fertiliser is greater than 2.3 m. [5]

Mark scheme
  1. \(\mathrm{H}_0\colon\) population mean height \(=2.3\); \(\mathrm{H}_1\colon\) population mean height \(>2.3\) B1 Official MS: B1, H0: pop mean height = 2.3, H1: pop mean height > 2.3; not just ‘mean’, allow μ.
  2. \(z=\dfrac{2.4-2.3}{0.4/\sqrt{60}}\) M1 Official MS: M1, for standardising, must have √60.
  3. \(=1.936\) A1 Official MS: A1, 1.936, 1.937 or 1.94.
  4. \(1.936<1.96\) M1 Official MS: M1, valid comparison with 1.96, or 2.64% > 2.5% OE; critical values method 2.4 < 2.401 accepted.
  5. Do not reject \(\mathrm{H}_0\). No evidence that the mean height of plants treated with fertiliser is more than 2.3 m. A1 Official MS: A1 FT (FT their z), no evidence that (mean) height (with fertiliser) is more than without; in context, not definite; no FT for a two-tail test.

Past paper

9709/62/O/N/22 Q2, official QP and official MS.

In the past, the mean length of a particular variety of worm has been 10.3 cm, with standard deviation 2.6 cm. Following a change in the climate, it is thought that the mean length of this variety of worm has decreased. The lengths of a random sample of 100 worms of this variety are found and the mean of this sample is found to be 9.8 cm.

Assuming that the standard deviation remains at 2.6 cm, carry out a test at the 2% significance level of whether the mean length has decreased. [5]

Mark scheme
  1. \(\mathrm{H}_0\colon\) population mean length \(=10.3\); \(\mathrm{H}_1\colon\) population mean length \(<10.3\) B1 Official MS: B1, H0: population mean length = 10.3 cm, H1: population mean length < 10.3 cm, or μ = 10.3, μ < 10.3 (not just ‘mean’).

  4. \(-1.923>-2.054\) M1 Official MS: M1 OE, valid comparison −1.923 > −2.054 or −2.055; or 0.0272 > 0.02; or critical value 9.8 > 9.766.

Quick check 1

In the past, a variable had mean 50 and standard deviation 4. A random sample of 36 values has mean 51.2. Find the value of the test statistic for a test of \(\mathrm{H}_0\colon\mu=50\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}=\dfrac{4}{6}\)
  2. \(z=\dfrac{51.2-50}{4/6}\)

Answer: \(1.8\)

Quick check 2

A test of \(\mathrm{H}_0\colon\mu=30\) against \(\mathrm{H}_1\colon\mu\gt 30\) is carried out at the 5% significance level, using the mean of a random sample of 25 values from a normal distribution with standard deviation 5. The critical value of \(z\) is 1.645. Find the smallest value of the sample mean for which \(\mathrm{H}_0\) is rejected.

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\bar{x}-30}{5/5}=1.645\)

Answer: \(31.6\) (3 s.f.)

Quick check 3

A random sample of 64 values from a population with standard deviation 6 has sum 2464. Find the value of the test statistic for a test of \(\mathrm{H}_0\colon\mu=40\) against \(\mathrm{H}_1\colon\mu\lt 40\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\bar{x}=\dfrac{2464}{64}=38.5\)
  2. \(z=\dfrac{38.5-40}{6/8}\)

Answer: \(-2\)
