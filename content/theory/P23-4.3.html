4.3 Derivatives of exponential functions

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton), Chapter 4 Differentiation, pp.87–90. Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: exponential function, e, chain rule, stationary point

The derivative of \(\mathrm{e}^x\)

The exponential function \(\mathrm{e}^x\) is its own derivative: at every point the gradient of \(y=\mathrm{e}^x\) equals its height.

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(\mathrm{e}^x\right)=\mathrm{e}^x$$

Why: \(\dfrac{\mathrm{e}^{x+\delta x}-\mathrm{e}^x}{\delta x}=\mathrm{e}^x\times\dfrac{\mathrm{e}^{\delta x}-1}{\delta x}\), and \(\dfrac{\mathrm{e}^{\delta x}-1}{\delta x}\to1\) as \(\delta x\to0\) (try \(\delta x=0.001\): \(1.0005\ldots\)). This is what makes the number e \(\approx2.718\) special.

Composite exponentials

By the chain rule, with \(u=\mathrm{f}(x)\):

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{f}(x)}\right)=\mathrm{f}^{\prime}(x)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{f}(x)}\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(\mathrm{e}^{ax+b}\right)=a\,\mathrm{e}^{ax+b}$$

Combine with the product and quotient rules. Because \(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{f}(x)}>0\) for all \(x\), at a stationary point you can divide by it: only the other factor can be zero.

Worked example 1: Differentiating exponentials

Book: Worked example 4.5, p.89; Exercise 4C p.90

Differentiate (a) \(y=4\mathrm{e}^{3x-1}\), (b) \(y=\mathrm{e}^{x^2+2x}\), (c) \(y=\dfrac{5}{\mathrm{e}^{2x}}\).

  1. (a) \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=4\times3\mathrm{e}^{3x-1}=12\mathrm{e}^{3x-1}\) B1
  2. (b) \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=(2x+2)\mathrm{e}^{x^2+2x}\) B1
  3. (c) \(y=5\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\Rightarrow\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=-10\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\) B1

Answer: (a) \(12\mathrm{e}^{3x-1}\) (b) \((2x+2)\mathrm{e}^{x^2+2x}\) (c) \(-10\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\)

Worked example 2: A stationary point with the product rule

Book: Worked example 4.6, p.89; Exercise 4C p.90

Find the exact coordinates of the stationary point of \(y=(x+3)\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\).

  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\mathrm{e}^{-2x}+(x+3)(-2\mathrm{e}^{-2x})\) M1 A1
  2. \(=\mathrm{e}^{-2x}(1-2x-6)=\mathrm{e}^{-2x}(-2x-5)\); \(\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\neq0\) M1
  3. \(x=-\frac52\), \(y=\frac12\mathrm{e}^{5}\) A1 A1

Answer: \(\left(-\frac52,\ \frac12\mathrm{e}^{5}\right)\)

Quick checks

Quick check 1

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=2\mathrm{e}^{3x}+5\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\) at the point where \(x=0\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=6\mathrm{e}^{3x}-10\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\)
  2. At \(x=0\): \(6-10\) (since \(\mathrm{e}^0=1\))

Answer: −4

Quick check 2

Find the \(x\)-coordinate of the stationary point of \(y=(2x-3)\mathrm{e}^{2x}\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=2\mathrm{e}^{2x}+(2x-3)\times2\mathrm{e}^{2x}=\mathrm{e}^{2x}(4x-4)\)
  2. \(\mathrm{e}^{2x}\neq0\), so \(4x-4=0\)

Answer: \(x=1\)

Quick check 3

The curve \(y=x^2\mathrm{e}^{-2x}\) has a stationary point with \(x\neq0\). Find its \(x\)-coordinate.

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=2x\mathrm{e}^{-2x}-2x^2\mathrm{e}^{-2x}=2x\mathrm{e}^{-2x}(1-x)\)
  2. \(x\neq0\), so \(1-x=0\)

Answer: \(x=1\)

Marks: M = method, A = accurate answer (depends on M), B = independent mark. Give exact answers unless the question asks for a decimal; differentiate first, substitute second.
