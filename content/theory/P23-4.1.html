4.1 The product rule

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton), Chapter 4 Differentiation, pp.81–83. Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: product rule, product, first principles, gradient, stationary point

When to use it

A function such as \(y=(x+1)^4(3x-2)^3\) is a product of two functions, \(u=(x+1)^4\) and \(v=(3x-2)^3\). Expanding is slow, so we use the product rule.

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(uv)=u\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}+v\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}$$

In words: (first \(\times\) derivative of second) \(+\) (second \(\times\) derivative of first). Each of \(u\) and \(v\) is usually differentiated with the chain rule.

Why it works (from first principles)

If \(y=uv\) and \(x\) increases by \(\delta x\), then \(y+\delta y=(u+\delta u)(v+\delta v)\), so \(\delta y=u\,\delta v+v\,\delta u+\delta u\,\delta v\). Divide by \(\delta x\) and let \(\delta x\to0\): the last term tends to \(0\times\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}=0\), leaving \(\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=u\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}+v\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}\) This method, starting from small changes \(\delta x\) and \(\delta y\), is called differentiating from first principles.

Using the derivative

The derivative gives the gradient of the curve at any point. Take out common factors before solving \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=0\) to find a stationary point.

Worked example 1: A product with a root

Book: Worked example 4.1, p.82; Exercise 4A p.83

Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) when \(y=(3x+1)\sqrt{2x-1}\), and find the gradient of the curve at \(x=5\).

  1. \(u=3x+1,\ v=(2x-1)^{\frac12}\); \(\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}=3,\ \frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}=\frac12(2x-1)^{-\frac12}\times2=(2x-1)^{-\frac12}\) B1
  2. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=(3x+1)(2x-1)^{-\frac12}+3(2x-1)^{\frac12}\) M1
  3. \(=\dfrac{(3x+1)+3(2x-1)}{\sqrt{2x-1}}=\dfrac{9x-2}{\sqrt{2x-1}}\) A1
  4. At \(x=5\): \(\dfrac{45-2}{\sqrt9}=\dfrac{43}{3}\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{9x-2}{\sqrt{2x-1}}\); gradient \(\frac{43}{3}\)

Worked example 2: Stationary points of a product

Book: Worked example 4.2, p.83; Exercise 4A p.83

Find the \(x\)-coordinates of the stationary points of \(y=(x-4)^2(2x+1)\).

  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=(x-4)^2\times2+(2x+1)\times2(x-4)\) M1
  2. \(=2(x-4)\left[(x-4)+(2x+1)\right]=2(x-4)(3x-3)\) A1
  3. \(2(x-4)(3x-3)=0\) M1
  4. \(x=4\) or \(x=1\) A1

Answer: \(x=1\) and \(x=4\)

Quick checks

Quick check 1

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=x^2(3x-1)^4\) at the point where \(x=1\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=2x(3x-1)^4+x^2\times4(3x-1)^3\times3\)
  2. At \(x=1\): \(2(1)(2)^4+12(1)^2(2)^3=32+96\)

Answer: 128

Quick check 2

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=(2x+1)\sqrt{x+3}\) at the point where \(x=1\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=2\sqrt{x+3}+(2x+1)\times\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x+3}}\)
  2. At \(x=1\): \(2\times2+\dfrac{3}{2\times2}=4+0.75\)

Answer: 4.75

Quick check 3

The curve \(y=(x-2)^2(x+1)\) has two stationary points. Find their \(x\)-coordinates.

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=2(x-2)(x+1)+(x-2)^2=(x-2)\left[2(x+1)+(x-2)\right]=3x(x-2)\)
  2. \(3x(x-2)=0\)

Answer: (a) \(x=0\) (b) \(x=2\)

Marks: M = method, A = accurate answer (depends on M), B = independent mark. Give exact answers unless the question asks for a decimal; differentiate first, substitute second.
