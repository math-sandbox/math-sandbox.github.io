17.3 Buying and selling

Based on Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics Core & Extended Coursebook (Morrison & Hamshaw), Chapter 17 Managing money, pp.571–580 (pp.572–573 missing from the scan). Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: cost price, selling price, profit, loss, mark-up, percentage profit, discount, sales tax (VAT)

Profit and loss

A trader buys at the cost price and sells at the selling price. The difference is a profit or a loss.

$$\text{profit}=\text{selling price}-\text{cost price}$$$$\text{loss}=\text{cost price}-\text{selling price}$$$$\text{percentage profit}=\frac{\text{profit}}{\text{cost price}}\times100$$

Mark-up: cost price \(+\) % mark-up \(=\) selling price. The cost price is always 100%, so a 30% mark-up means selling price \(=130\%\) of cost.

Reverse problems: if the selling price is 130% of the cost, then cost \(=\) selling price \(\div1.30\). Never take 30% off the selling price.

Discount

A discount is a reduction from the original price. A 15% discount means you pay 85%. Percentage discount \(=\dfrac{\text{discount}}{\text{original price}}\times100\).

Sales tax / VAT

Note: book pp.572–573 are missing from the scan; this part follows the standard 0580 approach.

Price including tax \(=\) price before tax \(\times\left(1+\frac{r}{100}\right)\). To find the price before tax, divide by \(\left(1+\frac{r}{100}\right)\).

Worked example 1: Percentage profit

Book: Worked example 18, p.574; Exercise 17.9 p.575

A trader buys a lamp for \$48 and sells it for \$61.20. Find the percentage profit.

  1. Profit \(=61.20-48=\$13.20\) B1
  2. \(\dfrac{13.20}{48}\times100\) M1
  3. \(=27.5\%\) A1

Answer: 27.5%

Worked example 2: Reverse problems: finding the cost price

Book: Worked examples 19–20, pp.574–575; Exercise 17.9 p.575

(a) A shop sells a bag for \$91 after a 30% mark-up. Find the cost price. (b) A bike is sold for \$153 at a loss of 15%. Find the cost price.

  1. (a) \$91 is \(130\%\) of the cost: \(91\div1.30\) M1
  2. \(=\$70.00\) A1
  3. (b) \$153 is \(100-15=85\%\) of the cost: \(153\div0.85\) M1
  4. \(=\$180.00\) A1

Answer: (a) \$70.00 (b) \$180.00

Worked example 3: Discount, then tax

Book: Worked example 21, p.576; Exercise 17.10 p.576–577 (tax: pp.572–573 missing)

A jacket priced at \$84 has a 20% discount. Sales tax of 7% is then added. (a) Find the final price. (b) A different coat is reduced from \$120 to \$99. Find the percentage discount.

  1. (a) Sale price \(=0.80\times84=\$67.20\) M1
  2. With tax \(=67.20\times1.07=71.904\) M1
  3. \(=\$71.90\) A1
  4. (b) Discount \(=120-99=\$21\) B1
  5. \(\dfrac{21}{120}\times100\) M1
  6. \(=17.5\%\) A1

Answer: (a) \$71.90 (b) 17.5%

Quick checks

Quick check 1

A phone costs a shop \$360. It is sold at a loss of 12.5%. Find the selling price.

Worked solution
  1. Selling price \(=87.5\%\) of cost
  2. \(0.875\times360=315\)

Answer: \$315.00

Quick check 2

A price of \$45.60 includes 14% sales tax. Find the price before tax.

Worked solution
  1. \$45.60 is 114% of the original
  2. \(45.60\div1.14=40\)

Answer: \$40.00

Quick check 3

A sofa is reduced from \$250 to \$212.50 in a sale. Find the percentage discount.

Worked solution
  1. Discount \(=250-212.50=37.50\)
  2. \(\dfrac{37.50}{250}\times100=15\)

Answer: 15%

Quick check 4

A trader buys 40 melons at \$3.50 each. She sells 32 of them at \$5.25 each and the rest at \$2.80 each. Find her percentage profit.

Worked solution
  1. Cost \(=40\times3.50=\$140\)
  2. Income \(=32\times5.25+8\times2.80\)
  3. \(=168+22.40=\$190.40\)
  4. Profit \(=190.40-140=\$50.40\)
  5. \(\dfrac{50.40}{140}\times100=36\)

Answer: 36%
