2.5 Hypothesis testing with the Poisson distribution

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 2 Coursebook, Chapter 2, printed pp.43–46 (null and alternative hypotheses for a Poisson mean, the critical region, Type I error, and carrying out the test). Printed pages were read from S2.pdf (printed 43–46 = PDF 55–58). Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: hypothesis test, null hypothesis, alternative hypothesis, significance level, critical region, Type I error, Poisson distribution

A hypothesis test for a Poisson mean compares an observed count with what a stated mean would lead you to expect. The null hypothesis \(H_0\) gives a specific value of the parameter \(\lambda\) of the Poisson distribution. The alternative hypothesis \(H_1\) is one-tailed or two-tailed, according to the claim being tested. The test is carried out at a stated significance level.

The critical region is the set of values of the test statistic for which \(H_0\) is rejected. It is found from the Poisson probabilities under \(H_0\), so that the probability of landing in the critical region does not exceed the significance level. That probability is the probability of a Type I error: rejecting \(H_0\) when it is true. If the observed value does not lie in the critical region, there is not enough evidence to reject \(H_0\).

Worked example 1

Book: scaling the Poisson mean to the length of the observed period, printed p.43. New numbers.

Under \(H_0\) the number of events per week has mean 4. A test uses a period of 5 weeks. Find the value of \(\lambda\) under \(H_0\) for the total number of events in that period.

  1. The means add, so \(\lambda=5\times 4=20\) under \(H_0\). A1

Answer: 20

Worked example 2

Book: forming the hypotheses and naming the significance level, printed pp.44–46. New numbers.

Under \(H_0\) the monthly mean is 3. A test is based on a period of 4 months, at the 5% significance level. Write down \(\lambda\) under \(H_0\) and the significance level as a percentage.

  1. \(\lambda=3\times 4=12\), and the significance level is 5%. A1

Answer: 12 and 5%

Common mistakes

  • Using the original unscaled mean (for example, the mean per month) as \(\lambda\) for a several-month total.
  • Putting the observed value into the alternative rather than comparing it with the critical region computed under \(H_0\).
  • Describing a Type I error without the context of the test (for example, saying only “rejecting a true null hypothesis” with no mention of what is being claimed).
