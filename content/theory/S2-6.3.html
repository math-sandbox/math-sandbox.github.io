6.3 Confidence intervals for population mean

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 2 Coursebook (Jayne Kranat), Chapter 6 Estimation, printed pp.131–137. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook confidence intervals for a population mean from a normal population or a large sample.

Key words: confidence interval, central limit theorem

What a confidence interval is

A single sample mean \(\bar{x}\) estimates the population mean \(\mu\), but it is unlikely to be exactly right. A confidence interval is a range of values calculated from the sample. For a 95% interval, if many samples were taken and an interval calculated from each, about 95% of those intervals would contain \(\mu\).

The formula

If \(\bar{X}\sim\mathrm{N}\bigl(\mu, \tfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\bigr)\), the interval is

\[\bar{x}-z\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\lt \mu\lt \bar{x}+z\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}},\]

Confidence level\(z\)
90%1.645
95%1.96
98%2.326
99%2.576

The width of the interval is \(2z\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\). A higher confidence level gives a wider interval; a larger sample gives a narrower one.

When the population is not normal

If the population is not known to be normal, the Central Limit theorem says that for a large sample \(\bar{X}\) is approximately normal, so the same formula can be used. If \(\sigma\) is unknown, a large sample also lets us use the unbiased estimate \(s\) in place of \(\sigma\).

Worked example 1: A 95% interval

Coursebook, 6.3, confidence interval with known variance. New numbers.

The masses of some parcels have standard deviation 0.8 kg. A random sample of 64 parcels has mean mass 5.2 kg. Find a 95% confidence interval for the population mean mass.

  1. \(z=1.96\) B1
  2. \(5.2\pm1.96\times\dfrac{0.8}{\sqrt{64}}=5.2\pm0.196\) M1
  3. \(5.00\lt \mu\lt 5.40\) (3 s.f.) A1

Answer: \((5.00, 5.40)\)

Worked example 2: Using a large sample

Coursebook, 6.3, large-sample interval with an estimated variance. New numbers.

For a random sample of 150 adults, the times spent travelling to work have \(\bar{x}=34.0\) minutes and unbiased variance estimate \(s^2=90\). Find a 90% confidence interval for the population mean time, and say why a normal model for the population is not needed.

  1. \(z=1.645\) B1
  2. \(34.0\pm1.645\times\sqrt{\dfrac{90}{150}}=34.0\pm1.274\) M1
  3. \(32.7\lt \mu\lt 35.3\) A1
  4. The sample is large, so by the Central Limit theorem the sample mean is approximately normal. B1

Answer: \((32.7, 35.3)\)

Common mistakes

  • Using \(\sigma\) instead of \(\sigma/\sqrt{n}\).
  • Using the one-tailed value 1.645 for a 95% interval. A 95% interval uses 1.96.
  • Giving a single value instead of an interval, or rounding the limits too early.

Quick check 1

A random sample of 100 values from a population with standard deviation 6 has mean 25. Find the upper limit of a 95% confidence interval for the population mean, using \(z=1.96\).

Worked solution
  1. \(1.96\times\dfrac{6}{10}=1.176\)
  2. \(25+1.176\)

Answer: \(26.2\) (3 s.f.)

Quick check 2

A 99% confidence interval for a population mean is calculated from a random sample of 64 values from a population with standard deviation 4. Using \(z=2.576\), find the width of the interval.

Worked solution
  1. Half-width \(=2.576\times\dfrac{4}{8}=1.288\)

Answer: \(2.58\) (3 s.f.)

Quick check 3

A 95% confidence interval for a population mean, calculated from a random sample of 49 values, is \((27.2, 32.8)\). Using \(z=1.96\), find the population standard deviation.

Worked solution
  1. Half-width \(=2.8\)
  2. \(1.96\times\dfrac{\sigma}{7}=2.8\)

Answer: \(\sigma=10.0\)
