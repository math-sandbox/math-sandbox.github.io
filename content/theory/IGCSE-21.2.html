21.2 Ratio and scale

Based on Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics Core and Extended Coursebook (Karen Morrison and Nick Hamshaw), Chapter 21 Ratio, rate and proportion. The wording is original and follows the published section titles and the standard Cambridge IGCSE treatment of the topic. Printed pp.720–724.

Key words: scale, scale drawing

Scales as ratios

A map scale such as \(1:50\,000\) means that 1 unit on the map stands for 50 000 of the same units on the ground. So 1 cm on the map is 50 000 cm, which is 500 m or 0.5 km. A scale drawing uses the same idea for a plan of a room or a model.

Useful conversions: \(1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}\) and \(1\text{ km}=100\,000\text{ cm}\).

Lengths

  • Map to real: multiply by the scale number, then convert units.
  • Real to map: convert to the map units (usually cm), then divide by the scale number.

Areas

If 1 cm represents \(k\) km, then a square of side 1 cm represents a square of side \(k\) km, so \(1\text{ cm}^2\) represents \(k^2\text{ km}^2\). Always square the length scale before you work with areas.

Worked example 1: Map length to real length

Coursebook, 21.2, map scales. New numbers.

A map has a scale of \(1:40\,000\). A lake is 7.5 cm long on the map. Find the real length of the lake in kilometres.

  1. Real length \(=7.5\times40\,000=300\,000\) cm M1
  2. \(300\,000\div100\,000=3\) km A1

Answer: 3 km

Worked example 2: Map area to real area

Coursebook, 21.2, scales and area. New numbers.

A map has a scale of \(1:500\,000\). A farm has an area of 3.2 \(\text{cm}^2\) on the map. Find the real area of the farm in square kilometres.

  1. 1 cm represents 500 000 cm \(=5\) km, so \(1\text{ cm}^2\) represents \(25\text{ km}^2\). M1
  2. Area \(=3.2\times25=80\text{ km}^2\) A1

Answer: \(80\text{ km}^2\)

Common mistakes

  • Multiplying a map area by the length scale instead of the square of the length scale.
  • Converting centimetres to kilometres by dividing by 1000. There are 100 000 cm in 1 km.
  • Dividing instead of multiplying when going from the map to the real object.

Past paper

0580/23/O/N/22 Q14, official QP and official MS.

A map has a scale of 1 : 200 000.

Find the area, in square kilometres, of a lake that has an area of 12.4 \(\text{cm}^2\) on the map. [2]

Mark scheme
  1. 1 cm represents 200 000 cm \(=2\) km, so \(1\text{ cm}^2\) represents \(2^2=4\text{ km}^2\), and the area is \(12.4\times4\). M1 Official MS: M1 for answer figs 496.
  2. \(=49.6\text{ km}^2\) A1 Official MS: 2 marks for 49.6.

Past paper

0580/22/M/J/16 Q7, official QP and official MS.

A map is drawn to a scale of 1 : 1 000 000.

A forest on the map has an area of 4.6 \(\text{cm}^2\).

Calculate the actual area of the forest in square kilometres. [2]

Mark scheme
  1. 1 cm represents 1 000 000 cm \(=10\) km, so \(1\text{ cm}^2\) represents \(100\text{ km}^2\). B1 Official MS: B1 for 1 cm² : 100 km² oe, or M1 for 4.6 × 1 000 000² ÷ 100 000² oe seen.
  2. Area \(=4.6\times100=460\text{ km}^2\) B1 Official MS: 2 marks for 460.

Quick check 1

A map has a scale of \(1:25\,000\). Two villages are 6 cm apart on the map. Find the actual distance between the villages in kilometres.

Worked solution
  1. \(6\times25\,000=150\,000\) cm
  2. \(150\,000\div100\,000\)

Answer: 1.5 km

Quick check 2

A map has a scale of \(1:50\,000\). The real distance between two points is 8 km. How far apart are the points on the map, in centimetres?

Worked solution
  1. \(8\text{ km}=800\,000\text{ cm}\)
  2. \(800\,000\div50\,000\)

Answer: 16 cm

Quick check 3

A map has a scale of \(1:200\,000\). Find the area, in square kilometres, of a lake that has an area of 12.4 \(\text{cm}^2\) on the map.

Worked solution
  1. 1 cm represents 2 km, so \(1\text{ cm}^2\) represents \(4\text{ km}^2\).
  2. \(12.4\times4\)

Answer: \(49.6\text{ km}^2\)
