6.3 Arithmetic progressions

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 6 Series, printed pp.166–171. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook formulae for the \(n\)th term and the sum of the first \(n\) terms of an arithmetic progression.

Key words: arithmetic progression, common difference

The \(n\)th term

An arithmetic progression is a sequence in which each term is found by adding the same number to the term before. That number is the common difference, \(d\). With first term \(a\), the terms are \(a,\ a+d,\ a+2d,\ \ldots\), so

\[u_n=a+(n-1)d.\]

The sum of the first \(n\) terms

Writing the sum forwards and backwards and adding pairs of terms gives

\[S_n=\dfrac{n}{2}\bigl(2a+(n-1)d\bigr)=\dfrac{n}{2}(a+l),\]

where \(l=a+(n-1)d\) is the last term. For example, the sum of the first \(n\) positive integers is \(\dfrac{n}{2}(n+1)\).

Two facts about a progression, such as two terms or two sums, give two equations in \(a\) and \(d\), which can be solved simultaneously.

Worked example 1: A term and a sum

Coursebook, 6.3, \(n\)th term and sum formulae. New numbers.

The arithmetic progression \(7, 11, 15, \ldots\) is given. Find the 20th term and the sum of the first 20 terms.

  1. \(a=7\), \(d=4\), so \(u_{20}=7+19\times4=83\). B1
  2. \(S_{20}=\dfrac{20}{2}(7+83)\) M1
  3. \(=900\) A1

Answer: \(u_{20}=83\), \(S_{20}=900\)

Worked example 2: Two terms given

Coursebook, 6.3, simultaneous equations for \(a\) and \(d\). New numbers.

The 3rd term of an arithmetic progression is 12 and the 8th term is 37. Find the sum of the first 10 terms.

  1. \(a+2d=12\) and \(a+7d=37\), so \(5d=25\), \(d=5\) and \(a=2\). M1
  2. \(S_{10}=\dfrac{10}{2}(2\times2+9\times5)\) M1
  3. \(=245\) A1

Answer: \(245\)

Common mistakes

  • Using \(a+nd\) for the \(n\)th term; the correct formula is \(a+(n-1)d\).
  • Using \(n(2a+(n-1)d)\) without the factor \(\dfrac{1}{2}\).
  • For an inequality such as \(S_n\gt k\), giving a non-integer value of \(n\); the number of terms must be a whole number.

Quick check 1

An arithmetic progression has first term 5 and common difference 3. Find the 15th term.

Worked solution
  1. \(u_{15}=5+14\times3=47\)

Answer: \(47\)

Quick check 2

An arithmetic progression has first term 4 and common difference 6. Find the sum of the first 12 terms.

Worked solution
  1. \(S_{12}=\dfrac{12}{2}(8+11\times6)=6\times74\)

Answer: \(444\)

Quick check 3

The first term of an arithmetic progression is 5 and the 10th term is 50. Find the common difference.

Worked solution
  1. \(5+9d=50\), so \(9d=45\).

Answer: \(d=5\)
