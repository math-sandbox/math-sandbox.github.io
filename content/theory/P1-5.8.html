5.8 Further trigonometric equations

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 5 Trigonometry, printed pp.149–152 (physical PDF page = printed page + 12). Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook method of using an identity, or a double-angle argument such as \(2x\), before solving a trigonometric equation in a given interval.

Key words: trigonometric identity, related angle

Using an identity, then solving

Some equations are first rewritten using a trigonometric identity from 5.7, or by writing the equation in the form \(\tan k\theta=c\), and only then solved in the given interval. If the unknown appears as \(2x\), the related-angle method is applied to \(2x\), and the interval for \(2x\) is twice as long as the interval for \(x\). Every valid value of \(2x\) is then divided by \(2\), and any values of \(x\) that fall outside the given interval are discarded.

For example, the equation \(\sin^2 2x=3\cos^2 2x\) becomes \(\tan^2 2x=3\) provided \(\cos 2x\neq 0\), and both the positive and the negative square roots of \(3\) must be considered.

Worked example 1: From sine and cosine to tangent

Coursebook, 5.8, express-as-tan style. New equation \(2\sin\theta=\cos\theta\) for \(0^\circ<\theta<180^\circ\).

Express the equation \(2\sin\theta=\cos\theta\) in the form \(\tan\theta=k\), and solve it for \(0^\circ<\theta<180^\circ\).

  1. Dividing by \(\cos\theta\) (which is not zero, because otherwise \(\sin\theta\) would also be zero) gives \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{1}{2}\). M1
  2. The related acute angle is \(26.6^\circ\) to 1 decimal place, and tangent is positive in the first quadrant only, in this interval, so \(\theta=26.6^\circ\). A1

Answer: \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{1}{2}\); \(\theta=26.6^\circ\)

Worked example 2: An equation in \(2x\)

Coursebook, 5.8, double-argument style. New equation \(\tan 2x=1\) for \(0^\circ<x<180^\circ\).

Solve \(\tan 2x=1\) for \(0^\circ<x<180^\circ\).

  1. Let \(\phi=2x\). Then \(\tan\phi=1\) and \(0^\circ<\phi<360^\circ\), so \(\phi=45^\circ\) or \(\phi=225^\circ\). M1
  2. Hence \(x=22.5^\circ\) or \(x=112.5^\circ\). A1

Answer: \(22.5^\circ\), \(112.5^\circ\)

Common mistakes

  • Solving for \(2x\) in the original interval for \(x\), instead of doubling the interval first, and so missing solutions.
  • Taking only the positive square root when an identity produces \(\tan^2\phi=k\).
  • Dividing an equation by \(\cos\theta\) without checking that this does not discard or introduce a solution.

Quick check 1

Express \(5\sin\theta=4\cos\theta\) in the form \(\tan\theta=k\). Give the value of \(5k\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{4}{5}\), so \(5k=4\).

Answer: \(4\)

Quick check 2

How many solutions of \(\tan 3x=1\) are there for \(0^\circ<x<180^\circ\)?

Worked solution
  1. \(3x\) runs through an open interval of length \(540^\circ\), and \(\tan\phi=1\) has one solution in each interval of \(180^\circ\), so there are \(3\) solutions.

Answer: \(3\)

Quick check 3

How many solutions of \(\tan 2x=\sqrt{3}\) are there for \(0^\circ<x<180^\circ\)?

Worked solution
  1. \(2x\) runs through an open interval of length \(360^\circ\), and \(\tan\phi=\sqrt{3}\) has one solution in each interval of \(180^\circ\), so there are \(2\) solutions.

Answer: \(2\)
