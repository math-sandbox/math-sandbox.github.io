6.5 Infinite geometric series

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 6 Series, printed pp.175–179. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook treatment of convergent geometric series and the formula for the sum to infinity.

Key words: sum to infinity, convergent, geometric progression, common ratio

When does an infinite series have a sum?

For a geometric progression with first term \(a\) and common ratio \(r\), the sum of the first \(n\) terms is

\[S_n=\frac{a(1-r^n)}{1-r}.\]

If \(-1\lt r\lt 1\), then \(r^n\) gets closer and closer to \(0\) as \(n\) increases, so \(S_n\) gets closer to a fixed value. The series is then convergent and this limit is called the sum to infinity.

\[S_\infty=\frac{a}{1-r},\qquad\text{valid only for } -1\lt r\lt 1.\]

If \(r\ge 1\) or \(r\le -1\), the terms do not get smaller, \(S_n\) does not settle to a single value and the series has no sum to infinity. Such a series is divergent.

Using the sum to infinity

Questions often give two facts, such as a term and the sum to infinity. Write each fact as an equation in \(a\) and \(r\), eliminate one unknown and solve. Then check that each value of \(r\) satisfies \(-1\lt r\lt 1\) and any other condition in the question.

Worked example 1: Finding a sum to infinity

Coursebook, 6.5, sum to infinity. New numbers.

Find the sum to infinity of the geometric series \(24+12+6+3+\dots\)

  1. \(a=24\) and \(r=\dfrac{12}{24}=\dfrac{1}{2}\), and \(-1\lt r\lt 1\) so the sum to infinity exists. B1
  2. \(S_\infty=\dfrac{24}{1-\frac{1}{2}}\) M1
  3. \(=48\) A1

Answer: \(48\)

Worked example 2: Two conditions

Coursebook, 6.5, problems with the sum to infinity. New numbers.

The second term of a geometric progression is 6 and the sum to infinity is 27. Find the two possible values of the common ratio and the corresponding first terms.

  1. \(ar=6\) and \(\dfrac{a}{1-r}=27\) B1
  2. Substitute \(a=\dfrac{6}{r}\): \(6=27r(1-r)\), so \(27r^2-27r+6=0\), that is \(9r^2-9r+2=0\). M1
  3. \((3r-1)(3r-2)=0\), so \(r=\dfrac{1}{3}\) or \(r=\dfrac{2}{3}\). A1
  4. \(a=\dfrac{6}{r}\) gives \(a=18\) or \(a=9\). A1

Answer: \(r=\dfrac{1}{3}\), \(a=18\) or \(r=\dfrac{2}{3}\), \(a=9\)

Common mistakes

  • Using \(S_\infty=\dfrac{a}{1-r}\) when \(r\) is not between \(-1\) and \(1\).
  • Forgetting to reject a value of \(r\) that does not meet a condition in the question, such as \(r\) being negative or greater than a given value.
  • Writing \(1-r\) as \(1+r\) when \(r\) is negative. For \(r=-\dfrac{2}{5}\), \(1-r=\dfrac{7}{5}\).

9709/12/O/N/21 Q6, official QP and official MS.

The second term of a geometric progression is 54 and the sum to infinity of the progression is 243. The common ratio is greater than \(\dfrac{1}{2}\).

Find the tenth term, giving your answer in exact form. [5]

Mark scheme
  1. \(ar=54\) and \(\dfrac{a}{1-r}=243\) B1 Official MS: B1, ar = 54 and a/(1 − r) = 243.
  2. Eliminate \(a\): \(\dfrac{54}{r}=243(1-r)\), so \(243r^2-243r+54=0\), that is \(9r^2-9r+2=0\). M1 Official MS: *M1, form a 3-term quadratic, 243r² − 243r + 54 = 0 [9r² − 9r + 2 = 0] or a² − 243a + 13122 = 0. Allow ± sign errors.
  3. \((3r-2)(3r-1)=0\), so \(r=\dfrac{2}{3}\) (since \(r\gt \dfrac{1}{2}\)). DM1 Official MS: DM1, solve, k(3r − 2)(3r − 1) or (a − 81)(a − 162).
  4. \(a=54\div\dfrac{2}{3}=81\) DM1 Official MS: DM1, 54 ÷ their 2/3 = a, or 54 ÷ their 81 = r. May be implied by final answer.
  5. Tenth term \(=81\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^9=\dfrac{512}{243}\) A1 Official MS: A1 OE, 512/243 (exact, e.g. 81 × (2/3)⁹ or 54 × (2/3)⁸). SC: if B1 M1 DM0 DM1 scored, SC B1 for correct final answer.

9709/11/O/N/23 Q7, official QP and official MS.

The sum of the first two terms of a geometric progression is 15 and the sum to infinity is \(\dfrac{125}{7}\). The common ratio of the progression is negative.

Find the third term of the progression. [7]

Mark scheme
  1. \(a(1+r)=15\) B1 Official MS: B1, accept a(1 − r²)/(1 − r) = 15.
  2. \(\dfrac{a}{1-r}=\dfrac{125}{7}\) B1 Official MS: B1.
  3. Eliminate \(a\): \(\dfrac{125}{7}(1-r)(1+r)=15\) M1 Official MS: M1, eliminate a.
  4. \(1-r^2=\dfrac{105}{125}\) M1 Official MS: M1.
  5. \(r^2=\dfrac{4}{25}\), so \(r=-\dfrac{2}{5}\) since the common ratio is negative. A1 Official MS: A1, r² = 4/25 leading to r = −2/5; condone 2/5 or ±.
  6. \(a=\dfrac{125}{7}\times\dfrac{7}{5}=25\) A1 Official MS: A1, a = 25; ignore a second answer.
  7. Third term \(=25\times\dfrac{4}{25}=4\) A1 Official MS: A1 CAO, 3rd term = 4. An alternative method is also given in the MS.

Quick check 1

A geometric progression has first term 12 and common ratio 0.25. Find the sum to infinity.

Worked solution
  1. \(S_\infty=\dfrac{12}{1-0.25}=\dfrac{12}{0.75}\)

Answer: \(16\)

Quick check 2

A geometric progression has first term 10 and sum to infinity 25. Find the common ratio.

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{10}{1-r}=25\), so \(1-r=0.4\).

Answer: \(r=0.6\)

Quick check 3

A geometric progression has common ratio 0.75 and sum to infinity 40. Find the first term.

Worked solution
  1. \(a=40\times(1-0.75)=40\times0.25\)

Answer: \(10\)
