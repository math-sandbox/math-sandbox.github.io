21.1 Working with ratio

Based on Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics Core and Extended Coursebook (Karen Morrison and Nick Hamshaw), Chapter 21 Ratio, rate and proportion. The wording is original and follows the published section titles and the standard Cambridge IGCSE treatment of the topic. Printed pp.712–719.

Key words: ratio, simplest form

What a ratio means

A ratio compares two or more quantities of the same kind. The ratio \(3:5\) means that for every 3 parts of the first quantity there are 5 parts of the second. A ratio has no units, so the quantities must be in the same units before you compare them.

Simplifying ratios

Divide every part of a ratio by the same number to write it in simplest form, just as you simplify a fraction. For example, \(12:18=2:3\) (divide by 6) and 40 cm : 2 m \(=40:200=1:5\). A ratio such as \(2.5:4\) can be multiplied by 2 to give whole numbers, \(5:8\).

Sharing in a given ratio

  1. Add the parts of the ratio to find the total number of parts.
  2. Divide the amount by the total number of parts to find one part.
  3. Multiply one part by each number in the ratio.

If you know one share, or the difference between two shares, use it to find one part first. For the ratio \(7:3\), the difference between the shares is \(7-3=4\) parts.

Worked example 1: Sharing an amount

Coursebook, 21.1, sharing in a ratio. New numbers.

Share 156 kg of rice between three shops in the ratio \(2:3:7\).

  1. There are \(2+3+7=12\) parts, so one part is \(156\div12=13\) kg. M1
  2. The shares are \(2\times13=26\) kg, \(3\times13=39\) kg and \(7\times13=91\) kg. A1

Answer: 26 kg, 39 kg and 91 kg

Worked example 2: Using one known share

Coursebook, 21.1, finding quantities from a ratio. New numbers.

Orange juice and lemonade are mixed in the ratio \(3:8\). A jug contains 450 ml of orange juice. How much lemonade is in the jug, and how much drink is there altogether?

  1. 3 parts \(=450\) ml, so one part \(=150\) ml. M1
  2. Lemonade \(=8\times150=1200\) ml. A1
  3. Total \(=11\times150=1650\) ml. A1

Answer: 1200 ml of lemonade and 1650 ml altogether

Common mistakes

  • Dividing the amount by one number of the ratio instead of by the total number of parts.
  • Comparing quantities in different units, such as centimetres and metres, without converting first.
  • Giving the shares in the wrong order. The order of the ratio matches the order of the names.

Past paper

0580/22/F/M/21 Q2, official QP and official MS.

Sahil and Anika share $78 in the ratio 5 : 8.

Calculate the amount each receives. [2]

Mark scheme
  1. One part is \(78\div(5+8)=78\div13=6\) dollars. M1 Official MS: M1 for 78 ÷ (5 + 8) × k oe, where k = 1, 5 or 8.
  2. Sahil receives \(5\times6=30\) dollars and Anika receives \(8\times6=48\) dollars. A1 Official MS: 2 marks for 30 and 48.

Past paper

0580/22/O/N/20 Q12, official QP and official MS.

Alex and Chris share sweets in the ratio Alex : Chris = 7 : 3.

Alex receives 20 more sweets than Chris.

Work out the number of sweets Chris receives. [2]

Mark scheme
  1. The difference is \(7-3=4\) parts, so 4 parts \(=20\) and one part \(=5\) sweets. M1 Official MS: M1 for 4 [parts] = 20 soi, or a correct equation, e.g. (x + 20)/7 = x/3 oe.
  2. Chris receives \(3\times5=15\) sweets. A1 Official MS: 2 marks for 15.

Quick check 1

Ravi and Mei share 120 stickers in the ratio \(5:3\). How many stickers does Ravi receive?

Worked solution
  1. There are 8 parts, so one part is \(120\div8=15\).
  2. \(5\times15\)

Answer: 75

Quick check 2

Alex and Chris share sweets in the ratio Alex : Chris \(=7:3\). Alex receives 20 more sweets than Chris. Work out the number of sweets Chris receives.

Worked solution
  1. 4 parts \(=20\), so one part \(=5\).
  2. \(3\times5\)

Answer: 15

Quick check 3

In a choir the ratio of boys to girls is \(4:5\). There are 24 boys. How many girls are there?

Worked solution
  1. 4 parts \(=24\), so one part \(=6\).
  2. \(5\times6\)

Answer: 30
