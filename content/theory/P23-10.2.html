10.2 Forming a differential equation from a problem

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 10 Differential equations, printed pp.252–261. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook translation of statements about rates of change into differential equations, then solving them with the given conditions.

Key words: differential equation, rate of change

From words to an equation

Many problems describe a rate of change. The rate of change of \(N\) with respect to time \(t\) is \(\dfrac{dN}{dt}\). Translate the words carefully:

  • '\(N\) increases at a rate proportional to \(N\)' gives \(\dfrac{dN}{dt}=kN\) with \(k\gt 0\).
  • '\(N\) decreases at a rate proportional to \(N\)' gives \(\dfrac{dN}{dt}=-kN\) with \(k\gt 0\), or \(\dfrac{dN}{dt}=kN\) with \(k\lt 0\).
  • 'the gradient of a curve is proportional to \(xy\)' gives \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=kxy\).

The constant \(k\) is unknown. A given rate at a known value lets you find \(k\), either straight away or after solving the differential equation. You then need a second condition for the constant of integration.

Exponential growth and decay

Solving \(\dfrac{dN}{dt}=kN\) by separating the variables gives \(\ln N=kt+c\), so \(N=N_0\mathrm{e}^{kt}\), where \(N_0\) is the value when \(t=0\). For example, the time to double is \(\dfrac{\ln2}{k}\). When an answer is found, interpret it in context, for example what happens to \(N\) as \(t\) becomes large.

Worked example 1: Forming and solving

Coursebook, 10.2, forming a differential equation. New numbers.

The mass \(m\) grams of a substance decreases at a rate proportional to \(m\). When \(t=0\), \(m=80\) and the mass is decreasing at 4 grams per day. Find \(m\) in terms of \(t\), and the time when \(m=40\).

  1. \(\dfrac{dm}{dt}=-km\), and \(4=k\times80\) gives \(k=0.05\) B1
  2. \(\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{m}\,dm=\int-0.05\,dt\), so \(\ln m=-0.05t+c\) M1
  3. \(t=0\), \(m=80\): \(m=80\mathrm{e}^{-0.05t}\) A1
  4. \(40=80\mathrm{e}^{-0.05t}\) gives \(t=\dfrac{\ln2}{0.05}=13.9\) days A1

Answer: \(m=80\mathrm{e}^{-0.05t}\); \(t=13.9\) days (3 s.f.)

Worked example 2: A gradient condition

Coursebook, 10.2, finding a curve from its gradient. New numbers.

The gradient of a curve at \((x, y)\) is proportional to \(xy\), where \(y\gt 0\). The curve passes through \((0, 2)\), and the gradient at \((1, 2\mathrm{e})\) is \(4\mathrm{e}\). Find \(y\) in terms of \(x\).

  1. \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=kxy\); at \((1, 2\mathrm{e})\): \(4\mathrm{e}=k\times1\times2\mathrm{e}\), so \(k=2\) B1
  2. \(\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{y}\,dy=\int2x\,dx\), so \(\ln y=x^2+c\) M1
  3. \((0, 2)\): \(c=\ln2\), so \(y=2\mathrm{e}^{x^2}\) A1

Answer: \(y=2\mathrm{e}^{x^2}\), which also passes through \((1, 2\mathrm{e})\)

Common mistakes

  • Leaving out the minus sign for a quantity that is decreasing.
  • Using the same letter \(k\) for the constant of proportionality and the constant of integration.
  • Finding k from a rate but forgetting to use the second condition for the constant of integration.

The variables \(N\) and \(t\) are treated as continuous, and it is given that when \(t=0\), \(N=1000\) and \(\dfrac{dN}{dt}=-10\).

(i) Show that \(N\) and \(t\) satisfy the differential equation

\(\displaystyle\frac{dN}{dt}=-0.01\mathrm{e}^{-0.02t}N.\) [1]

(ii) Solve the differential equation and find the value of \(t\) when \(N=800\). [6]

(iii) State what happens to the value of \(N\) as \(t\) becomes large. [1]

  4. (ii) \(0.5\mathrm{e}^{-0.02t}\) B1 Official MS: B1 OE, obtain term 0.5e^(−0.02t).
  5. (ii) \(t=0\), \(N=1000\): \(\ln1000=0.5+c\) M1 Official MS: M1, use N = 1000, t = 0 to evaluate a constant, or as limits, in a solution with terms a ln N and be^(−0.02t), where ab ≠ 0.

Quick check 1

The rate of increase of a population \(N\) is proportional to \(N\). When \(N=400\), the population is increasing at a rate of 10 per year. Writing \(\dfrac{dN}{dt}=kN\), find \(k\).

Worked solution
  1. \(10=400k\)

Answer: \(0.025\)

Quick check 2

A quantity \(N\) satisfies \(\dfrac{dN}{dt}=0.05N\), where \(t\) is the time in years. Find the time taken for \(N\) to double.

Worked solution
  1. \(N=N_0\mathrm{e}^{0.05t}\)
  2. \(\mathrm{e}^{0.05t}=2\), \(t=\dfrac{\ln2}{0.05}\)

Answer: \(13.9\) years (3 s.f.)

Quick check 3

The variables \(x\) and \(t\) satisfy \(\dfrac{dx}{dt}=0.2(50-x)\), where \(x\lt 50\), and \(x=0\) when \(t=0\). Find the value of \(x\) when \(t=3\).

Worked solution
  1. \(-\ln(50-x)=0.2t+c\), \(c=-\ln50\)
  2. \(x=50\bigl(1-\mathrm{e}^{-0.2t}\bigr)\), so \(x=50\bigl(1-\mathrm{e}^{-0.6}\bigr)\)

Answer: \(22.6\) (3 s.f.)
