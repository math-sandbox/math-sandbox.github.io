7.2 Partial fractions

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 and 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, Julian Gilbey), Chapter 7, printed pp.168–173. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook decomposition of a proper rational expression into partial fractions, including a repeated linear factor and an irreducible quadratic factor.

Key words: partial fractions

The standard forms

Once a rational expression is proper, it can be written as a sum of simpler fractions whose denominators are the factors of the original denominator. The usual cases are as follows.

  • A product of distinct linear factors, \((ax+b)(cx+d)\), gives \(\dfrac{A}{ax+b}+\dfrac{B}{cx+d}\).
  • A repeated linear factor, \((ax+b)^2\), gives \(\dfrac{A}{ax+b}+\dfrac{B}{(ax+b)^2}\).
  • An irreducible quadratic factor, \(x^2+px+q\), gives a numerator of the form \(Cx+D\) over that quadratic.

The constants are found by clearing the denominator and either substituting convenient values of \(x\) (the cover-up method, when the factor is linear and not repeated) or equating coefficients. An improper fraction must first be reduced, as in 7.1, before this decomposition begins.

Worked example 1: Distinct linear factors

Coursebook, 7.2, two-linear-factor style. New expression \(\dfrac{x+5}{(x+1)(x+2)}\).

Express \(\dfrac{x+5}{(x+1)(x+2)}\) in partial fractions.

  1. Write \(\dfrac{x+5}{(x+1)(x+2)}=\dfrac{A}{x+1}+\dfrac{B}{x+2}\), so \(x+5=A(x+2)+B(x+1)\). M1
  2. Putting \(x=-1\) gives \(A=4\); putting \(x=-2\) gives \(B=-3\). Hence \(\dfrac{4}{x+1}-\dfrac{3}{x+2}\). A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{4}{x+1}-\dfrac{3}{x+2}\)

Worked example 2: A repeated linear factor

Coursebook, 7.2, repeated-factor style. New expression \(\dfrac{3x+1}{(x+1)^2}\).

Express \(\dfrac{3x+1}{(x+1)^2}\) in partial fractions.

  1. Write \(\dfrac{3x+1}{(x+1)^2}=\dfrac{A}{x+1}+\dfrac{B}{(x+1)^2}\), so \(3x+1=A(x+1)+B\). M1
  2. Putting \(x=-1\) gives \(B=-2\); equating coefficients of \(x\) gives \(A=3\). Hence \(\dfrac{3}{x+1}-\dfrac{2}{(x+1)^2}\). A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{3}{x+1}-\dfrac{2}{(x+1)^2}\)

Common mistakes

  • Omitting the term \(\dfrac{B}{(ax+b)^2}\) when a linear factor is repeated, or using a constant numerator over an irreducible quadratic.
  • Starting the decomposition on an improper fraction.

Past paper

There is no whole past-paper question on this subsection alone. Questions that begin with partial fractions and then ask for a binomial expansion belong to 7.5.

Quick check 1

Express \(\dfrac{x+7}{(x+1)(x+3)}\) in partial fractions. Give the numerator of the term with denominator \(x+1\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{x+7}{(x+1)(x+3)}=\dfrac{A}{x+1}+\dfrac{B}{x+3}\). Putting \(x=-1\) gives \(A=3\).

Answer: \(3\)

Quick check 2

Express \(\dfrac{x+7}{(x+1)(x+3)}\) in partial fractions. Give the numerator of the term with denominator \(x+3\).

Worked solution
  1. Putting \(x=-3\) gives \(B=-2\).

Answer: \(-2\)

Quick check 3

Express \(\dfrac{2x+3}{(x+1)^2}\) in partial fractions. Give the numerator of the term with denominator \((x+1)^2\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{2x+3}{(x+1)^2}=\dfrac{A}{x+1}+\dfrac{B}{(x+1)^2}\). Putting \(x=-1\) gives \(B=1\).

Answer: \(1\)
