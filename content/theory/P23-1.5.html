1.5 The factor theorem

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Hugh Neill, Douglas Quadling and Julian Gilbey), Chapter 1 Algebra, printed pp.14–17 (Key points 1.3–1.4, Worked examples 1.9–1.11, Exercise 1E). Own wording and new numbers. The remainder theorem is left to the next subsection.

Key words: factor theorem, polynomial

If a polynomial \(P(x)\) divides exactly by a linear factor \(x-c\) to give a polynomial \(Q(x)\), then \[ P(x)=(x-c)Q(x). \] Substituting \(x=c\) gives \(P(c)=0\). This is the factor theorem.

Key point. If, for a polynomial \(P(x)\), \(P(c)=0\), then \(x-c\) is a factor of \(P(x)\).

The factor theorem can be extended. If \(P\bigl(\dfrac{b}{a}\bigr)=0\), then \(ax-b\) is a factor of \(P(x)\).

A common error is substituting x=c to test the factor x+c, which checks the wrong value.

For example, substituting \(x=4\) into \(x^3-6x^2+5x+12\) gives \[ 4^3-6(4)^2+5(4)+12=64-96+20+12=0, \] so \(x-4\) is a factor of \(x^3-6x^2+5x+12\).

Worked example 1: Showing a linear factor

Book: Worked example 1.9, p.15; new cubic

Use the factor theorem to show that \(x-4\) is a factor of \(x^3-6x^2+5x+12\).

  1. Let \(f(x)=x^3-6x^2+5x+12\). The factor theorem says that if \(f(4)=0\), then \(x-4\) is a factor. M1
  2. \(f(4)=4^3-6(4)^2+5(4)+12=64-96+20+12=0\). Hence \(x-4\) is a factor of \(x^3-6x^2+5x+12\). A1

Answer: \(f(4)=0\), so \(x-4\) is a factor.

Worked example 2: Unknown coefficients, then complete factorisation

Book: Worked example 1.10, pp.15–16; new cubic

It is given that \(2x^2-3x-2\) is a factor of \(2x^3+ax^2-11x+b\). Find the value of \(a\) and the value of \(b\), and hence factorise the cubic completely.

  1. Let \(f(x)=2x^3+ax^2-11x+b\). Factorising the known quadratic gives \(2x^2-3x-2=(2x+1)(x-2)\), so \(2x+1\) and \(x-2\) are factors of \(f(x)\). By the factor theorem, \(f\bigl(-\dfrac{1}{2}\bigr)=0\) and \(f(2)=0\). M1
  2. \(f(2)=0\) gives \(2(8)+a(4)-11(2)+b=0\), so \(16+4a-22+b=0\), or \(4a+b=6\). \(f\bigl(-\dfrac{1}{2}\bigr)=0\) gives \(2\bigl(-\dfrac{1}{8}\bigr)+a\bigl(\dfrac{1}{4}\bigr)-11\bigl(-\dfrac{1}{2}\bigr)+b=0\). Multiplying through by \(4\) produces \(-1+a+22+4b=0\), or \(a+4b=-21\). M1
  3. From \(4a+b=6\), \(b=6-4a\). Substituting into \(a+4(6-4a)=-21\) gives \(a+24-16a=-21\), so \(-15a=-45\) and \(a=3\). Then \(b=6-12=-6\). A1
  4. So \(f(x)=2x^3+3x^2-11x-6=(2x^2-3x-2)(x+3)=(2x+1)(x-2)(x+3)\). A1

Answer: \(a=3\), \(b=-6\); \(f(x)=(2x+1)(x-2)(x+3)\).

Common mistakes

  • Testing \(x=c\) in order to decide whether \(x+c\) is a factor, instead of whether \(x-c\) is a factor. For \(x+3\), the substitution is \(x=-3\).
  • For a factor \(ax-b\), substituting \(x=a/b\) instead of \(x=b/a\).
  • Stopping after one linear factor has been found, when the question asks for a complete factorisation.

Quick checks

Quick check 1

Evaluate \(f(4)\) for \(f(x)=x^3-6x^2+5x+12\).

Worked solution

\(f(4)=64-96+20+12=0\).

Quick check 2

Given that \(f(-5)=0\), what is the constant term in the linear factor \(x-c\) of \(f(x)\)? (Give the value of \(c\).)

Worked solution

By the factor theorem, \(x-(-5)=x+5\) is a factor, so \(c=-5\).

Quick check 3

Given that \(f\bigl(\dfrac{2}{3}\bigr)=0\), what is the coefficient of \(x\) in the linear factor \(ax-b\) of \(f(x)\) with positive integer \(a\) and with \(a\) and \(b\) coprime?

Worked solution

The extended factor theorem gives the factor \(3x-2\), so the coefficient of \(x\) is \(3\).

B marks are for a correct statement; M marks are for a correct method; A marks are for an accurate result.
