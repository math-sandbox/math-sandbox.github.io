4.6 Implicit differentiation

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton), Chapter 4 Differentiation, pp.99–102. Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: implicit function, explicit function, implicit differentiation, tangent, normal

Explicit and implicit functions

\(y=x^3-2x\) is an explicit function: \(y\) is the subject. An equation such as \(x^3+5xy+y^3=16\) defines \(y\) as an implicit function of \(x\); it cannot easily be rearranged.

Implicit differentiation

Differentiate every term with respect to \(x\), treating \(y\) as a function of \(x\). This is called implicit differentiation. Use the chain rule for powers of \(y\) and the product rule for terms such as \(xy\):

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(y^n\right)=ny^{n-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(xy)=x\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+y$$

Then collect the \(\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) terms on one side and factorise. The result is usually in terms of both \(x\) and \(y\), so you need both coordinates of the point. The gradient of the normal is \(-1\div\) the gradient of the tangent.

Worked example 1: Gradient at a point

Book: Worked examples 4.11–4.12, p.100; Exercise 4F pp.101–102

Find the gradient of the curve \(x^2+3xy-y^2=3\) at the point \((1,2)\).

  1. \(2x+3x\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+3y-2y\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=0\) B1 B1
  2. \(\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}(3x-2y)=-2x-3y\Rightarrow\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{-2x-3y}{3x-2y}\) M1
  3. At \((1,2)\): \(\dfrac{-2-6}{3-4}=8\) A1

Answer: \(8\)

Worked example 2: Equation of a tangent

Book: Worked example 4.13, p.101; Exercise 4F pp.101–102

Find the equation of the tangent to \(x^2y+y^3=10\) at the point \((3,1)\).

  1. \(2xy+x^2\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+3y^2\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=0\) M1 A1
  2. At \((3,1)\): \(6+9\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+3\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=0\Rightarrow\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=-\frac12\) A1
  3. \(y-1=-\frac12(x-3)\) M1
  4. \(x+2y-5=0\) A1

Answer: \(x+2y-5=0\)

Quick checks

Quick check 1

Find the gradient of the tangent to the circle \(x^2+y^2=25\) at the point \((3,4)\).

Worked solution
  1. \(2x+2y\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=0\Rightarrow\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=-\dfrac{x}{y}\)
  2. At \((3,4)\): \(-\dfrac34\)

Answer: −0.75

Quick check 2

Find the gradient of the curve \(x^2+xy+y^2=7\) at the point \((1,2)\).

Worked solution
  1. \(2x+x\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+y+2y\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=0\Rightarrow\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=-\dfrac{2x+y}{x+2y}\)
  2. At \((1,2)\): \(-\dfrac{4}{5}\)

Answer: −0.8

Quick check 3

Find the gradient of the curve \(x^2y+2y^3=6\) at the point \((2,1)\).

Worked solution
  1. \(2xy+x^2\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+6y^2\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=0\Rightarrow\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=-\dfrac{2xy}{x^2+6y^2}\)
  2. At \((2,1)\): \(-\dfrac{4}{10}\)

Answer: −0.4

Marks: M = method, A = accurate answer (depends on M), B = independent mark. Give exact answers unless the question asks for a decimal; differentiate first, substitute second.
