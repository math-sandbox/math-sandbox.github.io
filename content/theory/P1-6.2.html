6.2 Binomial coefficients

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 6 Series, printed pp.160–165. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook notation \(\binom{n}{r}\), its factorial formula, and the binomial theorem for a positive integer \(n\).

Key words: binomial coefficient

The notation \(\binom{n}{r}\)

The entries of row \(n\) of Pascal’s triangle are the binomial coefficients \(\binom{n}{0},\binom{n}{1},\ldots,\binom{n}{n}\). Each binomial coefficient can be found without the triangle:

\[\binom{n}{r}={}^{n}C_{r}=\dfrac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!},\qquad \binom{n}{0}=\binom{n}{n}=1,\qquad \binom{n}{r}=\binom{n}{n-r}.\]

For example, \(\binom{6}{2}=\dfrac{6\times5}{2\times1}=15\).

The binomial theorem

For a positive integer \(n\),

\[(a+b)^n=a^n+\binom{n}{1}a^{n-1}b+\binom{n}{2}a^{n-2}b^2+\cdots+\binom{n}{r}a^{n-r}b^r+\cdots+b^n.\]

The general term is \(\binom{n}{r}a^{n-r}b^r\). To find one coefficient, choose the value of \(r\) that gives the required power of \(x\). An unknown constant in the bracket can be found by setting a coefficient equal to its given value.

Worked example 1: One coefficient

Coursebook, 6.2, the general term. New numbers.

Find the coefficient of \(x^3\) in the expansion of \((2+x)^7\).

  1. The term in \(x^3\) has \(r=3\): \(\binom{7}{3}2^4x^3\). M1
  2. \(35\times16=560\) A1

Answer: \(560\)

Worked example 2: The term independent of \(x\)

Coursebook, 6.2, terms with negative powers. New numbers.

Find the term independent of \(x\) in the expansion of \(\left(x+\dfrac{2}{x}\right)^6\).

  1. The general term is \(\binom{6}{r}x^{6-r}\left(\dfrac{2}{x}\right)^r=\binom{6}{r}2^rx^{6-2r}\), so the power of \(x\) is zero when \(r=3\). M1
  2. \(\binom{6}{3}\times2^3=20\times8=160\) A1

Answer: \(160\)

Common mistakes

  • Using \(\binom{n}{r}\) with the wrong \(r\): the term in \(x^r\) of \((1+kx)^n\) uses \(\binom{n}{r}\), the \((r+1)\)th term.
  • Raising only \(x\), and not the constant in front of it, to the power \(r\).
  • Dropping the sign of a negative term, for example writing \(+80x^3\) for the term in \(x^3\) of \((1-2x)^5\).

Past paper

9709/11/O/N/10 Q2, official QP and official MS.

In the expansion of \((1+ax)^6\), where \(a\) is a constant, the coefficient of \(x\) is \(-30\). Find the coefficient of \(x^3\). [4]

Mark scheme
  1. The term in \(x\) is \(6ax\). B1 Official MS: B1, term in x = 6ax, correct only.
  2. \(6a=-30\), so \(a=-5\). B1 Official MS: B1 ft, equate with −30 to give a = −5; ft from his answer for 6ax.
  3. The term in \(x^3\) is \(\dfrac{6\times5\times4}{3!}a^3x^3=20a^3x^3\). B1 Official MS: B1, term in x³ = (6.5.4/3!) a³, correct only.
  4. Coefficient of \(x^3\) \(=20\times(-5)^3=-2500\) B1 Official MS: B1 ft, −2500; ft for 20 × a³.

The coefficient of \(x^3\) in the expansion of \((1-px)^5\) is \(-2160\). Find the value of the constant \(p\). [3]

Mark scheme
  1. The term in \(x^3\) is \({}^{5}C_{3}(-px)^3=-10p^3x^3\). B1 Official MS: B1, 5C3 [(−)(px)³] soi. Can be part of expansion. Condone omission of − sign.
  2. \(-10p^3=-2160\), so \(p^3=216\). M1 Official MS: M1, (−1)10p³ = −2160 then ÷ and cube root. Condone omission of − sign.

Quick check 1

Evaluate \(\dbinom{7}{3}\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dbinom{7}{3}=\dfrac{7\times6\times5}{3\times2\times1}=35\)

Answer: \(35\)

Quick check 2

Find the coefficient of \(x^3\) in the expansion of \((1-2x)^6\).

Worked solution
  1. The term is \(\dbinom{6}{3}(-2x)^3=20\times(-8)x^3\).

Answer: \(-160\)

Quick check 3

The coefficient of \(x^2\) in the expansion of \((1+x)^n\), where \(n\) is a positive integer, is 28. Find \(n\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}=28\), so \(n^2-n-56=0\).
  2. \((n-8)(n+7)=0\), so \(n=8\).

Answer: \(n=8\)
