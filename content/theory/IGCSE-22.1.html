22.1 Setting up equations to solve problems

Based on Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics Core and Extended Coursebook (Karen Morrison and Nick Hamshaw), Chapter 22 More equations, formulae and functions. The wording is original and follows the published section titles and the standard Cambridge IGCSE treatment of the topic. Printed pp.756–762.

Key words: equation, unknown, solve

From words to an equation

Many problems can be solved by turning the words into an equation. Follow these steps:

  1. Choose a letter for the unknown quantity and say what it stands for, including units.
  2. Write the other quantities in terms of that letter.
  3. Use the information in the question to write an equation.
  4. Solve the equation and answer the question in words, with units.
  5. Check the answer in the original problem.

Useful facts: angles in a triangle add up to \(180^\circ\); the perimeter of a rectangle is \(2\times\text{length}+2\times\text{width}\); time \(=\dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}\).

If the equation contains fractions with the unknown in the denominator, multiply every term by the common denominator. This often gives a quadratic equation. Reject any solution that does not make sense, such as a negative speed or length.

Worked example 1: Cinema tickets

Coursebook, 22.1, forming linear equations. New numbers.

An adult ticket costs $5 more than a child ticket. Two adult tickets and three child tickets cost $45. Find the cost of a child ticket.

  1. Let a child ticket cost $\(c\). An adult ticket costs $\((c+5)\). B1
  2. \(2(c+5)+3c=45\), so \(5c+10=45\) M1
  3. \(5c=35\), so \(c=7\) A1

Answer: $7

Worked example 2: A speed problem

Coursebook, 22.1, equations with fractions. New numbers.

A cyclist rides 30 km at \(x\) km/h and then 30 km at \((x+5)\) km/h. The second ride takes 1 hour less. Find \(x\).

  1. \(\dfrac{30}{x}-\dfrac{30}{x+5}=1\) M1
  2. Multiply by \(x(x+5)\): \(30(x+5)-30x=x(x+5)\), so \(x^2+5x-150=0\) M1
  3. \((x+15)(x-10)=0\), and a speed cannot be negative, so \(x=10\). A1

Answer: \(x=10\)

Common mistakes

  • Not defining the letter, so the final answer is unclear.
  • Forgetting the brackets, for example writing \(2c+5\) instead of \(2(c+5)\) for two adult tickets.
  • Keeping a negative solution for a length, speed or price.

Past paper

0580/22/F/M/22 Q18, official QP and official MS.

Mrs Kohli buys a jacket, 2 shirts and a hat.

The jacket costs $\(x\).

The shirts each cost $24 less than the jacket and the hat costs $16 less than the jacket.

Mrs Kohli spends exactly $100.

Write down an equation in terms of \(x\).

Solve this equation to find the cost of the jacket. [3]

Mark scheme
  1. \(x+2(x-24)+x-16=100\) M1 Official MS: M1 for x + 2(x − 24) + x − 16 = 100 OE.
  2. \(4x-64=100\), so \(4x=164\) M1 Official MS: M2 for 4x = 164 (or M1 for correctly simplifying their equation to the form kx = c, provided at least one part is correct from 2(x − 24) OE or x − 16).
  3. Jacket costs $41. A1 Official MS: 41 from a correct equation, 3 marks. B1 for answer 41 without an equation in x shown.

Past paper

0580/21/M/J/25 Q18, official QP and official MS.

One day, Anya runs 12 km at a speed of \(x\) km/h.

The next day she walks 10 km at a speed of \((x-4)\) km/h.

(a) Write down an expression, in terms of \(x\), for the time she spends running. [1]

(b) Write down an expression, in terms of \(x\), for the time she spends walking. [1]

(c) The time Anya spends walking is 1 hour more than the time she spends running.

Write an equation in terms of \(x\) and show that it simplifies to \(x^2-2x-48=0\). [4]

(d) Use factorisation to solve the equation \(x^2-2x-48=0\). [3]

(e) Find the time Anya spends running. [1]

Mark scheme
  1. (a) \(\dfrac{12}{x}\) B1 Official MS: 12/x, 1 mark.
  2. (b) \(\dfrac{10}{x-4}\) B1 Official MS: 10/(x − 4), 1 mark.
  3. (c) \(\dfrac{10}{x-4}-\dfrac{12}{x}=1\) M1 Official MS: M1, their 10/(x − 4) − their 12/x = 1 OE.
  4. (c) \(10x-12(x-4)=x(x-4)\), so \(10x-12x+48=x^2-4x\) M2 Official MS: M2, correctly multiplying their brackets and clearing algebraic fractions; or M1 for correctly clearing, or correctly collecting into a single fraction, two fractions both with different algebraic denominators.
  5. (c) Leading to \(0=x^2-2x-48\) A1 Official MS: A1, with no errors or omissions seen, dependent on M3.
  6. (d) \((x+6)(x-8)=0\) M2 Official MS: M2; M1 for x(x − 8) + 6(x − 8) or x(x + 6) − 8(x + 6), or (x + a)(x + b) where ab = −48 or a + b = −2.

  8. (e) \(\dfrac{12}{8}=1.5\) h B1 Official MS: 1.5 or 1½, 1 mark; FT 12 ÷ their positive root from (d).

Quick check 1

The length of a rectangle is 5 cm more than its width \(x\) cm. The perimeter is 38 cm. Find \(x\).

Worked solution
  1. \(2x+2(x+5)=38\)
  2. \(4x+10=38\), so \(4x=28\).

Answer: \(x=7\)

Quick check 2

The sum of three consecutive integers is 51. Find the smallest.

Worked solution
  1. \(n+(n+1)+(n+2)=51\)
  2. \(3n+3=51\), so \(3n=48\).

Answer: \(16\)

Quick check 3

The angles of a triangle are \(x^\circ\), \((x+60)^\circ\) and \(2x^\circ\). Find \(x\).

Worked solution
  1. \(4x+60=180\)
  2. \(4x=120\)

Answer: \(x=30\)
