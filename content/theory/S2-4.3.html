4.3 Finding the expectation and variance

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 2 Coursebook (Jayne Kranat), Chapter 4 Continuous random variables, printed pp.88–93 (Key point 4.5, Worked examples 4.6–4.7, Exercise 4C). Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: expectation, mean, variance, probability density function

The mean of a continuous random variable

For a discrete random variable the mean is \(\mathrm{E}(X)=\sum x\,\mathrm{P}(X=x)\). For a continuous random variable, \(\mathrm{P}(X=x)=0\) at every single point, so that sum cannot be used as it stands. Instead, the probability in a very small interval of width \(\delta x\) is about \(\mathrm{f}(x)\,\delta x\), and the sum becomes an integral.

Key point. If \(X\) is a continuous random variable with probability density function \(\mathrm{f}(x)\), then $$\mathrm{E}(X)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\,\mathrm{f}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.$$ In practice the integral is taken only over the interval where \(\mathrm{f}(x)\) is non-zero. If the PDF is defined in pieces, multiply each piece by \(x\) and integrate piece by piece with its own limits.

The variance

The same reasoning gives \(\mathrm{E}(X^2)=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^2\,\mathrm{f}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\). The variance is then found from the usual identity $$\mathrm{Var}(X)=\mathrm{E}(X^2)-\bigl\{\mathrm{E}(X)\bigr\}^2.$$ If the mean will later be used in a variance, keep it as an exact fraction (or work to at least four significant figures) rather than a prematurely rounded decimal.

Worked example 1: A linear PDF

Book: introductory calculation pp.88–89; Exercise 4C Q1

The continuous random variable \(X\) has probability density function \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{2x}{16}\) for \(0\le x\le 4\), and \(\mathrm{f}(x)=0\) otherwise. Find the mean and variance of \(X\).

  1. \(\mathrm{E}(X)=\displaystyle\int_0^4 x\cdot\dfrac{2x}{16}\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{2}{16}\Bigl[\dfrac{x^3}{3}\Bigr]_0^4=\dfrac{1}{8}\cdot\dfrac{64}{3}=\dfrac{8}{3}\) M1 A1
  2. \(\mathrm{E}(X^2)=\displaystyle\int_0^4 x^2\cdot\dfrac{2x}{16}\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{2}{16}\Bigl[\dfrac{x^4}{4}\Bigr]_0^4=\dfrac{1}{8}\cdot 64=8\) M1 A1
  3. \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=8-\left(\dfrac{8}{3}\right)^2=8-\dfrac{64}{9}=\dfrac{8}{9}\) A1

Answer: mean \(\dfrac{8}{3}\approx2.67\); variance \(\dfrac{8}{9}\approx0.889\)

Worked example 2: A quadratic PDF

Book: Worked example 4.6, pp.89–90; Exercise 4C Q1

The continuous random variable \(X\) has PDF \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{3x^2}{8}\) for \(0\le x\le 2\), and \(\mathrm{f}(x)=0\) otherwise. Find \(\mathrm{E}(X)\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\).

  1. \(\mathrm{E}(X)=\displaystyle\int_0^2 x\cdot\dfrac{3x^2}{8}\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{3}{8}\Bigl[\dfrac{x^4}{4}\Bigr]_0^2=\dfrac{3}{8}\cdot 4=\dfrac{3}{2}\) M1 A1
  2. \(\mathrm{E}(X^2)=\displaystyle\int_0^2 x^2\cdot\dfrac{3x^2}{8}\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{3}{8}\Bigl[\dfrac{x^5}{5}\Bigr]_0^2=\dfrac{3}{8}\cdot\dfrac{32}{5}=\dfrac{12}{5}\) M1 A1
  3. \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=\dfrac{12}{5}-\left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^2=\dfrac{12}{5}-\dfrac{9}{4}=\dfrac{48-45}{20}=\dfrac{3}{20}\) A1

Answer: mean \(\dfrac{3}{2}\); variance \(\dfrac{3}{20}=0.15\)

Worked example 3: A piecewise PDF

Book: Worked example 4.7, p.90; Exercise 4C Q2–3

The waiting time, \(X\) minutes, has PDF \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{x}{8}\) for \(0\le x\le 2\), \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{3}{8}\) for \(2\le x\le 4\), and \(\mathrm{f}(x)=0\) otherwise. A notice claims that the mean waiting time is less than 3 minutes. Determine whether this claim is true.

  1. \(\mathrm{E}(X)=\displaystyle\int_0^2 x\cdot\dfrac{x}{8}\,\mathrm{d}x+\displaystyle\int_2^4 x\cdot\dfrac{3}{8}\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{1}{8}\Bigl[\dfrac{x^3}{3}\Bigr]_0^2+\dfrac{3}{8}\Bigl[\dfrac{x^2}{2}\Bigr]_2^4\) M1
  2. \(=\dfrac{1}{8}\cdot\dfrac{8}{3}+\dfrac{3}{16}(16-4)=\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{9}{4}=\dfrac{31}{12}\) A1
  3. \(\dfrac{31}{12}\approx2.58\lt 3\), so the claim is true. A1

Answer: mean \(\dfrac{31}{12}\approx2.58\); the claim is true

Common mistakes

  • Integrating \(\mathrm{f}(x)\) instead of \(x\,\mathrm{f}(x)\) when finding the mean.
  • Forgetting to subtract \(\bigl\{\mathrm{E}(X)\bigr\}^2\) after finding \(\mathrm{E}(X^2)\).
  • Rounding the mean too early and then spoiling the variance.
  • Using the wrong limits, or only one piece of a piecewise PDF.

The rectangular (uniform) distribution on an interval from \(a\) to \(b\), for which \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{1}{b-a}\), is explored in the book: its mean and median both lie at the midpoint \(\dfrac{a+b}{2}\). That special case is not needed for the calculations in this section, which use the integral formulae directly.

Quick checks

Quick check 1

The continuous random variable \(X\) has probability density function \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{2x}{16}\) for \(0\le x\le 4\), and \(\mathrm{f}(x)=0\) otherwise. Find \(\mathrm{E}(X)\), giving your answer correct to 3 significant figures.

Worked solution
  1. \(\mathrm{E}(X)=\dfrac{2}{16}\displaystyle\int_0^4 x^2\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{1}{8}\cdot\dfrac{64}{3}=\dfrac{8}{3}\approx2.67\)

Answer: \(2.67\)

Quick check 2

For the same PDF as Quick check 1, find \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\), giving your answer correct to 3 significant figures.

Worked solution
  1. \(\mathrm{E}(X^2)=\dfrac{2}{16}\displaystyle\int_0^4 x^3\,\mathrm{d}x=8\), so \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=8-\left(\dfrac{8}{3}\right)^2=\dfrac{8}{9}\approx0.889\)

Answer: \(0.889\)

Quick check 3

The continuous random variable \(X\) has probability density function \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{3x^2}{8}\) for \(0\le x\le 2\), and \(\mathrm{f}(x)=0\) otherwise. Find \(\mathrm{E}(X)\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\mathrm{E}(X)=\dfrac{3}{8}\displaystyle\int_0^2 x^3\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{3}{8}\cdot 4=\dfrac{3}{2}\)

Answer: \(\dfrac{3}{2}\)

Quick check 4

For the same PDF as Quick check 3, find \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\mathrm{E}(X^2)=\dfrac{3}{8}\displaystyle\int_0^2 x^4\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{12}{5}\), so \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=\dfrac{12}{5}-\left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^2=\dfrac{3}{20}=0.15\)

Answer: \(0.15\)

Marks: M = method, A = accurate answer (depends on M), B = independent mark. Marks on worked examples are illustrative, not official. Give exact fractions or answers correct to 3 significant figures. \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=\mathrm{E}(X^2)-\{\mathrm{E}(X)\}^2\); do not stop at \(\mathrm{E}(X^2)\).
