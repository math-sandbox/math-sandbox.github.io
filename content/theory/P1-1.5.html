1.5 Solving more complex quadratic equations

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Hugh Neill, Douglas Quadling and Julian Gilbey), Chapter 1 Quadratics, printed pp.15–17 (Worked examples 1.10–1.12 style: a substitution \(y=x^2\), and a substitution \(y=3^x\)). Printed pages were read from P1.pdf (printed 15–17 = PDF 27–29). Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: substitution, quadratic equation, factorisation

Quadratic in a function of \(x\)

You may be asked to solve an equation that is not quadratic in \(x\) itself, but is a quadratic equation in some function of \(x\), such as \(x^2\) or \(3^x\). The method is a substitution: set a new letter equal to that function, solve the quadratic by factorisation (or otherwise), then return to \(x\). Every value of the new letter must be checked: for example \(x^2\) cannot be negative, and \(3^x\) is always positive.

Worked example 1: An equation in \(x^2\)

Book: Worked example 1.10 style, printed p.15 (the book solves \(4x^4-37x^2+9=0\)). New numbers.

Solve the equation \(x^4-10x^2+9=0\).

  1. Let \(u=x^2\). Then \(u^2-10u+9=0\). M1
  2. Factorise: \((u-1)(u-9)=0\), so \(u=1\) or \(u=9\). A1
  3. Hence \(x^2=1\) or \(x^2=9\), so \(x=\pm 1\) or \(x=\pm 3\). A1

Answer: \(x=-3\), \(x=-1\), \(x=1\), \(x=3\)

Worked example 2: An equation in \(2^x\)

Book: Worked example 1.12 style, printed p.16 (the book uses a quadratic in \(3^x\)). New numbers.

Solve the equation \(2^{2x}-10\times 2^x+16=0\).

  1. Note that \(2^{2x}=(2^x)^2\). Let \(u=2^x\). Then \(u^2-10u+16=0\). M1
  2. Factorise: \((u-2)(u-8)=0\), so \(u=2\) or \(u=8\). A1
  3. Hence \(2^x=2\) or \(2^x=8=2^3\), so \(x=1\) or \(x=3\). A1

Answer: \(x=1\), \(x=3\)

Common mistakes

  • Stopping after finding the values of \(u\), and not returning to \(x\).
  • Forgetting the negative solutions of \(x^2=k\) when \(k\gt 0\).
  • Accepting a negative value of \(u\) when \(u=2^x\) or \(u=x^2\), which cannot be negative.

Quick checks

Quick check 1

Solve \(x^4-5x^2+4=0\). What is the greatest real root?

Worked solution
  1. Let \(u=x^2\). Then \(u^2-5u+4=0\), so \((u-1)(u-4)=0\). Hence \(x^2=1\) or \(x^2=4\), and the real roots are \(\pm 1\) and \(\pm 2\). The greatest is \(2\) A1

Answer: \(2\)

Quick check 2

Solve \(x^4-5x^2+4=0\). What is the smaller of the two positive roots?

Worked solution
  1. As in Quick check 1, the positive roots are \(1\) and \(2\). The smaller is \(1\) A1

Answer: \(1\)

Quick check 3

Solve \(2^{2x}-6\times 2^x+8=0\). What is the greater of the two real values of \(x\)?

Worked solution
  1. Let \(u=2^x\). Then \(u^2-6u+8=0\), so \((u-2)(u-4)=0\). Hence \(2^x=2\) or \(2^x=4\), so \(x=1\) or \(x=2\). The greater value is \(2\) A1

Answer: \(2\)

Marks: M = method, A = accurate answer (depends on M), B = independent mark. Leave exact values exact.
