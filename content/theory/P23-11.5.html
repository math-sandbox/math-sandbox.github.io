11.5 Loci

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 11 Complex numbers, printed pp.288–296. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook loci |z − a| = r, |z − a| = |z − b| and arg(z − a) = θ, and regions defined by inequalities.

Key words: locus, Argand diagram, modulus of a complex number, argument, perpendicular bisector

Distance on an Argand diagram

For complex numbers \(z\) and \(a\), \(|z-a|\) is the distance between the points representing \(z\) and \(a\). A locus is the set of points that satisfy a condition.

Three standard loci

  • \(|z-a|=r\): a circle with centre \(a\) and radius \(r\). Note \(|z+3-2\mathrm{i}|=|z-(-3+2\mathrm{i})|\), so the centre is \(-3+2\mathrm{i}\).
  • \(|z-a|=|z-b|\): the perpendicular bisector of the line joining \(a\) and \(b\).
  • \(\arg(z-a)=\theta\): a half-line from \(a\) (not including \(a\)) at angle \(\theta\) to the positive real direction.

Regions

\(|z-a|\le r\) is the inside of the circle, including the circle; \(|z-a|\ge r\) is the outside. \(|z-a|\le|z-b|\) is the half-plane on the side of the bisector nearer to \(a\). Draw each boundary and shade the points that satisfy all the conditions.

Greatest and least values of |z|

For points on the circle \(|z-a|=r\) with \(O\) outside the circle, the least value of \(|z|\) is \(|a|-r\) and the greatest is \(|a|+r\), both along the line through \(O\) and the centre.

Worked example 1: A circle

Coursebook, 11.5, a circle locus. New numbers.

Sketch the locus \(|z-2+\mathrm{i}|=3\) and find the greatest value of \(|z|\) on it.

  1. \(|z-(2-\mathrm{i})|=3\): circle centre \((2, -1)\), radius 3 B1 B1
  2. Distance of centre from \(O\): \(\sqrt5\) M1
  3. Greatest \(|z|=\sqrt5+3\) A1

Answer: \(3+\sqrt5\)

Worked example 2: A perpendicular bisector

Coursebook, 11.5, the locus |z − a| = |z − b|. New numbers.

Find the Cartesian equation of the locus \(|z-4|=|z-2\mathrm{i}|\).

  1. \((x-4)^2+y^2=x^2+(y-2)^2\) M1
  2. \(-8x+16=-4y+4\) A1
  3. \(y=2x-3\) A1

Answer: \(y=2x-3\)

Common mistakes

  • Taking the centre of \(|z+3-2\mathrm{i}|=2\) as \(3-2\mathrm{i}\) instead of \(-3+2\mathrm{i}\).
  • Including the end point \(a\) on the half-line \(\arg(z-a)=\theta\).
  • Shading the wrong side of a boundary: test one point, such as the origin.

Quick check 1

Find the least value of \(|z|\) for \(|z-(4+3\mathrm{i})|=2\).

Worked solution
  1. \(|4+3\mathrm{i}|=5\)
  2. \(5-2\)

Answer: \(3\)

Quick check 2

Find the greatest value of \(|z|\) for \(|z-(-6+8\mathrm{i})|=3\).

Worked solution
  1. \(|-6+8\mathrm{i}|=10\)
  2. \(10+3\)

Answer: \(13\)

Quick check 3

Find the real value of \(z\) on the locus \(|z-(1+2\mathrm{i})|=|z-(3+4\mathrm{i})|\).

Worked solution
  1. \((x-1)^2+4=(x-3)^2+16\)
  2. \(4x=20\)

Answer: \(5\)
