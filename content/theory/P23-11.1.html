11.1 Imaginary numbers

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 11 Complex numbers, printed pp.267–268. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook definition of i as the square root of −1 and its use to solve quadratic equations with a negative discriminant.

Key words: imaginary number, complex number, discriminant

The number i

The equation \(x^2=-1\) has no real solution. We define a new number \(\mathrm{i}\) with \(\mathrm{i}^2=-1\), so \(\mathrm{i}=\sqrt{-1}\). A number such as \(3\mathrm{i}\) or \(-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{i}\) is an imaginary number.

For a positive real number \(a\), \(\sqrt{-a}=\sqrt{a}\,\sqrt{-1}=\sqrt{a}\,\mathrm{i}\). For example \(\sqrt{-25}=5\mathrm{i}\) and \(\sqrt{-12}=2\sqrt3\,\mathrm{i}\).

Powers of i

\(\mathrm{i}^2=-1\), \(\mathrm{i}^3=-\mathrm{i}\), \(\mathrm{i}^4=1\), and then the pattern repeats.

Quadratic equations with a negative discriminant

If the discriminant \(b^2-4ac\) of \(ax^2+bx+c=0\) is negative, the roots are not real. Using \(\mathrm{i}\), the quadratic formula still works and gives two roots of the form \(p\pm q\mathrm{i}\). Each root is a complex number, with a real part and an imaginary part.

Worked example 1: Completing the square

Coursebook, 11.1, a quadratic with complex roots. New numbers.

Solve \(x^2-6x+13=0\).

  1. \((x-3)^2-9+13=0\), so \((x-3)^2=-4\) M1
  2. \(x-3=\pm2\mathrm{i}\), so \(x=3\pm2\mathrm{i}\) A1

Answer: \(x=3+2\mathrm{i}\) or \(x=3-2\mathrm{i}\)

Worked example 2: The quadratic formula

Coursebook, 11.1, using the formula with a negative discriminant. New numbers.

Solve \(2x^2+2x+5=0\).

  1. \(b^2-4ac=4-40=-36\) B1
  2. \(x=\dfrac{-2\pm\sqrt{-36}}{4}=\dfrac{-2\pm6\mathrm{i}}{4}\) M1
  3. \(x=-\tfrac{1}{2}\pm\tfrac{3}{2}\mathrm{i}\) A1

Answer: \(x=-\tfrac{1}{2}\pm\tfrac{3}{2}\mathrm{i}\)

Common mistakes

  • Writing \(\sqrt{-9}=-3\) instead of \(3\mathrm{i}\).
  • Forgetting that \(\mathrm{i}^2=-1\), not \(1\).
  • Giving only one root: complex roots of a real quadratic come in pairs \(p\pm q\mathrm{i}\).

Quick check 1

Solve \(x^2+2x+5=0\) and give the imaginary part of the root with positive imaginary part.

Worked solution
  1. \((x+1)^2=-4\)
  2. \(x=-1\pm2\mathrm{i}\)

Answer: \(2\)

Quick check 2

Solve \(x^2+9=0\).

Worked solution
  1. \(x^2=-9\)
  2. \(x=\pm\sqrt{9}\,\mathrm{i}\)

Answer: \(x=\pm3\mathrm{i}\)

Quick check 3

Find the discriminant of \(2x^2+2x+5=0\).
