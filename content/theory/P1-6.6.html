6.6 Further arithmetic and geometric series

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 6 Series, printed pp.180–182. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook problems that use arithmetic and geometric progressions together.

Key words: arithmetic progression, geometric progression, sum to infinity

Telling the two types apart

In an arithmetic progression the difference between consecutive terms is constant. In a geometric progression the ratio of consecutive terms is constant. Summary of the formulae:

  • Arithmetic: \(u_n=a+(n-1)d\) and \(S_n=\dfrac{n}{2}\bigl(2a+(n-1)d\bigr)\).
  • Geometric: \(u_n=ar^{n-1}\), \(S_n=\dfrac{a(1-r^n)}{1-r}\) and, for \(-1\lt r\lt 1\), \(S_\infty=\dfrac{a}{1-r}\).

Problems that use both

Some questions give the same first terms and ask you to treat the progression first as geometric and then as arithmetic. Others link terms of one progression to terms of the other, for example the first three terms of a geometric progression being three terms of an arithmetic progression. Write each condition as an equation and use the property of a geometric progression: if \(x\), \(y\), \(z\) are consecutive terms, then \(\dfrac{y}{x}=\dfrac{z}{y}\), so \(y^2=xz\).

Worked example 1: The same two terms

Coursebook, 6.6, arithmetic and geometric cases. New numbers.

The first two terms of a progression are 20 and 16. (a) Given that the progression is geometric, find the sum to infinity. (b) Given instead that it is arithmetic, find the sum of the first 10 terms.

  1. (a) \(r=\dfrac{16}{20}=0.8\), so \(S_\infty=\dfrac{20}{1-0.8}\) M1
  2. (a) \(S_\infty=100\) A1
  3. (b) \(d=-4\) B1
  4. (b) \(S_{10}=\dfrac{10}{2}\bigl(40+9\times(-4)\bigr)\) M1
  5. (b) \(=5\times4=20\) A1

Answer: (a) \(100\) (b) \(20\)

Worked example 2: Terms of one progression inside the other

Coursebook, 6.6, linked progressions. New numbers.

The first, second and third terms of a geometric progression are the first, fourth and tenth terms of an arithmetic progression with first term 6 and common difference \(d\), where \(d\ne 0\). Find \(d\) and the common ratio of the geometric progression.

  1. The terms are \(6\), \(6+3d\) and \(6+9d\), so \((6+3d)^2=6(6+9d)\). M1
  2. \(36+36d+9d^2=36+54d\), so \(9d^2=18d\) and \(d=2\) since \(d\ne0\). A1
  3. The terms are 6, 12, 24, so \(r=\dfrac{12}{6}=2\). A1

Answer: \(d=2\) and \(r=2\)

Common mistakes

  • Using a geometric formula in the arithmetic part of a question, or the other way round. Write AP or GP next to each line of working.
  • Finding a sum to infinity when the common ratio is not between \(-1\) and \(1\).
  • Dividing both sides by \(d\) without saying \(d\ne0\), and losing a solution or including an impossible one.

Past paper

9709/13/M/J/14 Q2, official QP and official MS.

The first term in a progression is 36 and the second term is 32.

(i) Given that the progression is geometric, find the sum to infinity. [2]

(ii) Given instead that the progression is arithmetic, find the number of terms in the progression if the sum of all the terms is 0. [3]

Mark scheme
  1. (i) \(r=\dfrac{32}{36}=\dfrac{8}{9}\) and \(S_\infty=\dfrac{36}{1-\frac{8}{9}}\) M1 Official MS: M1, method for r and S∞, with |r| less than 1.
  2. (i) \(S_\infty=36\div\dfrac{1}{9}=324\) A1 Official MS: A1, 324.
  3. (ii) \(d=-4\) B1 Official MS: B1, d = −4.
  4. (ii) \(0=\dfrac{n}{2}\bigl(72+(n-1)(-4)\bigr)\) M1 Official MS: M1, Sn formula with a value for d other than 8/9.
  5. (ii) \(72-4n+4=0\), so \(n=19\) A1 Official MS: A1, n = 19; condone n = 0 but no other solution.

Past paper

9709/11/O/N/15 Q8, official QP and official MS.

The first term of a progression is \(4x\) and the second term is \(x^2\).

(i) For the case where the progression is arithmetic with a common difference of 12, find the possible values of \(x\) and the corresponding values of the third term. [4]

(ii) For the case where the progression is geometric with a sum to infinity of 8, find the third term. [4]

Mark scheme
  1. (i) \(x^2-4x=12\) M1 Official MS: M1; 4x − x² = 12 scores M1A0.
  2. (i) \((x-6)(x+2)=0\), so \(x=-2\) or \(x=6\) A1 Official MS: A1, x = −2 or 6.
  3. (i) When \(x=-2\) the third term is \((-2)^2+12=16\). A1 Official MS: A1, third term 16. SC1 for 16, 48 after x = 2, −6.
  4. (i) When \(x=6\) the third term is \(6^2+12=48\). A1 Official MS: A1, third term 48.
  5. (ii) \(r=\dfrac{x^2}{4x}=\dfrac{x}{4}\) M1 Official MS: M1, r = x/4 soi.
  6. (ii) \(\dfrac{4x}{1-\frac{x}{4}}=8\) M1 Official MS: M1, sum to infinity equation.
  7. (ii) \(4x=8-2x\), so \(x=\dfrac{4}{3}\) and \(r=\dfrac{1}{3}\) A1 Official MS: A1, x = 4/3 or r = 1/3.
  8. (ii) Third term \(=x^2\times\dfrac{x}{4}=\dfrac{16}{9}\times\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{16}{27}\) A1 Official MS: A1, 16/27 or 0.593. An alternative method is also given in the MS.

Quick check 1

The first term of a progression is 18 and the second term is 12. Given that the progression is geometric, find the sum to infinity.

Worked solution
  1. \(r=\dfrac{12}{18}=\dfrac{2}{3}\)
  2. \(S_\infty=\dfrac{18}{1-\frac{2}{3}}\)

Answer: \(54\)

Quick check 2

The first term of a progression is 18 and the second term is 12. Given that the progression is arithmetic, find the sixth term.

Worked solution
  1. \(d=-6\)
  2. \(u_6=18+5\times(-6)\)

Answer: \(-12\)

Quick check 3

An arithmetic progression has first term 4 and common difference 2. A geometric progression also has first term 4, and its sum to infinity is equal to the sum of the first 4 terms of the arithmetic progression. Find the common ratio of the geometric progression.

Worked solution
  1. \(S_4=\dfrac{4}{2}(8+3\times2)=28\)
  2. \(\dfrac{4}{1-r}=28\), so \(1-r=\dfrac{1}{7}\).

Answer: \(r=\dfrac{6}{7}\)
