6.3 Expectation and variance of a discrete random variable

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 1 Coursebook (Dean Chalmers), Chapter 6 Probability distributions, printed pp.156–161. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook definitions of E(X) and Var(X).

Key words: expected value, variance of a discrete random variable, probability distribution

Expectation

The expectation (mean) of a discrete random variable \(X\) is the long-run average value of \(X\) over many repetitions of the experiment:

\[E(X)=\sum x\,P(X=x).\]

\(E(X)\) is often called the expected value of \(X\). It does not have to be a value that \(X\) can actually take. The expected number of heads when a coin is tossed three times is 1.5.

Variance

The variance measures how spread out the values of \(X\) are about the mean. Find \(E(X^2)=\sum x^2P(X=x)\) first, then

\[\mathrm{Var}(X)=E(X^2)-\bigl(E(X)\bigr)^2.\]

The standard deviation is \(\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(X)}\). A variance can never be negative; if you get a negative answer, check your working.

Worked example 1: Mean and variance

Coursebook, 6.3, expectation and variance. New numbers.

\(X\) takes the values 1, 2, 4 with probabilities 0.5, 0.3, 0.2. Find \(E(X)\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\).

  1. \(E(X)=0.5+0.6+0.8=1.9\) B1
  2. \(E(X^2)=1\times0.5+4\times0.3+16\times0.2=4.9\) M1
  3. \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=4.9-1.9^2=1.29\) A1

Answer: \(E(X)=1.9\), \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=1.29\)

Worked example 2: Using a given mean

Coursebook, 6.3, unknown probabilities. New numbers.

\(X\) takes the values 1, 2, 3 with probabilities \(a\), 0.3, \(b\). Given \(E(X)=2.2\), find \(a\) and \(b\).

  1. \(a+b=0.7\) B1
  2. \(a+0.6+3b=2.2\), so \(a+3b=1.6\) B1
  3. Subtract: \(2b=0.9\), so \(b=0.45\) and \(a=0.25\) M1 A1

Answer: \(a=0.25\), \(b=0.45\)

Common mistakes

  • Using \(E(X^2)\) as the variance and forgetting to subtract \(\bigl(E(X)\bigr)^2\).
  • Squaring the probabilities instead of the values when finding \(E(X^2)\).
  • Taking \(E(X)\) as the mean of the values without weighting by the probabilities.

Quick check 1

\(X\) takes the values 1, 2, 3 only, with \(P(X=1)=0.2\) and \(P(X=2)=0.5\). Find \(E(X)\).

Worked solution
  1. \(P(X=3)=0.3\)
  2. \(E(X)=0.2+1.0+0.9\)

Answer: \(2.1\)

Quick check 2

\(P(X=0)=0.3\), \(P(X=1)=0.4\), \(P(X=2)=0.3\). Find \(\mathrm{Var}(X)\).

Worked solution
  1. \(E(X)=0.4+0.6=1\)
  2. \(E(X^2)=0.4+1.2=1.6\)
  3. \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=1.6-1^2\)

Answer: \(0.6\)

Quick check 3

\(X\) takes the values 0, 1, 2 with \(P(X=0)=0.2\), \(P(X=1)=p\), \(P(X=2)=q\), and \(E(X)=1.3\). Find \(p\).

Worked solution
  1. \(p+q=0.8\) and \(p+2q=1.3\)
  2. Subtract: \(q=0.5\), so \(p=0.3\).

Answer: \(p=0.3\)
