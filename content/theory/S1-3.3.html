3.3 Variance and standard deviation

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 1 Coursebook, Chapter 3, printed pp.65–82 (variance and standard deviation). Printed pages were read from S1.pdf (printed 65–82 = PDF 77–94). Own wording and new numbers. Teaching covers Key point 3.3 (the variance and standard deviation of ungrouped and grouped data, with divisor \(n\) or \(\Sigma f\)) and the use of the combined totals when two data sets are put together (Key point 3.4).

Key words: variance of a data set, standard deviation of a data set

The variance of a data set of \(n\) values is \(\dfrac{\Sigma(x-\bar x)^2}{n}=\dfrac{\Sigma x^2}{n}-\bar x^2\), where \(\bar x=\dfrac{\Sigma x}{n}\). The standard deviation of a data set is the square root of the variance. A useful memory phrase is “mean of the squares minus the square of the mean”.

For grouped data the same idea is used with frequencies: the divisor is \(\Sigma f\), and \(\bar x=\dfrac{\Sigma xf}{\Sigma f}\). In this syllabus the divisor is \(n\) (or \(\Sigma f\)), not \(n-1\).

When two data sets are combined, the mean and variance of the combined set are found from the combined totals \(\Sigma x\) and \(\Sigma x^2\) (Key point 3.4). A known mean and standard deviation can be turned back into those totals first.

Worked example 1

Book: Key point 3.3, printed pp.65–82. New integer numbers.

The four values 1, 3, 5 and 7 have mean 4. Find the variance of these values.

  1. \(\dfrac{(1-4)^2+(3-4)^2+(5-4)^2+(7-4)^2}{4}=\dfrac{9+1+1+9}{4}=5\). A1

Worked example 2

Book: Key point 3.3, printed pp.65–82. New integer numbers.

For the four values 2, 4, 6 and 8, \(\Sigma x=20\) and \(\Sigma x^2=120\). Find the variance.

  1. \(\dfrac{120}{4}-5^2=30-25=5\). A1

Common mistakes

  • Dividing by \(n-1\) rather than by \(n\), which is not the formula used in this syllabus for a data set.
  • Computing \(\dfrac{\Sigma x^2}{n}-\bar x\) and forgetting to square the mean, or taking the variance to be the standard deviation.
