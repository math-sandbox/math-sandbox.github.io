7.1 Derivatives and gradient functions

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 7 Differentiation, printed pp.191–198. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook approach from gradients of chords to the derivative and the rule for powers of x.

Key words: derivative, gradient function, chord

From chords to tangents

The gradient of a curve at a point \(P\) is the gradient of the tangent at \(P\). Take a second point \(Q\) on the curve close to \(P\). As \(Q\) moves towards \(P\), the gradient of the chord \(PQ\) gets closer and closer to the gradient of the tangent. The limit is the gradient of the curve at \(P\).

The gradient function, or derivative, is written \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) or \(f'(x)\). It gives the gradient of the curve at any value of \(x\).

Rules

  • If \(y=x^n\), then \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=nx^{n-1}\). This works for any real \(n\), including negative and fractional powers.
  • If \(y=kx^n\), then \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=knx^{n-1}\).
  • The derivative of a constant is 0.
  • Differentiate a sum term by term.

Before differentiating, write roots and fractions as powers: \(\sqrt{x}=x^{\frac{1}{2}}\) and \(\dfrac{1}{x^2}=x^{-2}\).

Worked example 1: Differentiating powers

Coursebook, 7.1, derivatives of powers. New numbers.

Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) for \(y=4x^3-\dfrac{2}{x}+5\).

  1. \(y=4x^3-2x^{-1}+5\) B1
  2. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=12x^2+2x^{-2}\) M1 A1

Answer: \(12x^2+\dfrac{2}{x^2}\)

Worked example 2: Gradient at a point

Coursebook, 7.1, gradient of a curve. New numbers.

Find the gradient of \(y=x^2(x-4)\) at the point \((1, -3)\).

  1. \(y=x^3-4x^2\) B1
  2. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=3x^2-8x\) M1
  3. At \(x=1\): \(3-8=-5\) A1

Answer: \(-5\)

Common mistakes

  • Differentiating a product term by term without expanding first: \(x^2(x-4)\) must be multiplied out.
  • Forgetting that the derivative of a constant is zero.
  • Errors with negative powers: the derivative of \(x^{-1}\) is \(-x^{-2}\), not \(-x^{0}\).

The equation of a curve is \(y=(x-3)\sqrt{x+1}+3\). The following points lie on the curve. Non-exact values are rounded to 4 decimal places.

\(A(2, k)\)  \( B(2.9, 2.8025)\)  \( C(2.99, 2.9800)\)  \( D(2.999, 2.9980)\)  \( E(3, 3)\)

(a) Find \(k\), giving your answer correct to 4 decimal places. [1]

(b) Find the gradient of \(AE\), giving your answer correct to 4 decimal places. [1]

The gradients of \(BE\), \(CE\) and \(DE\), rounded to 4 decimal places, are 1.9748, 1.9975 and 1.9997 respectively.

(c) State, giving a reason for your answer, what the values of the four gradients suggest about the gradient of the curve at the point \(E\). [2]

Mark scheme
  1. (a) \(k=(2-3)\sqrt{3}+3=3-\sqrt{3}=1.2679\) B1 Official MS: B1, AWRT 1.2679; ISW if correct answer seen.
  2. (b) Gradient \(AE=\dfrac{3-1.2679}{3-2}=1.7321\) B1 Official MS: B1, AWRT 1.7321; ISW if correct answer seen.
  3. (c) The gradient at \(E\) is 2. *B1 Official MS: *B1, sight of 2 or 2.0000 or two in reference to the gradient.
  4. (c) Because the gradient at \(E\) is the limit of the gradients of the chords as the \(x\)-value tends to 3. B1 Official MS: DB1, the gradient at E is the limit of the gradients of the chords as x tends to 3 or δx tends to 0; allow gets nearer, approaches, tends, almost, approximately.

A curve has equation \(y=3x^3-6x^2+4x+2\). Show that the gradient of the curve is never negative. [3]

Mark scheme
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=9x^2-12x+4\) M1 Official MS: M1, attempt to differentiate.
  2. Correct derivative \(9x^2-12x+4\) A1 Official MS: A1, correct derivative.
  3. \(9x^2-12x+4=(3x-2)^2\ge 0\), so the gradient is never negative. A1 Official MS: A1, (3x − 2)² ≥ 0.

Quick check 1

Find the gradient of \(y=2x^3-5x\) at \(x=2\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=6x^2-5\)
  2. \(6\times4-5\)

Answer: \(19\)

Quick check 2

Find the gradient of \(y=6\sqrt{x}+2x^2\) at \(x=9\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=3x^{-\frac{1}{2}}+4x\)
  2. \(\dfrac{3}{3}+36\)

Answer: \(37\)

Quick check 3

Find the \(x\)-coordinate of the point on \(y=x^2+3x\) where the gradient is 7.

Worked solution
  1. \(2x+3=7\)

Answer: \(x=2\)
