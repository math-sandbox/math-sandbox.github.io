7.3 Binomial expansion of \((1+x)^n\) for values of \(n\) that are not positive integers

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 and 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, Julian Gilbey), Chapter 7, printed pp.174–176. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook expansion of \((1+x)^n\) for a rational index that is not a positive integer, and the condition \(\lvert x\rvert<1\) for validity.

Key words: binomial expansion

The expansion and its range of validity

For any rational number \(n\) that is not a positive integer, and for \(\lvert x\rvert<1\),

\[(1+x)^n=1+nx+\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2!}x^2+\dfrac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{3!}x^3+\cdots.\]

The binomial expansion of \((1+x)^n\), when \(n\) is not a positive integer, is an infinite series. The same pattern continues for as many terms as are required. If the given expression is \((1+ax)^n\), the expansion is valid for \(\lvert ax\rvert<1\). Coefficients are usually asked for in simplified form.

Worked example 1: A square-root expansion

Coursebook, 7.3, square-root style. New expansion of \(\sqrt{1+4x}\) up to the term in \(x^2\).

Expand \(\sqrt{1+4x}\) in ascending powers of \(x\) up to and including the term in \(x^2\), and state the range of values of \(x\) for which the expansion is valid.

  1. \((1+4x)^{1/2}=1+\dfrac{1}{2}(4x)+\dfrac{\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\bigl(-\frac{1}{2}\bigr)}{2}(4x)^2+\cdots=1+2x-2x^2+\cdots\). M1
  2. The expansion is valid for \(\lvert 4x\rvert<1\), that is \(-\dfrac{1}{4}<x<\dfrac{1}{4}\). A1

Answer: \(1+2x-2x^2\); \(-\dfrac{1}{4}<x<\dfrac{1}{4}\)

Worked example 2: A cube-root expansion

Coursebook, 7.3, cube-root style, in the same family as the official past paper on this page but stopping at the term in \(x^2\) and using a new coefficient inside the bracket.

Expand \(\sqrt[3]{1-3x}\) in ascending powers of \(x\) up to and including the term in \(x^2\).

  1. \((1-3x)^{1/3}=1+\dfrac{1}{3}(-3x)+\dfrac{\bigl(\frac{1}{3}\bigr)\bigl(-\frac{2}{3}\bigr)}{2}(-3x)^2+\cdots\). M1
  2. This simplifies to \(1-x-x^2\). A1

Answer: \(1-x-x^2\)

Common mistakes

  • Using the expansion without the condition \(\lvert ax\rvert<1\), or writing a closed inequality when the series is valid only for a strict inequality.
  • Leaving binomial coefficients unsimplified when the question asks for simplified coefficients.

9709/31/M/J/11 Q1, official QP and official MS.

Expand \(\sqrt[3]{1-6x}\) in ascending powers of \(x\) up to and including the term in \(x^3\), simplifying the coefficients. [4]

Mark scheme
  1. A first-order term of the form \(1+kx\), with \(k=\pm 6\) or \(k=\pm\dfrac{1}{3}\), is obtained. M1 Official MS: M1, obtain \(1+kx\), where \(k=\pm 6\) or \(\pm\frac{1}{3}\).
  2. The first two terms are \(1-2x\). A1 Official MS: A1. (The official alternative method differentiates to obtain \(f'(x)=-2(1-6x)^{-2/3}\) and hence the same first two terms.)
  3. The term in \(x^2\) is \(-4x^2\). A1
  4. The term in \(x^3\) is \(-\dfrac{40}{3}x^3\), or an equivalent. A1 Official MS: A1, \(-\dfrac{40}{3}x^3\) or equivalent. An equivalent four-mark alternative method using successive derivatives at the origin is given in the official MS.

Past paper

9709/32/M/J/16 Q2, official QP and official MS.

Expand \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-2x}}\) in ascending powers of \(x\), up to and including the term in \(x^3\), simplifying the coefficients. [4]

Mark scheme
  1. A correct unsimplified version of the \(x\) or \(x^2\) or \(x^3\) term is stated. M1 Official MS: M1. Symbolic binomial coefficients are not sufficient for the M mark.
  2. The first two terms are \(1+x\). A1 Official MS: A1, \(1+x\).
  3. The term in \(x^2\) is \(\dfrac{3}{2}x^2\). A1 Official MS: A1, \(\dfrac{3}{2}x^2\).
  4. The term in \(x^3\) is \(\dfrac{5}{2}x^3\). A1 Official MS: A1, \(\dfrac{5}{2}x^3\). The expansion is \(1+x+\dfrac{3}{2}x^2+\dfrac{5}{2}x^3\).

Quick check 1

Find the coefficient of \(x\) in the expansion of \((1+3x)^{-1}\) in ascending powers of \(x\).

Worked solution
  1. The coefficient is \((-1)\times 3=-3\).

Answer: \(-3\)

Quick check 2

Find the coefficient of \(x^2\) in the expansion of \((1+3x)^{-1}\) in ascending powers of \(x\).

Worked solution
  1. The coefficient is \(\dfrac{(-1)(-2)}{2}\times 9=9\).

Answer: \(9\)

Quick check 3

Find the coefficient of \(x\) in the expansion of \(\sqrt{1-2x}\) in ascending powers of \(x\).

Worked solution
  1. The coefficient is \(\dfrac{1}{2}\times(-2)=-1\).

Answer: \(-1\)
