4.4 Derivatives of natural logarithmic functions

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, Julianne Hughes), Chapter 4 Differentiation, pp.91–94 (Key points 4.5–4.6, Worked examples 4.7–4.8, Exercise 4D). Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: natural logarithm, chain rule, product rule, quotient rule

The derivative of \(\ln x\)

The natural logarithm is the inverse of the exponential function: if \(y=\ln x\), then \(\mathrm{e}^y=x\). Differentiating both sides with respect to \(y\) gives \(\mathrm{e}^y=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}y}\). Replacing \(\mathrm{e}^y\) by \(x\) and taking the reciprocal,

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(\ln x)=\frac{1}{x}\qquad(x\gt 0).$$

The domain \(x\gt 0\) is needed because \(\ln x\) is defined only for positive \(x\). (The more general result \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\ln|x|=\dfrac{1}{x}\) for \(x\neq0\) is consistent with this, but in this section we usually work with a positive argument.)

The derivative of \(\ln\mathrm{f}(x)\)

For a composite logarithm, put \(u=\mathrm{f}(x)\) so that \(y=\ln u\). By the chain rule,

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigl(\ln\mathrm{f}(x)\bigr)=\frac{\mathrm{f}'(x)}{\mathrm{f}(x)},$$ provided \(\mathrm{f}(x)\gt 0\). In particular,

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigl[\ln(ax+b)\bigr]=\frac{a}{ax+b}\qquad(a\neq0,\ ax+b\gt 0).$$

The derivative of a constant times a logarithm is that constant times \(\mathrm{f}'/\mathrm{f}\). Logarithm laws can be used first when they help: \(\ln\bigl(\mathrm{f}(x)\bigr)^n=n\ln\mathrm{f}(x)\) and \(\ln\sqrt{\mathrm{f}(x)}=\dfrac12\ln\mathrm{f}(x)\). Combine the rule with the product rule and the quotient rule when \(\ln\mathrm{f}(x)\) is multiplied or divided by another function of \(x\).

Worked example 1: Differentiating logarithms

Book: Worked example 4.7, pp.92–93; Exercise 4D Q1, p.94

Differentiate with respect to \(x\): (a) \(\ln 5x\) (b) \(\ln(3x+4)\) (c) \(\ln\sqrt{6-x}\).

  1. (a) \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(\ln 5x)=\dfrac{5}{5x}=\dfrac{1}{x}\) B1
  2. (b) \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigl[\ln(3x+4)\bigr]=\dfrac{3}{3x+4}\) B1
  3. (c) Method 1: \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigl[\ln(6-x)^{1/2}\bigr]=\dfrac{\frac12(6-x)^{-1/2}(-1)}{(6-x)^{1/2}}=-\dfrac{1}{2(6-x)}\) M1 A1
  4. Method 2: \(\ln\sqrt{6-x}=\dfrac12\ln(6-x)\), so \(\dfrac12\cdot\dfrac{-1}{6-x}=-\dfrac{1}{2(6-x)}\) B1

Answer: (a) \(\dfrac{1}{x}\) (b) \(\dfrac{3}{3x+4}\) (c) \(-\dfrac{1}{2(6-x)}\)

Worked example 2: Product and quotient rules

Book: Worked example 4.8, p.93; Exercise 4D Q3, p.94

Differentiate with respect to \(x\): (a) \(3x^2\ln 2x\) (b) \(\dfrac{\ln(3x)}{x^2}\).

  1. (a) Product rule: \(3x^2\cdot\dfrac{2}{2x}+\ln 2x\cdot 6x=3x+6x\ln 2x\) M1 A1
  2. \(=3x(1+2\ln 2x)\) A1
  3. (b) Quotient rule: \(\dfrac{x^2\cdot\dfrac{3}{3x}-\ln(3x)\cdot 2x}{x^4}=\dfrac{x-2x\ln(3x)}{x^4}=\dfrac{1-2\ln(3x)}{x^3}\) M1 A1

Answer: (a) \(3x(1+2\ln 2x)\) (b) \(\dfrac{1-2\ln(3x)}{x^3}\)

Worked example 3: A gradient at a given point

Book: Exercise 4D Q4, p.94

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=\ln(2x+5)\) at the point where \(x=0\).

  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{2}{2x+5}\) M1
  2. At \(x=0\): \(\dfrac{2}{5}=0.4\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{2}{5}=0.4\)

Quick checks

Quick check 1

Differentiate \(\ln(4x-1)\) with respect to \(x\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{4}{4x-1}\)

Answer: \(\dfrac{4}{4x-1}\)

Quick check 2

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=\ln(3x+1)\) at the point where \(x=1\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{3}{3x+1}\)
  2. At \(x=1\): \(\dfrac{3}{4}=0.75\)

Answer: \(0.75\)

Quick check 3

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=x\ln x\) at the point where \(x=1\).

Worked solution
  1. Product rule: \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\ln x+x\cdot\dfrac{1}{x}=\ln x+1\)
  2. At \(x=1\): \(\ln 1+1=0+1=1\)

Answer: \(1\)

Quick check 4

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=\ln(5-2x)\) at the point where \(x=0\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{-2}{5-2x}\)
  2. At \(x=0\): \(-\dfrac{2}{5}=-0.4\)

Answer: \(-0.4\)

Marks: M = method, A = accurate answer (depends on M), B = independent mark. Marks on worked examples are illustrative, not official. Give exact answers unless the question asks for a decimal. Differentiate first, substitute second. The argument of \(\ln\) must be positive.
