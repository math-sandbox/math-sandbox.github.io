7.1 Improper algebraic fractions

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 and 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, Julian Gilbey), Chapter 7, printed pp.166–167. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook method of dividing an improper algebraic fraction so that it is written as a polynomial plus a proper fraction.

Key words: improper fraction

Proper and improper fractions

A rational expression \(\dfrac{p(x)}{q(x)}\), where \(p\) and \(q\) are polynomials, is a proper fraction if the degree of \(p\) is strictly less than the degree of \(q\). Otherwise it is an improper fraction. An improper fraction is first rewritten, by polynomial division (or by an equivalent rearrangement), in the form

\[ \dfrac{p(x)}{q(x)}=s(x)+\dfrac{r(x)}{q(x)}, \]

where \(s(x)\) is a polynomial (the quotient) and \(\dfrac{r(x)}{q(x)}\) is a proper fraction (the remainder over the divisor). Only after that step is a proper remainder decomposed into partial fractions, which is the work of the next subsection.

Worked example 1: A quadratic over a linear factor

Coursebook, 7.1, division style. New fraction \(\dfrac{x^2+3x+1}{x+1}\).

Express \(\dfrac{x^2+3x+1}{x+1}\) in the form \(ax+b+\dfrac{c}{x+1}\).

  1. Dividing, \(x^2+3x+1=(x+1)(x+2)-1\). M1
  2. Hence \(\dfrac{x^2+3x+1}{x+1}=x+2-\dfrac{1}{x+1}\). A1

Answer: \(x+2-\dfrac{1}{x+1}\)

Worked example 2: Checking the remainder

Coursebook, 7.1, remainder style. New dividend \(x^2+5x+3\), divisor \(x+1\).

Find the remainder when \(x^2+5x+3\) is divided by \(x+1\).

  1. By the remainder theorem, the remainder is the value at \(x=-1\): \((-1)^2+5(-1)+3=-1\). B1

Answer: \(-1\)

Common mistakes

  • Starting a partial-fraction decomposition on an improper fraction without first dividing.
  • Losing the sign of the remainder when writing the proper part as a separate term.

Past paper

There is no whole past-paper question on this subsection alone.

Quick check 1

Express \(\dfrac{x^2+5x+2}{x+2}\) in the form \(ax+b+\dfrac{c}{x+2}\). Give the value of \(c\).

Worked solution
  1. \(x^2+5x+2=(x+2)(x+3)-4\), so \(c=-4\).

Answer: \(-4\)

Quick check 2

Find the remainder when \(x^2+7x+5\) is divided by \(x+2\).

Worked solution
  1. The remainder is \((-2)^2+7(-2)+5=-5\).

Answer: \(-5\)

Quick check 3

When \(\dfrac{x^2+5x+2}{x+2}\) is written as \(x+b+\dfrac{c}{x+2}\), what is the value of \(b\)?

Worked solution
  1. The quotient is \(x+3\), so \(b=3\).

Answer: \(3\)
