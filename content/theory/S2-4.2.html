4.2 Finding the median and other percentiles of a continuous random variable

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 2 Coursebook (Jayne Kranat), Chapter 4 Continuous random variables, pp.83–87 (Key point 4.4, Worked examples 4.3–4.5, Exercise 4B). Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: median, percentile, lower quartile, upper quartile, interquartile range

The median of a continuous random variable

The median \(m\) of a continuous random variable \(X\) is the value that splits the probability in half: the area under the PDF to the left of \(m\) is \(\dfrac12\), and so is the area to the right.

$$\mathrm{P}(X\lt m)=\int_{-\infty}^{m}\mathrm{f}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac12.$$

Because a single value has probability zero, \(\mathrm{P}(X\lt m)=\mathrm{P}(X\le m)=\dfrac12\), and also \(\mathrm{P}(X\gt m)=\dfrac12\). The median is not, in general, the midpoint of the interval on which \(\mathrm{f}\) is non-zero: it depends on the shape of the density.

Quartiles and other percentiles

The median is also the 50th percentile. Other percentiles are found in the same way, replacing \(\dfrac12\) by the required proportion. The \(r\)th percentile \(x_r\) satisfies

$$\int_{-\infty}^{x_r}\mathrm{f}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{r}{100}.$$

In particular, the lower quartile (25th percentile) \(q_1\) and the upper quartile (75th percentile) \(q_3\) satisfy \(\mathrm{P}(X\lt q_1)=\dfrac14\) and \(\mathrm{P}(X\lt q_3)=\dfrac34\). The interquartile range is \(\mathrm{IQR}=q_3-q_1\).

When the PDF is defined in pieces, first work out the area of each complete piece, so that you know which piece contains the required percentile; then integrate only as far as is needed inside that piece. If the equation for the percentile is a quadratic, keep only the root that lies in the support of the PDF.

Give answers exactly, or correct to 3 significant figures, unless a question asks for a different accuracy.

Worked example 1: Median and interquartile range

Book: Worked example 4.3, pp.84–85; Exercise 4B

The continuous random variable \(X\) has PDF \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{x}{8}\) for \(0\le x\le4\), and \(\mathrm{f}(x)=0\) otherwise. Find the median and the interquartile range of \(X\).

  1. Median: \(\displaystyle\int_0^{m}\dfrac{x}{8}\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{m^2}{16}=\dfrac12\), so \(m^2=8\) and \(m=2\sqrt{2}\) (since \(m\gt 0\)) M1 A1
  2. Lower quartile: \(\dfrac{q_1^2}{16}=\dfrac14\), so \(q_1^2=4\) and \(q_1=2\) M1 A1
  3. Upper quartile: \(\dfrac{q_3^2}{16}=\dfrac34\), so \(q_3^2=12\) and \(q_3=2\sqrt{3}\) A1
  4. \(\mathrm{IQR}=2\sqrt{3}-2\approx1.46\) (3 s.f.) A1

Answer: median \(2\sqrt{2}\approx2.83\); IQR \(2\sqrt{3}-2\approx1.46\)

Worked example 2: A quadratic equation for the median

Book: Worked example 4.4, p.85

The continuous random variable \(X\) has PDF \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{1}{8}(6-x)\) for \(2\le x\le6\), and \(\mathrm{f}(x)=0\) otherwise. (a) Show that the median \(m\) satisfies \(m^2-12m+28=0\). (b) Find the value of \(m\), correct to 3 significant figures.

  1. (a) \(\displaystyle\int_2^{m}\dfrac{6-x}{8}\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac12\) M1
  2. \(\dfrac{1}{8}\left[6x-\dfrac{x^2}{2}\right]_2^{m}=\dfrac12\), so \(\left(6m-\dfrac{m^2}{2}\right)-(12-2)=4\) A1
  3. \(6m-\dfrac{m^2}{2}-10=4\), hence \(m^2-12m+28=0\) A1
  4. (b) \(m=6\pm\sqrt{8}=6\pm2\sqrt{2}\). The root in \(2\le m\le6\) is \(6-2\sqrt{2}\approx3.17\) M1 A1

Answer: (a) shown (b) \(3.17\)

Worked example 3: A piecewise PDF

Book: Worked example 4.5, p.86

The continuous random variable \(X\) has PDF \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{x}{6}\) for \(0\le x\le2\), \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac13\) for \(2\le x\le4\), and \(\mathrm{f}(x)=0\) otherwise. Find the median of \(X\).

  1. Area on \(0\le x\le2\): \(\displaystyle\int_0^2\dfrac{x}{6}\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{4}{12}=\dfrac13\lt \dfrac12\), so the median lies in the second piece M1 A1
  2. \(\dfrac13+\displaystyle\int_2^{m}\dfrac13\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac12\), so \(\dfrac13(m-2)=\dfrac16\) M1
  3. \(m-2=\dfrac12\), hence \(m=\dfrac52=2.5\) A1

Answer: \(2.5\)

Quick checks

Quick check 1

The continuous random variable \(X\) has PDF \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{x}{8}\) for \(0\le x\le4\), and \(\mathrm{f}(x)=0\) otherwise. Find the median of \(X\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\displaystyle\int_0^{m}\dfrac{x}{8}\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{m^2}{16}=\dfrac12\), so \(m^2=8\)
  2. \(m=2\sqrt{2}\approx2.83\) (3 s.f.)

Answer: \(2\sqrt{2}\approx2.83\)

Quick check 2

The continuous random variable \(X\) has PDF \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{3x^2}{8}\) for \(0\le x\le2\), and \(\mathrm{f}(x)=0\) otherwise. Find the median of \(X\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\displaystyle\int_0^{m}\dfrac{3x^2}{8}\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{m^3}{8}=\dfrac12\), so \(m^3=4\)
  2. \(m=4^{1/3}\approx1.59\) (3 s.f.)

Answer: \(4^{1/3}\approx1.59\)

Quick check 3

The continuous random variable \(X\) has PDF \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{x}{8}\) for \(0\le x\le4\), and \(\mathrm{f}(x)=0\) otherwise. Find the lower quartile of \(X\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{q_1^2}{16}=\dfrac14\), so \(q_1^2=4\)
  2. \(q_1=2\)

Answer: \(2\)

Quick check 4

The continuous random variable \(X\) has PDF \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{x}{6}\) for \(0\le x\le2\), \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac13\) for \(2\le x\le4\), and \(\mathrm{f}(x)=0\) otherwise. Find the median of \(X\).

Worked solution
  1. Area on \(0\le x\le2\) is \(\dfrac13\lt \dfrac12\), so the median is in the second piece
  2. \(\dfrac13+\dfrac13(m-2)=\dfrac12\), so \(m-2=\dfrac12\) and \(m=2.5\)

Answer: \(2.5\)
