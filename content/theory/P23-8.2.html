8.2 Integration of \(\dfrac{1}{a^2+x^2}\)

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 8 Further calculus, printed pp.187–188. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook result for integrating one over a squared plus x squared.

Key words: inverse tangent

The standard result

From 8.1, \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{x}{a}\right)=\dfrac{a}{a^2+x^2}\). Dividing by \(a\) gives

\[\int\frac{1}{a^2+x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{a}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)+c.\]

This result is given in the formula booklet. When the coefficient of \(x^2\) is not 1, take it out first: \(\dfrac{1}{4+9x^2}=\dfrac{1}{9}\times\dfrac{1}{\frac{4}{9}+x^2}\), so \(a=\dfrac{2}{3}\).

A fraction with a quadratic denominator that does not factorise, such as \(\dfrac{x^4+16}{x^2+4}\), may need division first, and partial fractions can produce a term of this type, which integrates to an inverse tangent.

Worked example 1: An indefinite integral

Coursebook, 8.2, the standard inverse tangent integral. New numbers.

Find \(\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{9+x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x\).

  1. Here \(a=3\). B1
  2. \(\dfrac{1}{3}\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{x}{3}\right)+c\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{1}{3}\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{x}{3}\right)+c\)

Worked example 2: A coefficient in front of x squared

Coursebook, 8.2, adapting the standard result. New numbers.

Find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2/3}\frac{1}{4+9x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x\).

  1. \(\dfrac{1}{9}\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{\frac{4}{9}+x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x\), so \(a=\dfrac{2}{3}\). M1
  2. \(\dfrac{1}{9}\times\dfrac{3}{2}\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{3x}{2}\right)=\dfrac{1}{6}\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{3x}{2}\right)\) A1
  3. \(\dfrac{1}{6}\bigl(\tan^{-1}1-\tan^{-1}0\bigr)=\dfrac{\pi}{24}\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{\pi}{24}\)

Common mistakes

  • Writing \(\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{x}{a}\right)\) without the factor \(\dfrac{1}{a}\).
  • Forgetting to take out the coefficient of \(x^2\) before using the result.
  • Giving a decimal when the question asks for an exact value in terms of \(\pi\).

(a) Find the quotient and remainder when \(x^4+16\) is divided by \(x^2+4\). [3]

(b) Hence show that \(\displaystyle\int_2^{2\sqrt{3}}\frac{x^4+16}{x^2+4}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{4}{3}(\pi+4)\). [5]

Mark scheme
  1. (a) The quotient is of the form \(x^2+k\). M1 Official MS: M1, quotient x² + k. Alternative: identity with B = 0, C = −4, D = 32.
  2. (a) Quotient \(x^2-4\) A1 Official MS: A1, x² − 4.
  3. (a) Remainder \(32\), so \(\dfrac{x^4+16}{x^2+4}=x^2-4+\dfrac{32}{x^2+4}\) A1 Official MS: A1, 32.
  4. (b) \(\displaystyle\int(x^2-4)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{3}x^3-4x\) B1 Official MS: B1 FT, ⅓x³ − 4x.
  5. (b) \(\displaystyle\int\frac{32}{x^2+4}\,\mathrm{d}x\) is of the form \(p\tan^{-1}(qx)\) M1 Official MS: M1, p tan⁻¹(qx) with q = 2 or ½.
  6. (b) \(=16\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}x\right)\) A1 Official MS: A1 FT, 16 tan⁻¹(½x).
  7. (b) Limits: \(\left(8\sqrt{3}-8\sqrt{3}+16\times\frac{\pi}{3}\right)-\left(\frac{8}{3}-8+16\times\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\) M1 Official MS: M1, use limits correctly in an expression with p tan⁻¹(qx) and rx³ + sx.
  8. (b) \(=\frac{16\pi}{3}-4\pi+\frac{16}{3}=\frac{4}{3}\pi+\frac{16}{3}=\frac{4}{3}(\pi+4)\) A1 Official MS: A1 AG, from full and correct working.

Quick check 1

Show that \(\displaystyle\int_0^2\frac{1}{4+x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x=k\pi\) and find the value of \(k\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\)
  2. \(\dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{\pi}{4}=\dfrac{\pi}{8}\)

Answer: \(k=\dfrac{1}{8}\)

Quick check 2

Find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^3\frac{6}{9+x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x\) in the form \(k\pi\), and give the value of \(k\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{6}{3}\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{x}{3}\right)=2\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{x}{3}\right)\)
  2. \(2\times\dfrac{\pi}{4}=\dfrac{\pi}{2}\)

Answer: \(k=\dfrac{1}{2}\)

Quick check 3

Let \(m=3\) and \(n=2\). Find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^{n/m}\frac{1}{4+9x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x\) in the form \(k\pi\), and give the value of \(k\).

Worked solution
  1. As in Worked example 2: \(\dfrac{1}{6}\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{3x}{2}\right)\)
  2. \(\dfrac{1}{6}\times\dfrac{\pi}{4}\)

Answer: \(k=\dfrac{1}{24}\)
