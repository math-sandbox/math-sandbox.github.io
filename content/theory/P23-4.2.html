4.2 The quotient rule

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton), Chapter 4 Differentiation, pp.84–86. Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: quotient rule, quotient, numerator, denominator, gradient

When to use it

A function such as \(y=\dfrac{x^2-5}{2x+1}\) is a quotient \(\dfrac{u}{v}\) with \(u=x^2-5\) and \(v=2x+1\). You could write it as \((x^2-5)(2x+1)^{-1}\) and use the product rule, but the quotient rule is usually quicker.

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)=\frac{v\dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}-u\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}}{v^2}$$

In words: (denominator \(\times\) derivative of numerator \(-\) numerator \(\times\) derivative of denominator) \(\div\) denominator squared. The order matters because of the minus sign.

Why it works

From \(y+\delta y=\dfrac{u+\delta u}{v+\delta v}\) we get \(\delta y=\dfrac{v\,\delta u-u\,\delta v}{v(v+\delta v)}\). Divide by \(\delta x\) and let \(\delta x\to0\); \(v(v+\delta v)\to v^2\).

Tips

Expand and simplify only the numerator; leave the denominator as \(v^2\). A gradient of zero needs only the numerator to be zero (with \(v\neq0\)).

Worked example 1: Quotient rule and a gradient

Book: Worked example 4.3, p.85; Exercise 4B p.87

Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) when \(y=\dfrac{x^2+3}{2x-1}\), and find the gradient of the curve at \(x=2\).

  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{(2x-1)(2x)-(x^2+3)(2)}{(2x-1)^2}\) M1
  2. \(=\dfrac{2x^2-2x-6}{(2x-1)^2}\) A1
  3. At \(x=2\): \(\dfrac{8-4-6}{3^2}=-\dfrac{2}{9}\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{2x^2-2x-6}{(2x-1)^2}\); gradient \(-\frac29\)

Worked example 2: Stationary points of a quotient

Book: Worked example 4.4, p.86; Exercise 4B p.87

Find the coordinates of the stationary points of \(y=\dfrac{x^2}{x-3}\).

  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{(x-3)(2x)-x^2(1)}{(x-3)^2}=\dfrac{x^2-6x}{(x-3)^2}\) M1 A1
  2. \(x(x-6)=0\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=6\) M1
  3. \(y=\dfrac{0}{-3}=0\) and \(y=\dfrac{36}{3}=12\) A1

Answer: \((0,0)\) and \((6,12)\)

Quick checks

Quick check 1

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=\dfrac{3x+1}{x-2}\) at the point where \(x=3\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{3(x-2)-(3x+1)(1)}{(x-2)^2}=-\dfrac{7}{(x-2)^2}\)
  2. At \(x=3\): \(-\dfrac{7}{1^2}\)

Answer: −7

Quick check 2

The curve \(y=\dfrac{x^2}{2x-1}\) has a stationary point where \(x\neq0\). Find (a) its \(x\)-coordinate, (b) its \(y\)-coordinate.

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{(2x-1)(2x)-x^2(2)}{(2x-1)^2}=\dfrac{x(2x-2)}{(2x-1)^2}\)
  2. \(x(2x-2)=0\), \(x\neq0\Rightarrow x=1\)
  3. \(y=\dfrac{1}{2-1}=1\)

Answer: (a) \(x=1\) (b) \(y=1\)

Quick check 3

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=\dfrac{x^2+5}{x+2}\) at the point where \(x=0\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{(x+2)(2x)-(x^2+5)(1)}{(x+2)^2}=\dfrac{x^2+4x-5}{(x+2)^2}\)
  2. At \(x=0\): \(-\dfrac{5}{4}\)

Answer: −1.25

Marks: M = method, A = accurate answer (depends on M), B = independent mark. Give exact answers unless the question asks for a decimal; differentiate first, substitute second.
