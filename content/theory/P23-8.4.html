8.4 Integration by substitution

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 8 Further calculus, printed pp.191–193. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook method of changing the variable, including changing the limits.

Key words: integration by substitution, definite integral

The method

Integration by substitution reverses the chain rule. To find \(\displaystyle\int\mathrm{f}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\) using \(u=\mathrm{g}(x)\):

  1. Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}\) and write \(\mathrm{d}x\) in terms of \(\mathrm{d}u\).
  2. Write the whole integrand in terms of \(u\). No \(x\) may be left.
  3. For a definite integral, change the limits to values of \(u\).
  4. Integrate with respect to \(u\). For an indefinite integral, write the answer in terms of \(x\) again.

In the examination the substitution is usually given. A good choice is often the inside of a bracket, a square root or a power, or a function whose derivative also appears, such as \(u=\ln x\) with \(\dfrac{1}{x}\).

Worked example 1: An indefinite integral

Coursebook, 8.4, substitution. New numbers.

Use the substitution \(u=x^2+1\) to find \(\displaystyle\int x(x^2+1)^3\,\mathrm{d}x\).

  1. \(\mathrm{d}u=2x\,\mathrm{d}x\), so \(x\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}u\). B1
  2. \(\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{2}u^3\,\mathrm{d}u\) M1
  3. \(=\frac{1}{8}u^4\) A1
  4. \(=\frac{1}{8}(x^2+1)^4+c\) A1

Answer: \(\frac{1}{8}(x^2+1)^4+c\)

Worked example 2: Changing the limits

Coursebook, 8.4, definite integrals by substitution. New numbers.

Use the substitution \(u=x^2+16\) to find \(\displaystyle\int_0^3x\sqrt{x^2+16}\,\mathrm{d}x\).

  1. \(x\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}u\); when \(x=0\), \(u=16\), and when \(x=3\), \(u=25\). B1
  2. \(\displaystyle\int_{16}^{25}\frac{1}{2}u^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}u\) M1
  3. \(=\Bigl[\frac{1}{3}u^{3/2}\Bigr]_{16}^{25}\) A1
  4. \(=\frac{1}{3}(125-64)=\frac{61}{3}\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{61}{3}\)

Common mistakes

  • Leaving some \(x\) terms in the integral after substituting.
  • Using the old \(x\) limits with the new \(u\) integral.
  • Forgetting the factor from \(\mathrm{d}x\), such as the \(\frac{1}{2}\) in \(x\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}u\).

Quick check 1

Use the substitution \(u=x^2+1\) to find the value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^12x(x^2+1)^3\,\mathrm{d}x\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\displaystyle\int_1^2u^3\,\mathrm{d}u\)
  2. \(\dfrac{16-1}{4}\)

Answer: \(3.75\)

Quick check 2

Use the substitution \(u=x^2+9\) to find the value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^4x\sqrt{x^2+9}\,\mathrm{d}x\), correct to 3 significant figures.

Worked solution
  1. \(\displaystyle\int_9^{25}\frac{1}{2}u^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}u\)
  2. \(\dfrac{1}{3}(125-27)=\dfrac{98}{3}\)

Answer: \(32.7\)

Quick check 3

Use the substitution \(u=\ln x\) to find the value of \(\displaystyle\int_1^4\frac{\ln x}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\), correct to 3 significant figures.

Worked solution
  1. \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\ln4}u\,\mathrm{d}u\)
  2. \(\dfrac{1}{2}(\ln4)^2\)

Answer: \(0.961\)
