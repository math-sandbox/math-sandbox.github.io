What this review is for

Chapter 4 collected several techniques for differentiating. A review question may use more than one of them in the same item: for example a product that includes a logarithm, or a parametric curve whose components are trigonometric. The methods themselves are not new; the skill is to choose the right rule, differentiate carefully, and only then substitute a value or form a tangent.

  • Product rule (4.1): if \(y=uv\) then \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=u\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}+v\dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}\).
  • Quotient rule (4.2): if \(y=\dfrac{u}{v}\) then \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{v\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}-u\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}}{v^2}\).
  • Standard derivatives (4.3–4.5): \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(\mathrm{e}^{kx})=k\mathrm{e}^{kx}\), \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigl(\ln(ax+b)\bigr)=\dfrac{a}{ax+b}\), and the trigonometric rules (radians), including the chain rule for a linear argument.
  • Implicit differentiation (4.6): differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\), treating \(y\) as a function of \(x\), then collect terms in \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\).
  • Parametric differentiation (4.7): \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y/\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}t}\), provided \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}\neq 0\).

Worked example 1: A product with a logarithm

Mixed practice in the style of the chapter review (product rule with Section 4.4)

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=(x+2)\ln(x+1)\) at the point where \(x=0\).

  1. Product rule: \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=1\cdot\ln(x+1)+(x+2)\cdot\dfrac{1}{x+1}\) M1 A1
  2. At \(x=0\): \(\ln 1+2\cdot\dfrac{1}{1}=0+2=2\) A1

Answer: \(2\)

Worked example 2: Trigonometric derivatives at the origin

Mixed practice in the style of the chapter review (Section 4.5)

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=4\sin x+\tan 2x\) at the point where \(x=0\).

  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=4\cos x+2\sec^2 2x\) M1 A1
  2. At \(x=0\): \(4\cos 0+2\sec^2 0=4\cdot 1+2\cdot 1=6\) A1

Answer: \(6\)

Worked example 3: A parametric gradient

Mixed practice in the style of the chapter review (Section 4.7)

A curve has parametric equations \(x=t^2\), \(y=4t^3\), for \(t\gt 0\). Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) at the point where \(t=1\).

  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}=2t\) and \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}=12t^2\), so \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{12t^2}{2t}=6t\) (for \(t\neq 0\)). M1 A1
  2. At \(t=1\), the gradient is \(6\). A1

Answer: \(6\)

Common mistakes

  • Substituting the given value before differentiating.
  • Using the product rule as if it were the quotient rule, or forgetting the chain-rule factor on \(\tan 2x\) or \(\ln(ax+b)\).
  • For parametric equations, computing \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}y}\) by mistake, or cancelling a factor of \(t\) that would exclude the required point.
  • In an implicit question, forgetting to apply the product rule to a term such as \(xy\).

Quick checks

Quick check 1

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=5\sin x+\tan 3x\) at the point where \(x=0\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=5\cos x+3\sec^2 3x\); at \(x=0\) this is \(5+3=8\)

Answer: \(8\)

Quick check 2

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=\ln(2x+1)\) at the point where \(x=0\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{2}{2x+1}\); at \(x=0\) this is \(2\)

Answer: \(2\)

Quick check 3

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=\dfrac{x-1}{x+3}\) at the point where \(x=0\).

Worked solution
  1. Quotient rule: \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{(x+3)-(x-1)}{(x+3)^2}=\dfrac{4}{(x+3)^2}\)
  2. At \(x=0\) the gradient is \(\dfrac{4}{9}\)

Answer: \(\dfrac{4}{9}\approx0.444\)

Quick check 4

A curve is given parametrically by \(x=t^2\), \(y=t^3+t\), for \(t\neq 0\). Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) in terms of \(t\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{3t^2+1}{2t}\)

Answer: \(\dfrac{3t^2+1}{2t}\)
