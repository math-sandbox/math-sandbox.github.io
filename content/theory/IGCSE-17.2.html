17.2 Borrowing and investing money

Based on Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics Core & Extended Coursebook (Morrison & Hamshaw), Chapter 17 Managing money, pp.558–570. Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: interest, simple interest, principal, interest rate, hire purchase, deposit, instalment, compound interest, exponential growth, exponential decay, depreciation

Simple interest

Simple interest is the same percentage of the original amount (the principal) every year.

$$I=\frac{PRT}{100}$$

\(P\) = principal, \(R\) = interest rate (% per year), \(T\) = time in years. Rearranged: \(P=\dfrac{100I}{RT}\), \(R=\dfrac{100I}{PT}\), \(T=\dfrac{100I}{PR}\). Amount \(=P+I\). Months: divide by 12.

Hire purchase (HP)

Pay a deposit, then equal instalments. Total HP price \(=\) deposit \(+\) all instalments; extra cost \(=\) HP price \(-\) cash price. A flat rate of interest is simple interest on the outstanding balance.

Compound interest

With compound interest, interest is added each period and then earns interest itself.

$$V=P\left(1+\frac{r}{100}\right)^n$$

Multiplier: increase \(r\%\) \(\to\times\left(1+\frac{r}{100}\right)\). To find \(P\): divide \(V\) by the multiplier\(^n\). To find \(n\): try whole numbers of years until the value passes the target. To find \(r\): \(1+\frac{r}{100}=\sqrt[n]{V/P}\).

Exponential growth and decay

Compound interest is an example of exponential growth.

$$\text{growth: } y=a(1+r)^n$$$$\text{decay: } y=a(1-r)^n$$

\(a\) = starting value, \(r\) = rate as a decimal, \(n\) = number of periods. Depreciation of cars and machines is decay.

Worked example 1: Simple interest and finding the time

Book: Worked examples 5–8, pp.559–560; Exercise 17.4 p.561–562

(a) Find the simple interest on \$2400 at 3.5% per year for 4 years. (b) How long does it take \$1800 to grow to \$2124 at 4% per year simple interest?

  1. (a) \(I=\dfrac{2400\times3.5\times4}{100}\) M1
  2. \(=\$336.00\) A1
  3. (b) \(I=2124-1800=\$324\) B1
  4. \(T=\dfrac{100\times324}{1800\times4}\) M1
  5. \(=4.5\) years A1

Answer: (a) \$336.00 (b) 4.5 years

Worked example 2: Hire purchase

Book: Worked examples 9–10, pp.562–563; Exercise 17.5 p.563

A motorbike has a cash price of \$1250. On HP you pay a 15% deposit and 18 monthly instalments of \$65. How much more than the cash price is the HP price?

  1. Deposit \(=0.15\times1250=\$187.50\) B1
  2. Instalments \(=18\times65=\$1170\) M1
  3. HP price \(=187.50+1170=\$1357.50\) A1
  4. Extra \(=1357.50-1250=\$107.50\) A1

Answer: \$107.50

Worked example 3: Compound interest: value, principal and years

Book: Worked examples 11–15, pp.564–569; Exercises 17.6 p.567 and 17.7 p.569–570

(a) \$6000 is invested at 2.8% per year compound interest. Find its value after 5 years. (b) What amount invested at 4% per year compound interest is worth \$5000 after 3 years? (c) A car worth \$24 000 loses 15% of its value each year. After how many whole years is it first worth less than \$10 000?

  1. (a) \(V=6000\times1.028^5\) M1
  2. \(=6888.375663\ldots\) A1 → \$6888.38
  3. (b) \(5000=P\times1.04^3\Rightarrow P=\dfrac{5000}{1.04^3}\) M1
  4. \(=4444.981793\ldots\) A1 → \$4444.98
  5. (c) Decay multiplier \(1-0.15=0.85\): value \(=24\,000\times0.85^n\) M1
  6. \(n=5\): \(24\,000\times0.85^5=10648.9275\ldots\) (still above); \(n=6\): \(24\,000\times0.85^6=9051.5884\ldots\) M1
  7. first below \$10 000 after 6 years A1

Answer: (a) \$6888.38 (b) \$4444.98 (c) 6 years
