17.1 Earning money

Based on Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics Core & Extended Coursebook (Morrison & Hamshaw), Chapter 17 Managing money, pp.553–557. Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: wages, salary, piece work, commission, retainer, overtime, gross income, deductions, net income, income tax

Ways of earning money

  • Wages: paid for each hour worked, usually weekly. Hours above the normal number are overtime, often paid at time-and-a-half (\(1.5\times\) the hourly rate) or double-time (\(2\times\)).
  • Salary: a fixed amount per year, paid monthly (\(\div 12\)) or weekly (\(\div 52\)).
  • Piece work: paid for each item made: earnings \(=\) number of items \(\times\) rate per item.
  • Commission: a percentage of the value of sales, sometimes on top of a fixed retainer.

Deductions and net income

Amounts taken off before you are paid (income tax, pension, insurance, union fees) are deductions.

$$\text{net income} = \text{gross income} - \text{deductions}$$

Tax tables

Income tax is often charged in brackets: a fixed amount for the lower bands plus a percentage of the income above the start of your band. Find the band first, then subtract its starting value.

Worked example 1: Wages with overtime

Book: Worked example 3, p.554; Exercise 17.1 p.554–555

Lina earns \$14.40 per hour for a normal 38-hour week. Overtime is paid at time-and-a-half on Saturdays and double-time on Sundays. One week she works 38 normal hours, 6 hours on Saturday and 2.5 hours on Sunday. Calculate her total pay.

  1. Normal pay \(=38\times14.40=\$547.20\) B1
  2. Saturday \(=1.5\times14.40\times6=\$129.60\) M1
  3. Sunday \(=2\times14.40\times2.5=\$72.00\) M1
  4. Total \(=547.20+129.60+72.00\) M1
  5. \(=\$748.80\) A1

Answer: \$748.80

Worked example 2: Retainer plus commission

Book: Worked example 2, p.553; Exercise 17.1 Q6 p.554

Omar is paid a retainer of \$350 per week plus 3.5% commission on all sales. One week he sells 5 laptops at \$1240 each. Calculate his earnings for the week.

  1. Sales \(=5\times1240=\$6200\) B1
  2. Commission \(=0.035\times6200=\$217.00\) M1
  3. Earnings \(=350+217.00=\$567.00\) A1

Answer: \$567.00

Worked example 3: Income tax from a table

Book: Worked example 4 and Information from tax tables, p.557

Taxable incomeTax
\$0–\$12 0000%
\$12 001–\$40 00015% of the amount over \$12 000
\$40 001 and above\$4200 plus 30% of the amount over \$40 000

Ana has a taxable income of \$52 600 per year. Find (a) her tax per year, (b) her tax per month.

  1. \$52 600 is in the top band; amount over \$40 000 \(=52\,600-40\,000=\$12\,600\) M1
  2. \(30\%\) of \(12\,600=\$3780\) M1
  3. (a) Tax \(=4200+3780=\$7980.00\) A1
  4. (b) \(7980\div12\) M1
  5. \(=\$665.00\) A1

Answer: (a) \$7980.00 (b) \$665.00 per month

Quick checks

Quick check 1

A worker is paid \$0.85 for each box packed. She packs 640 boxes in a week. Calculate her earnings.

Worked solution
  1. \(640\times0.85=544\)

Answer: \$544.00

Quick check 2

Kai has gross monthly income \$3250. Deductions are income tax \$412.50, pension \$162.50 and insurance \$48.75. Find (a) his net income, (b) net income as a percentage of gross income.

Worked solution
  1. Deductions \(=412.50+162.50+48.75\)
  2. \(=\$623.75\)
  3. (a) Net \(=3250-623.75=\$2626.25\)
  4. (b) \(\dfrac{2626.25}{3250}\times100=80.8077\ldots\%\)

Answer: (a) \$2626.25 (b) 80.8%

Quick check 3

A salary of \$46 800 per year is paid in 52 equal weekly amounts. How much is paid each week?

Worked solution
  1. \(46\,800\div52=900\)

Answer: \$900.00

Quick check 4

Use the tax table in Worked example 3 to find the tax on a taxable income of \$31 000.

Worked solution
  1. \$31 000 is in the 15% band
  2. Amount over \$12 000 \(=19\,000\)
  3. \(0.15\times19\,000=2850\)

Answer: \$2850.00
