4.5 Derivatives of trigonometric functions

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, Julianne Hughes), Chapter 4 Differentiation, pp.95–98 (Key points 4.7–4.9, Worked examples 4.9–4.10, Exercise 4E). Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: chain rule, product rule, quotient rule, radian

The derivatives of \(\sin x\), \(\cos x\) and \(\tan x\)

Throughout this section, and in all calculus work unless a question says otherwise, the angle is measured in radians. From the definition of the derivative, and the expansions of \(\sin(x+\delta x)\) and \(\cos(x+\delta x)\), it follows that

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(\sin x)=\cos x,\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(\cos x)=-\sin x.$$

The graph of the gradient function of \(y=\sin x\) is the same shape as \(y=\cos x\): the gradient of sine is \(1\) at the origin, zero at its peaks, and negative as the curve falls.

The derivative of \(\tan x\) follows from the quotient rule, writing \(\tan x=\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}\):

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(\tan x)=\frac{\cos x\cdot\cos x-\sin x\cdot(-\sin x)}{\cos^2 x}=\frac{\cos^2 x+\sin^2 x}{\cos^2 x}=\sec^2 x.$$

Linear arguments

By the chain rule, with \(u=ax+b\) and \(a\neq0\),

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigl[\sin(ax+b)\bigr]=a\cos(ax+b),\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigl[\cos(ax+b)\bigr]=-a\sin(ax+b),$$ $$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigl[\tan(ax+b)\bigr]=a\sec^2(ax+b).$$

Combine these with the product rule and the quotient rule when a trigonometric factor is multiplied or divided by another function of \(x\). A power such as \((3+2\sin x)^4\) is also a chain-rule composite.

Worked example 1: Standard rules and a product

Book: Worked example 4.9, pp.96–97; Exercise 4E

Differentiate with respect to \(x\): (a) \(5\sin x\) (b) \(x\sin x\) (c) \(\dfrac{\cos x}{x}\).

  1. (a) \(5\cos x\) B1
  2. (b) Product rule: \(\sin x+x\cos x\) M1 A1
  3. (c) Quotient rule: \(\dfrac{x(-\sin x)-\cos x\cdot 1}{x^2}=\dfrac{-x\sin x-\cos x}{x^2}\) M1 A1

Answer: (a) \(5\cos x\) (b) \(\sin x+x\cos x\) (c) \(\dfrac{-x\sin x-\cos x}{x^2}\)

Worked example 2: A power and a linear argument

Book: Worked example 4.10, pp.97–98

Differentiate with respect to \(x\): (a) \(4\sin 3x\) (b) \((2-3\cos x)^5\) (c) \(\tan\left(2x-\dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)\).

  1. (a) \(4\times 3\cos 3x=12\cos 3x\) B1
  2. (b) Chain rule: \(5(2-3\cos x)^4\times 3\sin x=15\sin x(2-3\cos x)^4\) M1 A1
  3. (c) \(2\sec^2\left(2x-\dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)\) B1

Answer: (a) \(12\cos 3x\) (b) \(15\sin x(2-3\cos x)^4\) (c) \(2\sec^2\left(2x-\dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)\)

Worked example 3: A gradient at a given point

Book: Key point 4.9, p.97

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=3\cos 2x+4\sin x\) at the point where \(x=0\).

  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=-6\sin 2x+4\cos x\) M1 A1
  2. At \(x=0\): \(-6\sin 0+4\cos 0=4\) A1

Answer: \(4\)

Quick checks

Quick check 1

Differentiate \(7\cos 2x\) with respect to \(x\).

Worked solution
  1. \(7\times\bigl(-2\sin 2x\bigr)=-14\sin 2x\)

Answer: \(-14\sin 2x\)

Quick check 2

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=5\sin 3x\) at the point where \(x=0\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=15\cos 3x\)
  2. At \(x=0\): \(15\cos 0=15\)

Answer: \(15\)

Quick check 3

Find the gradient of the curve \(y=2\tan x\) at the point where \(x=0\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=2\sec^2 x\)
  2. At \(x=0\): \(2\sec^2 0=2\cdot 1=2\)

Answer: \(2\)

Quick check 4

Differentiate \((1+2\sin x)^3\) with respect to \(x\).

Worked solution
  1. \(3(1+2\sin x)^2\times 2\cos x=6\cos x(1+2\sin x)^2\)

Answer: \(6\cos x(1+2\sin x)^2\)

Marks: M = method, A = accurate answer (depends on M), B = independent mark. Marks on worked examples are illustrative, not official. Give exact answers unless the question asks for a decimal. In calculus, angles are in radians unless a question says otherwise. Differentiate first, substitute second.
