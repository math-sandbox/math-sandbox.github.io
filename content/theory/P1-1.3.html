1.3 The quadratic formula

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton), Chapter 1 Quadratics, printed pp.10–11 (Key point 1.2, the proof by completing the square, and Worked example 1.8). Printed pages were read from P1.pdf (PDF pages 22–23). Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: quadratic formula

The formula

When a quadratic equation does not factorise easily over the integers, it can still be solved using the quadratic formula. If \(ax^2+bx+c=0\), where \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) are constants and \(a\neq 0\), then

\[ x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}. \]

This is Key point 1.2 in the book. The formula itself can be proved by completing the square for \(ax^2+bx+c=0\). The quantity \(b^2-4ac\) under the square-root must be positive if there are two distinct real roots, and you should leave answers either in exact form or to the degree of accuracy asked for in the question.

Worked example 1: Two real roots, to 3 significant figures

Book: Worked example 1.8, printed p.11. New coefficients. The book exercise often asks for 2 decimal places; answers here are given to 3 significant figures, which is an acceptance mode the practice engine supports.

Solve \(3x^2+5x-1=0\), giving your answers correct to 3 significant figures.

  1. Here \(a=3\), \(b=5\) and \(c=-1\), so \(x=\dfrac{-5\pm\sqrt{5^2-4\times 3\times(-1)}}{2\times 3}=\dfrac{-5\pm\sqrt{37}}{6}\) M1
  2. \(\sqrt{37}=6.08276\ldots\), so the roots are \(\dfrac{-5+6.08276\ldots}{6}=0.18046\ldots\) and \(\dfrac{-5-6.08276\ldots}{6}=-1.84712\ldots\) M1
  3. To 3 significant figures, \(x=0.180\) or \(x=-1.85\) A1 A1

Answer: \(x=0.180\) or \(x=-1.85\) (3 s.f.)

Worked example 2: A negative linear coefficient

Book: Worked example 1.8 style, printed p.11. New equation.

Solve \(2x^2-7x-3=0\), giving your answers correct to 3 significant figures.

  1. Here \(a=2\), \(b=-7\) and \(c=-3\), so \(x=\dfrac{-(-7)\pm\sqrt{(-7)^2-4\times 2\times(-3)}}{4}=\dfrac{7\pm\sqrt{73}}{4}\) M1
  2. \(\sqrt{73}=8.54400\ldots\), so the roots are \(\dfrac{7+8.54400\ldots}{4}=3.88600\ldots\) and \(\dfrac{7-8.54400\ldots}{4}=-0.38600\ldots\) M1
  3. To 3 significant figures, \(x=3.89\) or \(x=-0.386\) A1 A1

Answer: \(x=3.89\) or \(x=-0.386\) (3 s.f.)

Common mistakes

  • Using \(b\) rather than \(-b\) in the numerator, especially when \(b\) is already negative.
  • Computing \(b^2-4ac\) with the wrong sign of \(c\).
  • Forgetting the \(\pm\), and so finding only one root.

Quick checks

Quick check 1

For the equation \(2x^2+5x-3=0\), find the value of \(b^2-4ac\).

Worked solution
  1. \(b^2-4ac=5^2-4\times 2\times(-3)=25+24=49\) A1

Answer: \(49\)

Quick check 2

The equation \(5x^2+7x-2=0\) is solved using the quadratic formula. Write down the value of \(2a\) used in the formula.

Worked solution
  1. \(a=5\), so \(2a=10\) A1

Answer: \(10\)

Quick check 3

Solve \(x^2-6x+5=0\) using the quadratic formula. Write down the smaller root.

Worked solution
  1. \(x=\dfrac{-(-6)\pm\sqrt{(-6)^2-4\times 1\times 5}}{2}=\dfrac{6\pm\sqrt{16}}{2}=\dfrac{6\pm 4}{2}\) M1
  2. The roots are \(5\) and \(1\). The smaller root is \(1\) A1

Answer: \(1\)

Marks: M = method, A = accurate answer (depends on M), B = independent mark. Identify \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) carefully, keep the \(\pm\), and give decimal roots to the accuracy asked for in the question.
