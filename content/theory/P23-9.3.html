9.3 The scalar product

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 9 Vectors, printed pp.225–230. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook definition of the scalar product and its use for angles and perpendicular vectors.

Key words: scalar product, perpendicular vectors, angle between two vectors

Definition

The scalar product (dot product) of \(\mathbf{a}=a_1\mathbf{i}+a_2\mathbf{j}+a_3\mathbf{k}\) and \(\mathbf{b}=b_1\mathbf{i}+b_2\mathbf{j}+b_3\mathbf{k}\) is the number

\[\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}=a_1b_1+a_2b_2+a_3b_3=\lvert \mathbf{a}\rvert \lvert \mathbf{b}\rvert \cos\theta,\]

where \(\theta\) is the angle between the two vectors when they both point away from the same point.

Using the scalar product

  • Angle between two vectors: \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{\lvert \mathbf{a}\rvert \lvert \mathbf{b}\rvert }\).
  • Perpendicular vectors: \(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}=0\) (for non-zero vectors).
  • If \(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}\lt 0\), the angle is obtuse.

For an angle such as \(OAB\) in a triangle, use the two vectors that start at \(A\): \(\overrightarrow{AO}\) and \(\overrightarrow{AB}\).

Worked example 1: Angle between two vectors

Coursebook, 9.3, angle between vectors. New numbers.

Find the angle between \(\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}+2\mathbf{k}\) and \(\mathbf{b}=3\mathbf{i}-4\mathbf{k}\).

  1. \(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}=3+0-8=-5\) M1
  2. \(\lvert \mathbf{a}\rvert =3\), \(\lvert \mathbf{b}\rvert =5\), so \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{-5}{15}\) M1
  3. \(\theta=109.5^\circ\) A1

Answer: \(109.5^\circ\)

Worked example 2: Perpendicular vectors

Coursebook, 9.3, perpendicular vectors. New numbers.

Find \(k\) so that \(2\mathbf{i}+k\mathbf{j}-\mathbf{k}\) is perpendicular to \(\mathbf{i}-3\mathbf{j}+4\mathbf{k}\).

  1. \(2-3k-4=0\) M1
  2. \(k=-\dfrac{2}{3}\) A1

Answer: \(k=-\dfrac{2}{3}\)

Common mistakes

  • Using \(\overrightarrow{OA}\) and \(\overrightarrow{OB}\) for angle \(OAB\). The angle at \(A\) needs vectors starting at \(A\).
  • Getting the acute angle instead of the obtuse one: keep the sign of the scalar product.
  • Writing the scalar product as a vector. It is a number.

Quick check 1

Find the scalar product of \(\begin{pmatrix}3\\-1\\2\end{pmatrix}\) and \(\begin{pmatrix}1\\4\\5\end{pmatrix}\).

Worked solution
  1. \(3-4+10\)

Answer: \(9\)

Quick check 2

Find the cosine of the angle between \(\begin{pmatrix}2\\1\\-2\end{pmatrix}\) and \(\begin{pmatrix}4\\4\\2\end{pmatrix}\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}=8+4-4=8\)
  2. \(\lvert \mathbf{a}\rvert \lvert \mathbf{b}\rvert =3\times6=18\)

Answer: \(\dfrac{8}{18}=0.444\) (3 s.f.)

Quick check 3

The vectors \(\begin{pmatrix}2\\p\\1\end{pmatrix}\) and \(\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\-8\end{pmatrix}\) are perpendicular. Find \(p\).

Worked solution
  1. \(2+2p-8=0\)

Answer: \(p=3\)
