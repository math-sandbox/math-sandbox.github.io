8.4 Rates of change

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 8 Further differentiation, printed pp.227–229. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook use of the chain rule to connect the rates of change of two variables with time.

Key words: rate of change, chain rule

Rates with respect to time

When a quantity changes with time \(t\), its rate of change is its derivative with respect to \(t\). For example, if \(x\) increases at 3 units per second, then \(\dfrac{dx}{dt}=3\); a negative value means the quantity is decreasing.

Connecting two rates

If \(y\) is a function of \(x\) and \(x\) changes with time, the chain rule connects the rates:

\[\frac{dy}{dt}=\frac{dy}{dx}\times\frac{dx}{dt}.\]

So find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) from the equation, substitute the value of \(x\) at that instant, then multiply by the given rate. If both rates are given, divide them to find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\), and solve for \(x\).

Worked example 1: Finding dy/dt

Coursebook, 8.4, connected rates on a curve. New numbers.

A point moves along \(y=x^3+2x\) so that \(x\) increases at 0.5 units per second. Find the rate of increase of \(y\) when \(x=2\).

  1. \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=3x^2+2=14\) at \(x=2\) B1
  2. \(\dfrac{dy}{dt}=14\times0.5\) M1
  3. \(=7\) units per second A1

Answer: \(7\) units per second

Worked example 2: A decreasing quantity

Coursebook, 8.4, rates of change. New numbers.

A point moves along \(y=\dfrac{6}{x}\), \(x\gt 0\), so that \(x\) increases at 0.2 units per second. Find the rate of change of \(y\) when \(x=3\).

  1. \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac{6}{x^2}=-\dfrac{2}{3}\) at \(x=3\) B1
  2. \(\dfrac{dy}{dt}=-\dfrac{2}{3}\times0.2\) M1
  3. \(=-0.133\) (3 s.f.), so \(y\) is decreasing A1

Answer: \(-0.133\) units per second

Common mistakes

  • Substituting the value of \(x\) before differentiating.
  • Dividing by \(\dfrac{dx}{dt}\) when you should multiply.
  • Giving a positive answer for a quantity that is decreasing.

A curve has equation \(y=x^2-2x-3\). A point is moving along the curve in such a way that at \(P\) the \(y\)-coordinate is increasing at 4 units per second and the \(x\)-coordinate is increasing at 6 units per second.

Find the \(x\)-coordinate of \(P\). [4]

Mark scheme
  1. \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2x-2\) B1 Official MS: B1, dy/dx = 2x − 2.
  2. \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{4}{6}\) B1 Official MS: B1 OE, SOI.
  3. \(2x-2=\dfrac{4}{6}\) M1 Official MS: M1, their (2x − 2) = their 4/6; LHS and RHS must be their dy/dx expression and value.
  4. \(x=\dfrac{4}{3}\) A1 Official MS: A1 OE, x = 4/3.

Quick check 1

A point moves along the curve \(y=x^2+4x\) so that its \(x\)-coordinate increases at a rate of 5 units per second. Find the rate of increase of the \(y\)-coordinate when \(x=3\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2x+4=10\)
  2. \(10\times5\)

Answer: \(50\) units per second

Quick check 2

A point moves along the curve \(y=\dfrac{8}{x}\) so that its \(x\)-coordinate increases at a rate of 3 units per second. Find the rate of change of the \(y\)-coordinate when \(x=2\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac{8}{4}=-2\)
  2. \(-2\times3\)

Answer: \(-6\) units per second

Quick check 3

A point moves along the curve \(y=x^2-2x\). At the point \(P\), the \(y\)-coordinate is increasing at 12 units per second and the \(x\)-coordinate is increasing at 3 units per second. Find the \(x\)-coordinate of \(P\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{12}{3}=4\)
  2. \(2x-2=4\)

Answer: \(3\)
