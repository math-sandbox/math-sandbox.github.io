22.2 Using and rearranging formulae

Based on Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics Core and Extended Coursebook (Karen Morrison and Nick Hamshaw), Chapter 22 More equations, formulae and functions. The wording is original and follows the published section titles and the standard Cambridge IGCSE treatment of the topic. Printed pp.763–767.

Key words: formula, subject of a formula, substitution

Changing the subject

The subject of a formula is the letter on its own on one side, such as \(v\) in \(v=u+at\). To make another letter the subject, do the same operation to both sides, undoing the operations in reverse order, just as when solving an equation.

For example, to make \(t\) the subject of \(v=u+at\): subtract \(u\) to get \(v-u=at\), then divide by \(a\) to get \(t=\dfrac{v-u}{a}\).

When the new subject appears twice

  • Clear any fractions by multiplying both sides by the denominator.
  • Expand any brackets.
  • Collect all the terms containing the new subject on one side and everything else on the other side.
  • Factorise so that the new subject appears only once.
  • Divide by the bracket.

After rearranging, you can substitute values to find the new subject.

Worked example 1: Simple rearrangement

Coursebook, 22.2, rearranging formulae. New numbers.

Make \(r\) the subject of \(A=\pi r^2\), and find \(r\) when \(A=50\).

  1. Divide by \(\pi\): \(r^2=\dfrac{A}{\pi}\) M1
  2. \(r=\sqrt{\dfrac{A}{\pi}}\) A1
  3. \(r=\sqrt{\dfrac{50}{\pi}}=3.99\) (3 s.f.) B1

Answer: \(r=\sqrt{\dfrac{A}{\pi}}\), \(3.99\)

Worked example 2: Subject on both sides

Coursebook, 22.2, the subject appears twice. New numbers.

Make \(x\) the subject of \(y=\dfrac{x+4}{x-1}\).

  1. \(y(x-1)=x+4\), so \(xy-y=x+4\) M1
  2. \(xy-x=y+4\) M1
  3. \(x(y-1)=y+4\) M1
  4. \(x=\dfrac{y+4}{y-1}\) A1

Answer: \(x=\dfrac{y+4}{y-1}\)

Common mistakes

  • Dividing only one term by a number: from \(v-u=at\), writing \(t=v-\dfrac{u}{a}\).
  • Leaving the new subject on both sides of the formula.
  • Making a sign error when moving a term to the other side.

Make \(p\) the subject of the formula.

\[rp+5=3p+8r\]

[3]

Mark scheme
  1. \(rp-3p=8r-5\) M1 Official MS: M1 for correctly collecting terms in p on one side and terms not in p on the other side.
  2. \(p(r-3)=8r-5\) M1 Official MS: M1 for correct factorising.
  3. \(p=\dfrac{8r-5}{r-3}\) A1 Official MS: (8r − 5)/(r − 3) OE final answer, 3 marks; the third mark is M1 for correct division dependent on p appearing only once in a factorised expression. Maximum M2 for an incorrect final answer.

\[x=\frac{3m}{2-m}\]

[4]

Mark scheme
  1. \(x(2-m)=3m\), so \(2x-xm=3m\) M1 Official MS: M1 for correctly clearing the denominator and expanding the bracket.
  2. \(2x=3m+xm\) M1 Official MS: M1 for correctly collecting terms in m on one side and terms not in m on the other.

  4. \(m=\dfrac{2x}{3+x}\) A1 Official MS: 2x/(3 + x) OE final answer, 4 marks; the fourth mark is M1 for correct division dependent on m appearing only once in a factorised expression.

Quick check 1

Make \(t\) the subject of \(v=u+at\). Find \(t\) when \(v=23\), \(u=5\) and \(a=3\).

Worked solution
  1. \(t=\dfrac{v-u}{a}\)
  2. \(t=\dfrac{18}{3}\)

Answer: \(t=6\)

Quick check 2

Make \(h\) the subject of \(A=\dfrac{1}{2}(a+b)h\). Find \(h\) when \(A=42\), \(a=5\) and \(b=9\).

Worked solution
  1. \(h=\dfrac{2A}{a+b}\)
  2. \(h=\dfrac{84}{14}\)

Answer: \(h=6\)

Quick check 3

Make \(p\) the subject of \(rp+5=3p+8r\). Find \(p\) when \(r=5\).

Worked solution
  1. \(p(r-3)=8r-5\), so \(p=\dfrac{8r-5}{r-3}\)
  2. \(p=\dfrac{35}{2}\)

Answer: \(p=17.5\)
