7.3 Tangents and normals

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 7 Differentiation, printed pp.201–205. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook method for equations of tangents and normals.

Key words: tangent, normal, gradient

Equations of tangents and normals

The tangent to a curve at a point \(P(x_1, y_1)\) is the straight line through \(P\) with the same gradient as the curve at \(P\). The normal at \(P\) is the straight line through \(P\) perpendicular to the tangent.

  1. Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) and substitute \(x_1\) to get the tangent gradient \(m\).
  2. The normal gradient is \(-\dfrac{1}{m}\) (perpendicular gradients multiply to \(-1\)).
  3. Use \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\) with the gradient you need.

Worked example 1: Tangent and normal

Coursebook, 7.3, tangents and normals. New numbers.

Find the equations of the tangent and the normal to \(y=x^3-2x\) at the point \((2, 4)\).

  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=3x^2-2=10\) at \(x=2\) M1 A1
  2. Tangent: \(y-4=10(x-2)\), so \(y=10x-16\) A1
  3. Normal: \(y-4=-\dfrac{1}{10}(x-2)\), so \(x+10y-42=0\) M1 A1

Answer: \(y=10x-16\); \(x+10y-42=0\)

Worked example 2: A tangent with a given gradient

Coursebook, 7.3, finding a point from the gradient. New numbers.

The tangent to \(y=x^2-4x+7\) at \(P\) is parallel to \(y=2x\). Find the coordinates of \(P\).

  1. \(2x-4=2\) M1
  2. \(x=3\), \(y=9-12+7=4\) A1

Answer: \(P(3, 4)\)

Common mistakes

  • Using the derivative expression instead of its value at the point as the gradient of a straight line.
  • Forgetting to take the negative reciprocal for a normal.
  • Using the wrong point: substitute the coordinates of the point on the curve.

9709/12/F/M/24 Q5, official QP and official MS.

A curve has the equation \(y=\dfrac{3}{2x^2-5}\).

Find the equation of the normal to the curve at the point \((2, 1)\), giving your answer in the form \(ax+by+c=0\), where \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) are integers. [6]

Mark scheme
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=kx(2x^2-5)^{-2}\) M1 Official MS: M1, differentiate to obtain the form kx(2x² − 5)⁻².
  2. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=-12x(2x^2-5)^{-2}\) A1 Official MS: A1 OE.
  3. At \((2, 1)\): gradient \(=-\dfrac{24}{9}=-\dfrac{8}{3}\) A1 Official MS: A1 OE, −24/9 or −8/3; allow −2.67.
  4. Gradient of the normal \(=\dfrac{3}{8}\) *M1 Official MS: *M1, apply the negative reciprocal to their numerical gradient; must have been some attempt at differentiation.
  5. \(y-1=\dfrac{3}{8}(x-2)\) M1 Official MS: DM1, attempt the equation of the normal using their normal gradient and (2, 1).
  6. \(3x-8y+2=0\) A1 Official MS: A1, allow multiples or equivalent of the requested form, e.g. 8y − 3x − 2 = 0.

Quick check 1

Find the gradient of the normal to \(y=2x^2+3x+1\) at \(x=1\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=4x+3=7\)

Answer: \(-\dfrac{1}{7}=-0.143\) (3 s.f.)

Quick check 2

The tangent to \(y=2x^2+3x+1\) at \(x=1\) meets the \(y\)-axis at \(Q\). Find the \(y\)-coordinate of \(Q\).

Worked solution
  1. Point \((1, 6)\), gradient 7
  2. \(y-6=7(x-1)\), so \(y=7x-1\)

Answer: \(-1\)

Quick check 3

The normal to \(y=x^2+2x+1\) at \(x=1\) meets the \(x\)-axis at \(R\). Find the \(x\)-coordinate of \(R\).

Worked solution
  1. Point \((1, 4)\), tangent gradient 4, normal gradient \(-\dfrac{1}{4}\)
  2. \(0-4=-\dfrac{1}{4}(x-1)\), so \(x-1=16\)

Answer: \(17\)
