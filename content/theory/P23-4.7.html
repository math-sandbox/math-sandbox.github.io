4.7 Parametric differentiation

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, Julianne Hughes), Chapter 4 Differentiation, pp.103–107 (Worked examples 4.14–4.15, Exercise 4G). Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: parameter, parametric equations, parametric differentiation, chain rule

Parametric equations

Sometimes \(x\) and \(y\) are both given in terms of a third variable, called a parameter. The two equations are then the parametric equations of the curve. For example, \(x=1+4\sin t\) and \(y=2\cos t\), for \(0\le t\le 2\pi\), describe an ellipse: as \(t\) varies, the point \((x,y)\) traces the curve.

Parametric differentiation

To find \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) in terms of the parameter, use the chain rule:

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}\div\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{\mathrm{d}y/\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}t},$$ provided \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}\neq0\). This is parametric differentiation. If \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}=0\) but \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}\neq0\), the tangent is parallel to the \(y\)-axis. If \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}=0\) but \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}\neq0\), the tangent is parallel to the \(x\)-axis (a stationary point on the curve).

Once you have \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) in terms of the parameter, you can find a gradient at a given value of \(t\), and then the equation of a tangent or a normal, by first finding the corresponding coordinates \((x,y)\).

Worked example 1: A tangent from a parameter

Book: Worked example 4.14, p.104; Exercise 4G Q1, p.105

The parametric equations of a curve are \(x=t^2\), \(y=7-3t\). (a) Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) in terms of \(t\). (b) Find the equation of the tangent to the curve at the point where \(t=2\).

  1. (a) \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}=2t\), \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}=-3\), so \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{-3}{2t}\) M1 A1
  2. (b) At \(t=2\): gradient \(=-\dfrac{3}{4}\), point \((4,\,1)\) B1
  3. \(y-1=-\dfrac{3}{4}(x-4)\), so \(y=-\dfrac{3}{4}x+4\) M1 A1

Answer: (a) \(-\dfrac{3}{2t}\) (b) \(y=-\dfrac34 x+4\)

Worked example 2: A tangent parallel to an axis

Book: Worked example 4.15, pp.104–105; Exercise 4G Q2, p.105

The parametric equations of a curve are \(x=t^2-3\), \(y=t^3-3t\). Find the values of \(t\) at which the tangent is parallel to the \(x\)-axis, and the corresponding points on the curve.

  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}=2t\), \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}=3t^2-3\), so \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{3t^2-3}{2t}\) M1 A1
  2. Parallel to the \(x\)-axis: \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}=0\) and \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}\neq0\), so \(3(t^2-1)=0\) and \(t\neq0\) M1
  3. \(t=\pm1\). When \(t=1\): \((-2,\,-2)\); when \(t=-1\): \((-2,\,2)\) A1 A1

Answer: \(t=1\) at \((-2,\,-2)\); \(t=-1\) at \((-2,\,2)\)

Worked example 3: A gradient at a given value of the parameter

Book: Exercise 4G Q1, p.105

The parametric equations of a curve are \(x=2t^3\), \(y=6t\). Find the gradient of the curve at the point where \(t=1\).

  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}=6t^2\), \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}=6\), so \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{6}{6t^2}=\dfrac{1}{t^2}\) M1 A1
  2. At \(t=1\): gradient \(=1\) A1

Answer: \(1\)

Quick checks

Quick check 1

The parametric equations of a curve are \(x=3t^2\), \(y=6t\). Find the gradient of the curve at the point where \(t=2\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}=6t\), \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}=6\), so \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{6}{6t}=\dfrac{1}{t}\)
  2. At \(t=2\): \(\dfrac12=0.5\)

Answer: \(0.5\)

Quick check 2

The parametric equations of a curve are \(x=t^3\), \(y=t^2-5\). Find the gradient of the curve at the point where \(t=1\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}=3t^2\), \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}=2t\), so \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{2t}{3t^2}=\dfrac{2}{3t}\)
  2. At \(t=1\): \(\dfrac23\)

Answer: \(\dfrac23\approx0.667\)

Quick check 3

The parametric equations of a curve are \(x=2t\), \(y=t^3-12t\). Find the positive value of \(t\) at which the tangent is parallel to the \(x\)-axis.

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}=3t^2-12\), \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}=2\)
  2. \(3t^2-12=0\), so \(t^2=4\). The positive value is \(t=2\)

Answer: \(2\)

Quick check 4

The parametric equations of a curve are \(x=t^2\), \(y=4t\). Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) in terms of \(t\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}=2t\), \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}=4\), so \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{4}{2t}=\dfrac{2}{t}\)

Answer: \(\dfrac{2}{t}\)

Marks: M = method, A = accurate answer (depends on M), B = independent mark. Marks on worked examples are illustrative, not official. Give exact answers unless the question asks for a decimal. Find \(\mathrm{d}y/\mathrm{d}t\) and \(\mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}t\) first, then divide. The tangent is parallel to the \(y\)-axis when \(\mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}t=0\) but \(\mathrm{d}y/\mathrm{d}t\neq0\).
