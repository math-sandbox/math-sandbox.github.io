9.5 Intersection of two lines

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 9 Vectors, printed pp.236–239. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook method for deciding whether two lines are parallel, intersect or are skew.

Key words: skew lines, intersecting lines, parallel lines

Three possibilities in three dimensions

Two lines in three dimensions are either parallel, intersecting, or skew. Skew lines are not parallel and do not meet.

  1. Compare the direction vectors. If one is a multiple of the other, the lines are parallel (or the same line).
  2. If not, write a general point on each line, using different parameters such as \(\lambda\) and \(\mu\).
  3. Equate two pairs of components and solve for \(\lambda\) and \(\mu\).
  4. Substitute into the third component. If it agrees, the lines intersect; find the point by substituting \(\lambda\) into the first line. If it does not agree, the lines are skew.

Angle between two lines

The angle between two lines is the angle between their direction vectors. For the acute angle, use \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{\lvert \mathbf{b}_1\cdot\mathbf{b}_2\rvert }{\lvert \mathbf{b}_1\rvert \lvert \mathbf{b}_2\rvert }\).

Worked example 1: Intersecting lines

Coursebook, 9.5, intersection of lines. New numbers.

\(l\): \(\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{i}+\lambda(\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}+\mathbf{k})\) and \(m\): \(\mathbf{r}=2\mathbf{i}+3\mathbf{j}+\mathbf{k}+\mu(\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}+\mathbf{k})\). Show that the lines intersect and find the point of intersection.

  1. \(x\): \(1+\lambda=2+\mu\); \(y\): \(\lambda=3-\mu\) M1
  2. So \(4-\mu=2+\mu\), giving \(\mu=1\) and \(\lambda=2\) A1
  3. \(z\): \(\lambda=2\) and \(1+\mu=2\) agree, so the lines intersect at \((3, 2, 2)\). A1

Answer: \((3, 2, 2)\)

Worked example 2: Skew lines

Coursebook, 9.5, skew lines. New numbers.

\(l\): \(\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{i}+\lambda(\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}+\mathbf{k})\) and \(m\): \(\mathbf{r}=4\mathbf{j}+\mu(\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}+\mathbf{k})\). Show that the lines are skew.

  1. The direction vectors are not multiples of each other, so the lines are not parallel. B1
  2. \(x\): \(1+\lambda=\mu\); \(y\): \(\lambda=4-\mu\), so \(\lambda=\dfrac{3}{2}\), \(\mu=\dfrac{5}{2}\) M1 A1
  3. \(z\): \(\lambda=\dfrac{3}{2}\) but \(\mu=\dfrac{5}{2}\), so the lines do not meet: they are skew. A1

Answer: Not parallel and do not intersect, so skew

Common mistakes

  • Using the same parameter for both lines.
  • Solving two component equations and forgetting to check the third.
  • Calling non-parallel lines that do not meet parallel. In three dimensions they are skew.

Quick check 1

\(l\): \(\mathbf{r}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\0\end{pmatrix}+\lambda\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\\2\end{pmatrix}\) and \(m\): \(\mathbf{r}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\\5\end{pmatrix}+\mu\begin{pmatrix}2\\1\\-1\end{pmatrix}\) intersect. Find \(\lambda\) at the point of intersection.

Worked solution
  1. \(1+\lambda=1+2\mu\) and \(2-\lambda=-1+\mu\)
  2. So \(\lambda=2\mu\) and \(3=3\mu\): \(\mu=1\), \(\lambda=2\); \(z\): \(4=4\).

Answer: \(\lambda=2\)

Quick check 2

  1. \(z=0+2\times2\)
