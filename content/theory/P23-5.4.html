5.4 Further integration of trigonometric functions

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton and Julianne Hughes), Chapter 5 Integration, printed pp.121–125 (Worked examples 5.8–5.13, Exercise 5D). Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: trigonometric identity, double-angle formula, indefinite integral, definite integral

Rewriting before you integrate

Sometimes an integrand such as \(\sin^2 x\) or \(\tan^2 x\) is not in a form that can be integrated directly with the results of Section 5.3. The method is to use a trigonometric identity to rewrite the integrand, and only then integrate. The result is an indefinite integral (plus a constant) or, when limits are given, a definite integral.

To integrate \(\sin^2 x\) and \(\cos^2 x\), rearrange the double-angle identities \(\cos 2x\equiv 1-2\sin^2 x\) and \(\cos 2x\equiv 2\cos^2 x-1\): $$\sin^2 x\equiv\frac{1}{2}(1-\cos 2x),\qquad\cos^2 x\equiv\frac{1}{2}(1+\cos 2x).$$ The same idea applies to a linear argument: for example, \(\cos^2 2x\equiv\dfrac{1}{2}(1+\cos 4x)\). To integrate \(\tan^2 x\), rearrange \(1+\tan^2 x\equiv\sec^2 x\) to obtain \(\tan^2 x\equiv\sec^2 x-1\), and then use \(\int\sec^2 x\,\mathrm{d}x=\tan x+c\).

Worked example 1: Integrating a sine square

Book: Worked example 5.8, p.122; Exercise 5D Q1

Find \(\displaystyle\int 2\sin^2 x\,\mathrm{d}x\).

  1. \(2\sin^2 x=2\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}(1-\cos 2x)=1-\cos 2x\) M1
  2. \(\displaystyle\int(1-\cos 2x)\,\mathrm{d}x=x-\dfrac{1}{2}\sin 2x+c\) A1

Answer: \(x-\dfrac{1}{2}\sin 2x+c\)

Worked example 2: A definite integral of a tangent square

Book: Worked example 5.10, p.123; Exercise 5D Q2

Find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/6} 3\tan^2 x\,\mathrm{d}x\).

  1. \(3\tan^2 x=3(\sec^2 x-1)\) B1
  2. \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/6} 3(\sec^2 x-1)\,\mathrm{d}x=\bigl[3\tan x-3x\bigr]_0^{\pi/6}\) M1 A1
  3. \(=\bigl(3\tan\dfrac{\pi}{6}-3\cdot\dfrac{\pi}{6}\bigr)-(0)=\bigl(3\cdot\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\bigr)=\sqrt{3}-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\) A1

Answer: \(\sqrt{3}-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\)

Worked example 3: Integrating by reversing a derivative

Book: Worked example 5.11, p.123; Exercise 5D Q4

It is given that \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(\sec x)=\sec x\tan x\). Hence find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/4} 3\sec x\tan x\,\mathrm{d}x\).

  1. The integrand is three times the given derivative, so the antiderivative is \(3\sec x\). M1
  2. \(\bigl[3\sec x\bigr]_0^{\pi/4}=3\sec\dfrac{\pi}{4}-3\sec 0=3\sqrt{2}-3\cdot 1=3(\sqrt{2}-1)\) A1

Answer: \(3(\sqrt{2}-1)\)

Common mistakes

  • Writing \(\int\sin^2 x\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{1}{3}\sin^3 x+c\). The power-reduction identity must be used first.
  • Forgetting the factor \(\dfrac{1}{2}\) in \(\sin^2 x\equiv\dfrac{1}{2}(1-\cos 2x)\), or using \(\cos x\) instead of \(\cos 2x\).
  • Integrating \(\tan^2 x\) as if it were \(\sec^2 x\), without subtracting 1.
  • Leaving out \(+c\) from an indefinite integral, or using degrees instead of radians in a definite integral.

Quick checks

Quick check 1

Find \(\displaystyle\int\sin^2 x\,\mathrm{d}x\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\sin^2 x=\dfrac{1}{2}(1-\cos 2x)\), so the integral is \(\dfrac{1}{2}x-\dfrac{1}{4}\sin 2x+c\)

Answer: \(\dfrac{1}{2}x-\dfrac{1}{4}\sin 2x+c\)

Quick check 2

The indefinite integral \(\displaystyle\int 6\sin^2 2x\,\mathrm{d}x\) can be written in the form \(ax+b\sin 4x+c\). Find the value of \(a\).

Worked solution
  1. \(6\sin^2 2x=6\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}(1-\cos 4x)=3-3\cos 4x\), so the coefficient of \(x\) is 3

Answer: \(3\)

Quick check 3

Find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/4}(4\tan^2 x+4)\,\mathrm{d}x\).

Worked solution
  1. \(4\tan^2 x+4=4(\sec^2 x-1)+4=4\sec^2 x\)
  2. \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/4} 4\sec^2 x\,\mathrm{d}x=\bigl[4\tan x\bigr]_0^{\pi/4}=4\tan\dfrac{\pi}{4}-0=4\)

Answer: \(4\)

Quick check 4

Find \(\displaystyle\int\tan^2 x\,\mathrm{d}x\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\tan^2 x=\sec^2 x-1\), so the integral is \(\tan x-x+c\)

Answer: \(\tan x-x+c\)

Marks: M = method, A = accurate answer (depends on M), B = independent mark. Marks on worked examples are illustrative, not official. Give exact answers unless a question asks for a decimal. In calculus, angles are in radians unless a question says otherwise. Always include \(+c\) in an indefinite integral.
