6.3 Using iterative processes to solve problems involving other areas of mathematics

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton and Julianne Hughes, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 6 Numerical solutions of equations, printed pp.149–154 (physical PDF page = printed page + 12). Own wording. The method is the same iterative process as in 6.2; the new work is to obtain the equation first from another topic (here, a derivative), and only then to locate and improve the root.

Key words: iterative process, change of sign

Iteration after other mathematics

In 6.1 and 6.2 the equation to be solved was usually given. In this section the equation itself often comes from another piece of work: a derivative set equal to a given gradient, a volume or area condition, a trigonometric identity, or the intersection of a curve with a line.

The numerical part is unchanged:

  1. Obtain an equation for the unknown, and rearrange it into a form \(x=\mathrm{F}(x)\) if an iterative formula is to be used.
  2. Show, by a change of sign, that the required root lies in a stated interval (the 6.1 test).
  3. Apply an iterative formula based on that rearrangement, and justify the final accuracy (the 6.2 process).

The first of those steps is the “other area of mathematics”. The last two steps are the iterative process you already have.

Worked example 1: A gradient condition leading to an iteration

6.3 style: a derivative is used to obtain a transcendental equation, then a starting interval is checked and the root is improved by iteration. New curve, y equals e to the x plus x squared, gradient 6 at x equals p.

The curve \(y=\mathrm{e}^x+x^2\) has gradient \(6\) at the point where \(x=p\).

(i) Show that \(p=\ln(6-2p)\).

(ii) Show by calculation that \(1.2<p<1.4\).

(iii) Use the iterative formula \(p_{n+1}=\ln(6-2p_n)\) with \(p_1=1.3\) to find \(p\) correct to 3 decimal places. Give each iterate to 5 decimal places.

  1. (i) \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\mathrm{e}^x+2x\). At \(x=p\) this equals \(6\), so \(\mathrm{e}^p=6-2p\), hence \(p=\ln(6-2p)\) M1
  2. (i) The rearrangement is confirmed. A1
  3. (ii) Let \(g(p)=\ln(6-2p)-p\). Then \(g(1.2)=0.081\) and \(g(1.4)=-0.237\) (3 s.f.). There is a change of sign, so \(1.2<p<1.4\) M1
  4. (ii) The argument is complete. A1
  5. (iii) \(p_2=\ln(6-2\times 1.3)=\ln 3.4\)
    \(=1.22378\).
    \(p_3=1.26764\), \(p_4=1.24263\),
    \(p_5=1.25696\), \(p_6=1.24878\).
    \(p_7=1.25346\), \(p_8=1.25078\),
    \(p_9=1.25232\), \(p_{10}=1.25144\).
    \(p_{11}=1.25194\), \(p_{12}=1.25165\).
    Later terms lie in \((1.2515,1.2525)\), so \(p=1.252\) to 3 decimal places M1
  6. (iii) The value is \(1.252\), justified by the bounding interval. A1

Answer: \(p=\ln(6-2p)\); \(1.2<p<1.4\); \(p=1.252\)

Common mistakes

  • Differentiating incorrectly, or forgetting to set the derivative equal to the given gradient, so the later iteration is applied to the wrong equation.
  • Treating the sign-change check or the iteration as a new method, rather than the 6.1 and 6.2 process already met.
  • Using a starting value that has not been justified by a calculation or a sketch.

Past paper

9709/22/M/J/16 Q5, official QP and official MS.

The equation of a curve is \(y=6x\mathrm{e}^{x/3}\). At the point on the curve with \(x\)-coordinate \(p\), the gradient of the curve is \(40\).

(i) Show that \(p=3\ln\left(\dfrac{20}{p+3}\right)\). [4]

(ii) Show by calculation that \(3.3<p<3.5\). [2]

(iii) Use an iterative formula based on the equation in part (i) to find the value of \(p\) correct to 3 decimal places. Give the result of each iteration to 5 decimal places. [3]

Mark scheme
  1. (i) Use the product rule to obtain a form \(k_1\mathrm{e}^{x/3}+k_2 x\mathrm{e}^{x/3}\). M1
  2. (i) Obtain the correct derivative \(6\mathrm{e}^{x/3}+2x\mathrm{e}^{x/3}\). A1
  3. (i) Equate the first derivative to \(40\) and obtain an equation without \(\mathrm{e}\) present (dependent on the previous method mark). M1
  4. (i) Confirm \(p=3\ln\dfrac{20}{p+3}\), or the same with \(x\) in place of \(p\). A1
  5. (ii) Consider the sign of \(3\ln\dfrac{20}{p+3}-p\) at \(3.3\) and at \(3.5\), or equivalent. M1
  6. (ii) Complete the argument correctly with appropriate calculations. A1
  7. (iii) Carry out the iterative process correctly at least once. M1
  8. (iii) Obtain the final answer \(3.412\). A1
  9. (iii) Show sufficient iterations to justify accuracy to 3 decimal places, or show a sign change in the interval \((3.4115,3.4125)\). B1

Quick checks

Quick check 1

The gradient of \(y=x^3+2x\) is \(14\) when \(x=p\). Find \(p^2\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=3x^2+2=14\), so \(3p^2=12\) and \(p^2=4\)

Answer: \(4\)

Quick check 2

The iterative formula \(p_{n+1}=\dfrac{10-p_n}{3}\) is used with \(p_1=1\). Find \(p_2\).

Worked solution
  1. \(p_2=\dfrac{10-1}{3}=3\)

Answer: \(3\)

Quick check 3

The same formula is used, and \(p_2=3\). Find \(3p_3\).

Worked solution
  1. \(p_3=\dfrac{10-3}{3}=\dfrac{7}{3}\), so \(3p_3=7\)

Answer: \(7\)
