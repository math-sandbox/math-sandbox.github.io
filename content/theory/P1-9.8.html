9.8 Improper integrals

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 9 Integration, printed pp.264–268. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook treatment of an infinite limit and of an integrand that is undefined at a limit, as limits of definite integrals.

Key words: improper integral, definite integral, asymptote

Two kinds of improper integral

An improper integral is a definite integral where either a limit is infinite, or the function is not defined at one of the limits. It is found as the limit of an ordinary definite integral, if that limit exists.

An infinite limit

\[\int_a^{\infty}\mathrm{f}(x)\,dx=\lim_{X\to\infty}\int_a^X\mathrm{f}(x)\,dx.\]

For example, \(\dfrac{k}{X}\to0\) as \(X\to\infty\). Show clearly that the term from the infinite limit tends to 0.

A function undefined at a limit

\(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\) is not defined at \(x=0\), and the \(y\)-axis is an asymptote. Integrate from a small positive number \(t\) and let \(t\to0\). If the limit is not finite, for example \(\displaystyle\int_0^1\frac{1}{x^2}\,dx\), the integral has no value.

Worked example 1: To infinity

Coursebook, 9.8, an infinite upper limit. New numbers.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_1^{\infty}\frac{6}{x^4}\,dx\).

  1. \(\Bigl[-\dfrac{2}{x^3}\Bigr]_1^X=-\dfrac{2}{X^3}+2\) B1
  2. As \(X\to\infty\), \(\dfrac{2}{X^3}\to0\) M1
  3. \(=2\) A1

Answer: \(2\)

Worked example 2: Undefined at 0

Coursebook, 9.8, an integrand undefined at a limit. New numbers.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^9\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx\).

  1. \(\Bigl[2\sqrt{x}\Bigr]_t^9=6-2\sqrt{t}\) B1
  2. As \(t\to0\), \(2\sqrt{t}\to0\) M1
  3. \(=6\) A1

Answer: \(6\)

Common mistakes

  • Substituting \(\infty\) as if it were a number, without explaining that the term tends to 0.
  • Not noticing that the function is undefined at a limit.
  • Giving a value when the limit does not exist.

9709/12/F/M/24 Q1, official QP and official MS.

Find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_3^{\infty}\frac{2}{x^2}\,dx\). [3]

Mark scheme
  1. \(\displaystyle\int\frac{2}{x^2}\,dx=-2x^{-1}\) B1 Official MS: B1 OE, integrate to obtain −2x^(−1).
  2. \(\Bigl[-\dfrac{2}{x}\Bigr]_3^{\infty}=0-\Bigl(-\dfrac{2}{3}\Bigr)\) M1 Official MS: M1, substitute limits correctly with clear indication seen that upper limit gives 0 (for an integral of the form −kx^(−n), k > 0, n > 0).
  3. \(=\dfrac{2}{3}\) A1 Official MS: A1 WWW; accept 0.667.

Quick check 1

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_2^{\infty}\frac{5}{x^2}\,dx\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\Bigl[-\dfrac{5}{x}\Bigr]_2^{X}=-\dfrac{5}{X}+\dfrac{5}{2}\to\dfrac{5}{2}\)

Answer: \(2.5\)

Answer: \(12\)
