8.4 The normal approximation to the binomial distribution

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 1 Coursebook (Dean Chalmers), Chapter 8 The normal distribution, printed pp.208–214. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook conditions np > 5 and nq > 5, the mean np and variance npq, and continuity corrections.

Key words: normal approximation, continuity correction, binomial distribution, normal distribution

When can a normal distribution be used?

For large \(n\), the bar chart of \(X\sim\mathrm{B}(n, p)\) has a bell shape. If \(np\gt 5\) and \(nq\gt 5\), where \(q=1-p\), the binomial distribution can be approximated by the normal distribution with the same mean and variance:

\[X\sim\mathrm{B}(n, p)\quad\text{is approximated by}\quad\mathrm{N}(np,\ npq).\]

This normal approximation saves adding many binomial probabilities, for example \(P(X\lt 20)\) when \(n=80\).

Continuity corrections

A binomial variable takes only whole-number values, but a normal variable is continuous. Each whole number \(r\) is represented by the interval from \(r-0.5\) to \(r+0.5\). This adjustment is called a continuity correction.

BinomialNormal (after correction)
\(P(X\le 20)\)\(P(Y\lt 20.5)\)
\(P(X\lt 20)\)\(P(Y\lt 19.5)\)
\(P(X\ge 20)\)\(P(Y\gt 19.5)\)
\(P(X\gt 20)\)\(P(Y\gt 20.5)\)
\(P(X=20)\)\(P(19.5\lt Y\lt 20.5)\)

To decide the correction, list the whole numbers that are included and take the edge of the first or last one.

Worked example 1: Fewer than

Coursebook, 8.4, normal approximation with a continuity correction. New numbers.

\(X\sim\mathrm{B}(50, 0.4)\). Use a suitable approximation to find \(P(X\lt 17)\).

  1. \(np=20\gt 5\), \(nq=30\gt 5\); \(np=20\), \(npq=12\) B1
  2. \(P(X\lt 17)=P(X\le 16)\approx P\Bigl(Z\lt \dfrac{16.5-20}{\sqrt{12}}\Bigr)\) M1 M1
  3. \(=P(Z\lt -1.010)=1-\Phi(1.010)=1-0.8438=0.156\) M1 A1

Answer: \(0.156\) (3 s.f.)

Worked example 2: A range of values

Coursebook, 8.4, a two-sided interval. New numbers.

A fair coin is thrown 100 times. Use a suitable approximation to find the probability of obtaining from 45 to 55 heads inclusive.

  1. \(np=50\), \(npq=25\), \(\sigma=5\) B1
  2. \(P(45\le X\le 55)\approx P(44.5\lt Y\lt 55.5)=P(-1.1\lt Z\lt 1.1)\) M1 M1
  3. \(=2\Phi(1.1)-1=2\times0.8643-1=0.729\) M1 A1

Answer: \(0.729\) (3 s.f.)

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the continuity correction, or moving it the wrong way: 'fewer than 20' is \(19.5\), 'more than 20' is \(20.5\).
  • Using the variance \(npq\) instead of the standard deviation \(\sqrt{npq}\) when standardising.
  • Not checking \(np\gt 5\) and \(nq\gt 5\) before using the approximation.

Quick check 1

\(X\sim\mathrm{B}(60, 0.4)\). A normal approximation is used to find \(P(X\lt 20)\). Find the standardised value \(z\).

Worked solution
  1. \(np=24\), \(npq=14.4\)
  2. \(P(X\lt 20)=P(X\le 19)\), use \(19.5\)
  3. \(z=\dfrac{19.5-24}{\sqrt{14.4}}\)

Answer: \(-1.19\) (3 s.f.)

Quick check 2

\(X\sim\mathrm{B}(n, 0.15)\). Find the least value of \(n\) for which \(np\gt 5\) and \(nq\gt 5\).

Worked solution
  1. The smaller of \(p\) and \(q\) is \(0.15\)
  2. \(n\gt \dfrac{5}{0.15}=33.3\ldots\)

Answer: \(34\)

Quick check 3

\(X\sim\mathrm{B}(100, 0.3)\). Using a normal approximation, \(P(X\gt 35)\approx1-\Phi(z)\). Find \(z\).

Worked solution
  1. \(np=30\), \(npq=21\)
  2. Use \(35.5\)
  3. \(z=\dfrac{35.5-30}{\sqrt{21}}\)

Answer: \(1.20\) (3 s.f.)
