21.3 Rates

Based on Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics Core and Extended Coursebook (Karen Morrison and Nick Hamshaw), Chapter 21 Ratio, rate and proportion. The wording is original and follows the published section titles and the standard Cambridge IGCSE treatment of the topic. Printed pp.725–728.

Key words: rate, average speed

What a rate is

A rate compares two quantities of different kinds, for example litres per minute, dollars per hour or kilometres per hour. A rate tells you how much of the first quantity there is for one unit of the second.

Speed

Speed is the rate at which distance changes with time.

\[\text{speed}=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}},\qquad \text{distance}=\text{speed}\times\text{time},\qquad \text{time}=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}\]

For a whole journey, average speed \(=\dfrac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}}\). It is not the mean of the separate speeds.

To change km/h into m/s, multiply by 1000 and divide by 3600, which is the same as dividing by 3.6. So \(54\text{ km/h}=15\text{ m/s}\).

Worked example 1: Average speed

Coursebook, 21.3, speed and average speed. New numbers.

A bus travels 30 km in 40 minutes and then 45 km in 50 minutes. Find its average speed for the whole journey in km/h.

  1. Total distance \(=75\) km and total time \(=90\) minutes \(=1.5\) hours. M1
  2. Average speed \(=75\div1.5=50\) km/h A1

Answer: 50 km/h

Worked example 2: A rate of pay

Coursebook, 21.3, rates in context. New numbers.

Nadia earns 126 dollars for 9 hours of work. At the same rate, how much does she earn for 14 hours?

  1. Rate \(=126\div9=14\) dollars per hour M1
  2. \(14\times14=196\) dollars A1

Answer: 196 dollars

Common mistakes

  • Finding the mean of two speeds instead of total distance divided by total time.
  • Mixing minutes and hours. Change 40 minutes to \(\dfrac{40}{60}\) hours before using km/h.
  • Forgetting the length of a train when it crosses a bridge or passes through a tunnel.

Past paper

0580/22/F/M/24 Q14, official QP and official MS.

A train, 1750 metres long, is travelling at 55 km/h.

Calculate how long it will take for the whole train to completely cross a bridge that is 480 metres long.

Give your answer in seconds, correct to the nearest second. [3]

Mark scheme
  1. The front must travel the length of the bridge plus the length of the train: distance \(=1750+480=2230\) m. M1 Official MS: M1 for distance = 1750 + 480 oe, or for 55 × 1000 (÷ 60 ÷ 60).
  2. Time \(=\dfrac{2230}{55\times1000}\times60\times60\) seconds M1 Official MS: M2 for (1750 + 480)/(55 × 1000) × 60 × 60 oe.
  3. \(=145.96\ldots\), so 146 seconds A1 Official MS: 3 marks for 146 cao.

Past paper

0580/23/O/N/21 Q8, official QP and official MS.

Beatrice walks 1 km at a speed of 4 km/h and then 2 km at a speed of 4.5 km/h.

Work out Beatrice’s average speed for the whole journey. [3]

Mark scheme
  1. Times: \(\dfrac{1}{4}\) h and \(\dfrac{2}{4.5}\) h B1 Official MS: B1 for 1/4 oe or 2/4.5 oe seen.
  2. Average speed \(=\dfrac{1+2}{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{2}{4.5}}\) M1 Official MS: M1 dep on B1 for (1 + 2)/(their 1/4 + their 2/4.5) oe.
  3. \(=4.32\) km/h A1 Official MS: 3 marks for 4.32.

Quick check 1

A car travels at 72 km/h. Write this speed in metres per second.

Worked solution
  1. 72 km/h is 72 000 m in 3600 s.
  2. \(72\,000\div3600\)

Answer: 20 m/s

Quick check 2

Ola walks 2 km at a speed of 4 km/h and then 3 km at a speed of 6 km/h. Work out Ola's average speed for the whole journey.

Worked solution
  1. Time \(=\dfrac{2}{4}+\dfrac{3}{6}=1\) hour
  2. Average speed \(=5\div1\)

Answer: 5 km/h

Quick check 3

Water flows into a tank at a rate of 12 litres per minute. How many minutes does it take to put 600 litres of water into the tank?

Worked solution
  1. \(600\div12\)

Answer: 50 minutes
