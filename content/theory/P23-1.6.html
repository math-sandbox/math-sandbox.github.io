1.6 The remainder theorem

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Hugh Neill, Douglas Quadling and Julian Gilbey), Chapter 1 Algebra, printed pp.18–21 (Key points 1.5–1.6, Worked examples 1.12–1.13, Exercise 1F). Own wording and new numbers. Long division itself was taught in P23-1.4; the method here is substitution.

Key words: remainder theorem, remainder, polynomial

If a polynomial \(P(x)\) is divided by \(x-c\) to give a polynomial \(Q(x)\) and a remainder \(R\), then \[ P(x)=(x-c)Q(x)+R. \] Substituting \(x=c\) gives \(P(c)=R\). This is the remainder theorem.

A common error is substituting the wrong point for a remainder, for example using \(P(c)\) when the divisor is \(x+c\).

Key point. If a polynomial \(P(x)\) is divided by \(x-c\), the remainder is \(P(c)\).

The remainder theorem can be extended. If a polynomial \(P(x)\) is divided by \(ax-b\), the remainder is \(P\bigl(\dfrac{b}{a}\bigr)\).

When the remainder is \(0\), the divisor is a factor, which recovers the factor theorem of the previous subsection. The remainder can be found by substitution, without carrying out the long division.

Worked example 1: A remainder by substitution

Book: Worked example 1.12, p.19; new cubic

Use the remainder theorem to find the remainder when \(x^3-5x+7\) is divided by \(x+2\).

  1. Let \(f(x)=x^3-5x+7\). Division is by \(x-(-2)\), so the remainder is \(f(-2)\). M1
  2. \(f(-2)=(-2)^3-5(-2)+7=-8+10+7=9\). A1

Answer: \(9\)

Worked example 2: Two remainder conditions

Book: Worked example 1.13, pp.19–20; new cubic

The function \(f(x)=x^3+ax^2+bx-6\) is such that, when \(f(x)\) is divided by \(x-1\), the remainder is \(4\), and when \(f(x)\) is divided by \(x+2\), the remainder is \(-2\). Find the value of \(a\) and the value of \(b\).

  1. By the remainder theorem, \(f(1)=4\) and \(f(-2)=-2\). M1
  2. \(f(1)=1+a+b-6=4\), so \(a+b=9\). \(f(-2)=-8+4a-2b-6=-2\), so \(4a-2b=12\), or \(2a-b=6\). M1
  3. Adding \(a+b=9\) and \(2a-b=6\) gives \(3a=15\), so \(a=5\). Then \(b=9-5=4\). A1

Answer: \(a=5\), \(b=4\)

Common mistakes

  • For division by \(x+c\), substituting \(x=c\) instead of \(x=-c\).
  • For division by \(ax-b\), substituting \(x=a/b\) instead of \(x=b/a\).
  • Carrying out a full long division when the question only asks for the remainder, which the remainder theorem gives directly.

Quick checks

Quick check 1

Find the remainder when \(x^3-5x+7\) is divided by \(x+2\).

Worked solution

The remainder is \(f(-2)=(-2)^3-5(-2)+7=9\).

Quick check 2

Find the remainder when \(x^3-5x+7\) is divided by \(x-2\).

Worked solution

The remainder is \(f(2)=8-10+7=5\).

Quick check 3

When \(x^3+ax^2+bx-6\) is divided by \(x-1\) the remainder is \(4\), and when it is divided by \(x+2\) the remainder is \(-2\). What is the value of \(a\)?

Worked solution

As in Worked example 2, \(a=5\).
