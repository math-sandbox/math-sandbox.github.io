7.2 The geometric distribution

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 1 Coursebook (Dean Chalmers), Chapter 7 The binomial and geometric distributions, printed pp.175–184. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook conditions for a geometric model, the formula for geometric probabilities, the results for P(X > r) and the mean 1/p.

Key words: geometric distribution, success, trial

When is a geometric model suitable?

Sometimes we repeat a trial until the first success and count how many trials are needed. If the trials are independent and the probability of success \(p\) is the same for every trial, the number of trials \(X\) up to and including the first success has a geometric distribution. We write \(X\sim\mathrm{Geo}(p)\).

There is no fixed number of trials, so \(X\) can take any of the values \(1, 2, 3, \dots\). This is the main difference from a binomial model.

Geometric probabilities

For the first success to be on trial \(r\), the first \(r-1\) trials must be failures and then trial \(r\) a success. With \(q=1-p\),

\[P(X=r)=q^{r-1}p,\qquad r=1, 2, 3, \dots\]

More than \(r\) trials are needed exactly when the first \(r\) trials are all failures, so \(P(X\gt r)=q^r\) and \(P(X\le r)=1-q^r\). The most likely value (the mode) is always \(X=1\).

The mean

If \(X\sim\mathrm{Geo}(p)\), then \(E(X)=\dfrac{1}{p}\). For example, the mean number of throws of a fair die needed to obtain a six is 6.

Worked example 1: The first success on the third trial

Coursebook, 7.2, geometric probabilities. New numbers.

\(X\sim\mathrm{Geo}(0.2)\). Find \(P(X=3)\) and \(P(X\le 3)\).

  1. \(P(X=3)=0.8^2\times0.2\) M1
  2. \(=0.128\) A1
  3. \(P(X\le 3)=1-0.8^3=1-0.512=0.488\) B1

Answer: \(P(X=3)=0.128\), \(P(X\le 3)=0.488\)

Worked example 2: Waiting for a six

Coursebook, 7.2, P(X > r) and the mean. New numbers.

A fair die is thrown until a six is obtained. Find the probability that more than 4 throws are needed, and the mean number of throws.

  1. \(X\sim\mathrm{Geo}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{6}\bigr)\) B1
  2. \(P(X\gt 4)=\bigl(\tfrac{5}{6}\bigr)^4\) M1
  3. \(=0.482\) (3 s.f.) A1
  4. \(E(X)=\dfrac{1}{1/6}=6\) B1

Answer: \(P(X\gt 4)=0.482\), \(E(X)=6\)

Common mistakes

  • Writing \(P(X=r)=q^rp\). Only \(r-1\) failures come before the first success.
  • Mixing up \(P(X\gt r)=q^r\) and \(P(X\ge r)=q^{r-1}\).
  • Using a geometric model when the number of trials is fixed. Then the number of successes is binomial.

Quick check 2

\(X\sim\mathrm{Geo}(0.25)\). Find \(P(X\gt 5)\).

Worked solution
  1. The first 5 trials are all failures.
  2. \(P(X\gt 5)=0.75^5\)

Answer: \(0.237\) (3 s.f.)

Quick check 3

A fair spinner has 6 equal sectors numbered 1 to 6. It is spun repeatedly until a 1 is obtained. Find the probability that a 1 is obtained in at most 3 spins.

Worked solution
