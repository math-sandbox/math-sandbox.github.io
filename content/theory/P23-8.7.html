8.7 Further integration

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 8 Further calculus, printed pp.201–202. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook advice on choosing a method of integration.

Key words: integration by substitution, integration by parts, partial fractions

Choosing a method

Before integrating, look at the structure of the integrand and decide which method fits.

  • A standard integral: powers, \(\mathrm{e}^{ax+b}\), \(\dfrac{1}{ax+b}\), trigonometric functions and \(\dfrac{1}{a^2+x^2}\).
  • The numerator is a multiple of the derivative of the denominator: a logarithm (8.3).
  • A function and its derivative appear together, such as \(\sin x\cos^3x\): reverse the chain rule or use integration by substitution (8.4).
  • A rational function whose denominator factorises: partial fractions (8.5). Divide first if the fraction is improper.
  • A product of two different types of function, such as \(x\mathrm{e}^{2x}\) or \(x\ln x\): integration by parts (8.6).
  • Powers of trigonometric functions: use identities, such as \(\cos^2x=\frac{1}{2}(1+\cos2x)\).

Some questions need two methods, for example a substitution that leads to an integral done by parts.

Worked example 1: Recognition

Coursebook, 8.7, choosing a method. New numbers.

Find \(\displaystyle\int\sin x\cos^3x\,\mathrm{d}x\).

  1. The derivative of \(\cos x\) is \(-\sin x\), so try \(\cos^4x\): \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\cos^4x=-4\sin x\cos^3x\). M1
  2. \(-\dfrac{1}{4}\cos^4x+c\) A1

Answer: \(-\dfrac{1}{4}\cos^4x+c\)

Worked example 2: An improper fraction

Coursebook, 8.7, dividing before integrating. New numbers.

Find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^1\frac{x}{x+1}\,\mathrm{d}x\).

  1. \(\dfrac{x}{x+1}=1-\dfrac{1}{x+1}\) M1
  2. \(\bigl[x-\ln(x+1)\bigr]_0^1\) A1
  3. \(=1-\ln2\) A1

Answer: \(1-\ln2\)

Common mistakes

  • Using integration by parts when a simple substitution or recognition works.
  • Trying partial fractions on an improper fraction without dividing first.
  • Forgetting to check a result by differentiating it.

9709/32/O/N/20 Q9, official QP and official MS.

Let \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{7x+18}{(3x+2)(x^2+4)}\).

(a) Express \(\mathrm{f}(x)\) in partial fractions. [5]

(b) Hence find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^2\mathrm{f}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\). [6]

Mark scheme
  1. (a) \(\dfrac{A}{3x+2}+\dfrac{Bx+C}{x^2+4}\) B1 Official MS: B1, correct form.
  2. (a) \(7x+18=A(x^2+4)+(Bx+C)(3x+2)\); use a correct method to find a constant. M1 Official MS: M1.
  3. (a) \(A=3\) A1 Official MS: A1.
  4. (a) \(B=-1\) A1 Official MS: A1.
  5. (a) \(C=3\), so \(\mathrm{f}(x)=\dfrac{3}{3x+2}+\dfrac{3-x}{x^2+4}\) A1 Official MS: A1.
  6. (b) \(\displaystyle\int\frac{3}{3x+2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\ln(3x+2)\) B1 Official MS: B1 FT.
  7. (b) \(\displaystyle\int\frac{-x}{x^2+4}\,\mathrm{d}x\) is of the form \(k\ln(x^2+4)\) M1 Official MS: M1.
  8. (b) \(-\frac{1}{2}\ln(x^2+4)\) A1 Official MS: A1 FT.
  9. (b) \(\displaystyle\int\frac{3}{x^2+4}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{3}{2}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}x\right)\) B1 Official MS: B1 FT.
  10. (b) Limits: \(\ln8-\frac{1}{2}\ln8+\frac{3}{2}\times\frac{\pi}{4}-\ln2+\frac{1}{2}\ln4\) M1 Official MS: M1, substitute limits correctly in at least two terms and subtract in the correct order.
  11. (b) \(=\frac{3}{2}\ln2+\frac{3}{8}\pi\) A1 Official MS: A1, or exact equivalent.

Quick check 1

Find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin x\cos^3x\,\mathrm{d}x\).

Worked solution
  1. \(-\dfrac{1}{4}\cos^4x\)
  2. \(-\dfrac{1}{4}\times(0-1)\)

Answer: \(\dfrac{1}{4}\)

Quick check 2

Find the value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^3\frac{x}{x+1}\,\mathrm{d}x\), correct to 3 significant figures.

Worked solution
  1. \(x-\ln(x+1)\)
  2. \(3-\ln4\)

Answer: \(1.61\)

Quick check 3

Find the value of \(\displaystyle\int_1^2\frac{\ln x}{x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x\), correct to 3 significant figures.

Worked solution
  1. \(-\dfrac{\ln x+1}{x}\)
  2. \(-\dfrac{\ln2+1}{2}+1\)

Answer: \(0.153\)
