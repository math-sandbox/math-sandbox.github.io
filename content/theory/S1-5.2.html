5.2 Permutations

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 1 Coursebook (Dean Chalmers), Chapter 5 Permutations and combinations, printed pp.125–134. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook treatment of permutations of distinct objects, of objects with repetitions, of arrangements with restrictions, and of \({}^{n}P_{r}\).

Key words: permutation

Arranging different objects

A permutation is an arrangement of objects in which the order matters. There are \(n\) choices for the first place in a line of \(n\) different objects, then \(n-1\) choices for the second place, and so on, so

\[\text{the number of arrangements of } n \text{ different objects in a line is } n!.\]

Objects that are not all different

If some of the objects are identical, swapping identical objects does not give a new arrangement. For \(n\) objects of which \(p\) are identical of one type, \(q\) of another and \(r\) of a third, the number of arrangements is

\[\dfrac{n!}{p!\,q!\,r!}.\]

Arrangements with restrictions

  • Objects that must be together: treat them as a single unit, arrange the units, then multiply by the number of ways of arranging the objects inside the unit.
  • Objects that must not be together: either subtract the arrangements in which they are together from the total, or arrange the other objects first and place the separated objects in different gaps between them.
  • Objects in fixed places, such as at the ends: fill the fixed places first, then arrange the rest.

Permutations of \(r\) objects from \(n\)

The number of ordered arrangements of \(r\) objects chosen from \(n\) different objects is

\[{}^{n}P_{r}=\dfrac{n!}{(n-r)!}=n(n-1)\cdots(n-r+1).\]

Worked example 1: Letters with repeats

Coursebook, 5.2, permutations of objects with repetitions. New word.

Find the number of different arrangements of the 6 letters of the word PEPPER.

  1. PEPPER has 3 Ps, 2 Es and 1 R, so the number of arrangements is \(\dfrac{6!}{3!\,2!\,1!}\). M1
  2. \(=\dfrac{720}{12}=60\) A1

Answer: \(60\)

Worked example 2: Together and not together

Coursebook, 5.2, permutations with restrictions. New numbers.

Six different books, including a dictionary and an atlas, are arranged in a row on a shelf. Find the number of arrangements in which (a) the dictionary and the atlas are next to each other, (b) the dictionary and the atlas are not next to each other.

  1. (a) Treat the dictionary and the atlas as one unit: 5 units in \(5!\) ways, and the two books inside the unit in \(2!\) ways. M1
  2. (a) \(5!\times2!=240\) A1
  3. (b) Total \(6!=720\), so the number not together is \(720-240\). M1
  4. (b) \(=480\) A1

Answer: (a) \(240\) (b) \(480\)

Worked example 3: Ordered choices

Coursebook, 5.2, permutations of \(r\) objects from \(n\). New numbers.

A race has 10 runners. In how many ways can the first, second and third places be filled, if there are no ties?

  1. Order matters, so the number of ways is \({}^{10}P_{3}=10\times9\times8\). M1
  2. \(=720\) A1

Answer: \(720\)

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to divide by the factorials of repeated letters, or dividing by the wrong ones.
  • Treating a group that must be together as one unit but forgetting the arrangements inside the unit (unless the objects in it are identical).
  • Counting the gaps wrongly: \(k\) objects in a line have \(k+1\) gaps, including the two ends.

Past paper

9709/51/M/J/20 Q2, official QP and official MS.

(a) Find the number of different arrangements that can be made from the 9 letters of the word JEWELLERY in which the three Es are together and the two Ls are together. [2]

(b) Find the number of different arrangements that can be made from the 9 letters of the word JEWELLERY in which the two Ls are not next to each other. [4]

Mark scheme
  1. (a) Treat EEE and LL as single units, which gives 6 different units to arrange: \(6!\). M1 Official MS: M1, 6!.
  2. (a) \(720\) A1 Official MS: A1, 720.
  3. (b) Total number of arrangements: \(\dfrac{9!}{3!\,2!}=30\,240\). M1 Official MS: M1, total number 9!/(3!2!) (30240).
  4. (b) Number with the Ls together: \(\dfrac{8!}{3!}=6720\). M1 Official MS: M1, number with Ls together = 8!/3! (6720).
  5. (b) Number with the Ls not together: \(\dfrac{9!}{3!\,2!}-\dfrac{8!}{3!}=30\,240-6720\). M1 Official MS: M1, number with Ls not together = 9!/(3!2!) − 8!/3!.
  6. (b) \(23\,520\) A1 Official MS: A1, 23 520. Alternative method: 7! × 8 × 7 / (3! × 2), with M1 for 7! × k in numerator, k integer ≥ 1; M1 for 8 × 7 × m in numerator or 8C2 × m, m integer ≥ 1; M1 for 3! in denominator; A1 for 23 520.

Past paper

9709/52/F/M/20 Q4, official QP and official MS.

Richard has 3 blue candles, 2 red candles and 6 green candles. The candles are identical apart from their colours. He arranges the 11 candles in a line.

(a) Find the number of different arrangements of the 11 candles if there is a red candle at each end. [2]

(b) Find the number of different arrangements of the 11 candles if all the blue candles are together and the red candles are not together. [4]

Mark scheme
  1. (a) With a red candle at each end, the 3 blue and 6 green candles fill the 9 places in between: \(\dfrac{9!}{3!\,6!}\). M1 Official MS: M1, 9! alone on numerator, 3! × k or 6! × k on denominator.
  2. (a) \(=84\) A1 Official MS: A1, 84.
  3. (b) Treat the 3 blue candles as one unit and arrange it with the 6 green candles: \(\dfrac{7!}{6!}=7\) ways. M1 Official MS: M1, 7!/6! × k or 7k seen, k an integer > 0.
  4. (b) Place the 2 red candles in different gaps between and around these 7 items: \(\dfrac{7!}{6!}\times\dfrac{8\times7}{2}\). M1 Official MS: M1, m × n(n – 1)/2 or m × nC2 or m × nP2, n = 7, 8 or 9, m an integer > 0.
  5. (b) There are 8 such gaps, so \(n=8\) is used. M1 Official MS: M1, n = 8 used in above expression.
  6. (b) \(=196\) A1 Official MS: A1, 196. Alternative: arrangements with the blues together minus arrangements with the blues together and the reds together, 9!/(2!6!) − 8!/6! = 252 – 56; M1 for 9! seen alone or as numerator with subtraction, M1 for 8! seen alone or as numerator in a second term and no other terms, M1 for all terms divided by 6! × k, k an integer, A1 for 196.

Quick check 1

In how many different ways can 7 different books be arranged in a row on a shelf?

Worked solution
  1. The number of arrangements is \(7!=5040\).

Answer: \(5040\)

Quick check 2

A row is made from 3 identical red counters, 2 identical blue counters and 2 identical green counters. How many different arrangements of the 7 counters are possible?

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{7!}{3!\,2!\,2!}=\dfrac{5040}{24}=210\)

Answer: \(210\)

Quick check 3

3 different prizes are to be given to 3 of 8 people, and no person can receive more than one prize. In how many different ways can the prizes be given?

Worked solution
  1. \({}^{8}P_{3}=8\times7\times6=336\)

Answer: \(336\)
