4.4 Conditional probability

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 1 Coursebook (Dean Chalmers), Chapter 4 Probability, pp.108–111 (Worked examples 4.9–4.11, Exercise 4E). Own wording and new numbers. The formal formula \(\mathrm{P}(A\mid B)=\mathrm{P}(A\cap B)/\mathrm{P}(B)\) is not introduced on these pages; this section uses a reduced sample space.

Key words: conditional probability, given that

Probabilities that depend on extra information

The word conditional is used for a probability that depends on additional information about an outcome or event. We write \(\mathrm{P}(A\mid B)\) for the conditional probability of \(A\) given that \(B\) has occurred, and we read the symbol \(\mid\) as “given that”.

The extra information reduces the set of possible outcomes. If the remaining outcomes are equally likely,

$$\mathrm{P}(A\mid B)=\frac{n(A\cap B)}{n(B)}.$$

In other words, we only count inside the event \(B\): the new denominator is the number of outcomes in \(B\), and the new numerator is the number of those outcomes that are also in \(A\). A two-way table or a Venn diagram is often the clearest way to read off the two counts.

The same idea applies to a second selection when we are told the result of the first: the bag (or the group of people) is reduced by that first outcome, and we work with what is left.

Worked example 1: A reduced sample space

Book: Worked example 4.9, p.108; Exercise 4E Q1, p.110

A letter is selected at random from the eight letters in the word PARALLEL. Find the probability that an L is selected, given that a vowel is not selected.

  1. The word has 8 letters: A, A, E (vowels) and P, R, L, L, L (not vowels), so 5 letters are not vowels B1
  2. Of those 5 letters, 3 are L, so \(\mathrm{P}(\text{L}\mid\text{not a vowel})=\dfrac{3}{5}=0.6\) M1 A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{3}{5}=0.6\)

Worked example 2: A two-way table and a Venn diagram

Book: Worked example 4.10, p.109; Exercise 4E Q2, p.110

The table shows the numbers of students in a class of 24 who study Art (\(A\)) and who study Drama (\(D\)).

\(A\)\(A'\)Totals
\(D\)8412
\(D'\)6612
Totals141024

One student is chosen at random. Find the probability that the student (a) studies Drama, given that they study Art (b) does not study Art, given that they do not study Drama.

  1. (a) 14 study Art, and 8 of these study Drama, so \(\mathrm{P}(D\mid A)=\dfrac{8}{14}=\dfrac{4}{7}\) M1 A1
  2. (b) 12 do not study Drama, and 6 of these do not study Art, so \(\mathrm{P}(A'\mid D')=\dfrac{6}{12}=\dfrac12\) M1 A1

Answer: (a) \(\dfrac{4}{7}\approx0.571\) (b) \(\dfrac12=0.5\)

Worked example 3: A second selection, given the first

Book: Worked example 4.11, p.109; Exercise 4E Q3, p.110

Two children are selected at random from a group of 6 girls and 4 boys. Find the probability that the second child selected is a girl, given that the first child selected is (a) a girl (b) a boy.

  1. (a) If the first is a girl, 5 girls and 4 boys remain, so \(\mathrm{P}(\text{second is a girl}\mid\text{first is a girl})=\dfrac{5}{9}\) B1
  2. (b) If the first is a boy, 6 girls and 3 boys remain, so \(\mathrm{P}(\text{second is a girl}\mid\text{first is a boy})=\dfrac{6}{9}=\dfrac23\) B1

Answer: (a) \(\dfrac{5}{9}\approx0.556\) (b) \(\dfrac23\approx0.667\)

Independence, seen through a conditional probability

Section 4.3 defined independent events by the multiplication law. A matching description is that each event is unaffected by the occurrence of the other: the probability that \(X\) occurs is the same when \(Y\) occurs and when \(Y\) does not occur, that is \(\mathrm{P}(X\mid Y)=\mathrm{P}(X\mid Y')\). (This formal statement begins at the end of p.111 and is completed at the top of p.112, immediately before the heading of Section 4.5.)

Quick checks

Quick check 1

A letter is selected at random from the word BANANA. Find the probability that an N is selected, given that an A is not selected.

Worked solution
  1. BANANA has 6 letters, of which 3 are not A (B, N, N)
  2. Of those 3 letters, 2 are N, so the probability is \(\dfrac{2}{3}\)

Answer: \(\dfrac{2}{3}\approx0.667\)

Quick check 2

In a group of 20 students, 12 study French and 8 of those 12 also study Spanish. One student is chosen at random. Find the probability that the student studies Spanish, given that the student studies French.

Worked solution
  1. 12 study French, and 8 of these study Spanish, so the probability is \(\dfrac{8}{12}=\dfrac{2}{3}\)

Answer: \(\dfrac{2}{3}\approx0.667\)

Quick check 3

Two counters are selected at random from a bag containing 5 red counters and 3 blue counters. Find the probability that the second counter is red, given that the first counter is red.

Worked solution
  1. If the first is red, 4 red and 3 blue remain, so the probability is \(\dfrac{4}{7}\)

Answer: \(\dfrac{4}{7}\approx0.571\)

Quick check 4

The following table shows the numbers of members of a club.
