7.4 Second derivatives

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 7 Differentiation, printed pp.205–208. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook notation for the second derivative.

Key words: second derivative, derivative

Differentiating twice

Differentiating \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) again gives the second derivative, written \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}\) or \(f''(x)\). It measures how fast the gradient is changing.

For example, if \(y=x^4-3x^2\), then \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=4x^3-6x\) and \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}=12x^2-6\).

Write roots and fractions as powers before differentiating, and differentiate the simplified first derivative.

In Chapter 8 the sign of the second derivative is used to decide whether a stationary point is a maximum or a minimum.

Worked example 1: A polynomial

Coursebook, 7.4, second derivatives. New numbers.

Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}\) for \(y=2x^4-x^3+5x\), and its value at \(x=-1\).

  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=8x^3-3x^2+5\) M1
  2. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}=24x^2-6x\) A1
  3. At \(x=-1\): \(24+6=30\) A1

Answer: \(24x^2-6x\); \(30\)

Worked example 2: Negative and fractional powers

Coursebook, 7.4, second derivatives of powers. New numbers.

Find \(f''(x)\) for \(f(x)=\dfrac{6}{x^2}+4\sqrt{x}\).

  1. \(f(x)=6x^{-2}+4x^{\frac{1}{2}}\) B1
  2. \(f'(x)=-12x^{-3}+2x^{-\frac{1}{2}}\) M1
  3. \(f''(x)=36x^{-4}-x^{-\frac{3}{2}}\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{36}{x^4}-\dfrac{1}{x\sqrt{x}}\)

Common mistakes

  • Squaring the first derivative instead of differentiating it again.
  • Sign errors with negative powers: \(-12x^{-3}\) differentiates to \(+36x^{-4}\).
  • Forgetting that a constant term in the first derivative disappears.

Past paper questions use the second derivative together with stationary points, in Chapter 8.

Quick check 1

Given \(y=2x^3+3x^2\), find \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}\) at \(x=1\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=6x^2+6x\)
  2. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}=12x+6\)

Answer: \(18\)

Quick check 2

Given \(y=\dfrac{4}{x}\), find \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}\) at \(x=2\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=-4x^{-2}\)
  2. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}=8x^{-3}=\dfrac{8}{8}\)

Answer: \(1\)

Quick check 3

Given \(y=8\sqrt{x}\), find \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}\) at \(x=4\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=4x^{-\frac{1}{2}}\)
  2. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}=-2x^{-\frac{3}{2}}=-\dfrac{2}{8}\)

Answer: \(-0.25\)
