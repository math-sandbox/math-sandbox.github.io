6.1 Unbiased estimates of population mean and variance

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 2 Coursebook (Jayne Kranat), Chapter 6 Estimation, printed pp.122–126. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook formulae for unbiased estimates from raw, summarised and coded data.

Key words: unbiased estimate, population variance, sample mean

Estimating population parameters

The mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\) of a population are usually unknown. We estimate them from a random sample. An estimate is unbiased if, averaged over all possible samples, it equals the true value.

The sample mean is an unbiased estimate of the population mean:

\[\hat{\mu}=\bar{x}=\frac{\sum x}{n}.\]

The variance of the sample, \(\dfrac{\sum x^2}{n}-\bar{x}^2\), tends to underestimate \(\sigma^2\). Multiplying by \(\dfrac{n}{n-1}\) gives the unbiased estimate:

\[s^2=\frac{n}{n-1}\left(\frac{\sum x^2}{n}-\bar{x}^2\right)=\frac{1}{n-1}\left(\sum x^2-\frac{(\sum x)^2}{n}\right).\]

Coded data

If you are given \(\sum(x-a)\) and \(\sum(x-a)^2\), work with \(y=x-a\). Then \(\bar{x}=a+\bar{y}\), and the variance of \(x\) equals the variance of \(y\), because subtracting a constant does not change the spread.

Worked example 1: From summary statistics

Coursebook, 6.1, unbiased estimates. New numbers.

A random sample of 20 values gives \(\sum x=86\) and \(\sum x^2=410\). Find unbiased estimates of \(\mu\) and \(\sigma^2\).

  1. \(\bar{x}=\dfrac{86}{20}=4.3\) B1
  2. \(s^2=\dfrac{1}{19}\Bigl(410-\dfrac{86^2}{20}\Bigr)=\dfrac{1}{19}(410-369.8)\) M1
  3. \(s^2=2.12\) (3 s.f.) A1

Answer: \(\hat{\mu}=4.3\), \(s^2=2.12\)

Worked example 2: Coded data

Coursebook, 6.1, coded data. New numbers.

For a random sample of 25 values, \(\sum(x-100)=-15\) and \(\sum(x-100)^2=129\). Find unbiased estimates of \(\mu\) and \(\sigma^2\).

  1. \(\bar{x}=100+\dfrac{-15}{25}=99.4\) B1
  2. \(s^2=\dfrac{1}{24}\Bigl(129-\dfrac{(-15)^2}{25}\Bigr)=\dfrac{1}{24}(129-9)\) M1
  3. \(s^2=5\) A1

Answer: \(\hat{\mu}=99.4\), \(s^2=5\)

Common mistakes

  • Giving the sample variance (dividing by \(n\)) when an unbiased estimate is asked for.
  • Forgetting to add the coding constant back when estimating the mean from coded data.
  • Adding the coding constant to the variance. Subtracting a constant does not change the variance.

\(n=75\)  \(\sum x=153.2\)  \(\sum x^2=340.24\)

Quick check 1

A random sample of 40 values gives \(\sum x=200\) and \(\sum x^2=1120\). Find an unbiased estimate of the population variance.

Worked solution
  1. \(\bar{x}=5\) and \(\dfrac{1120}{40}-5^2=3\)
  2. \(s^2=\dfrac{40}{39}\times3\)

Answer: \(3.08\) (3 s.f.)

Quick check 2

A random sample of 40 values gives \(\sum(x-30)=24\). Find an unbiased estimate of the population mean.

Worked solution
  1. \(\bar{x}=30+\dfrac{24}{40}\)

Answer: \(30.6\)

Quick check 3

A random sample of 30 values gives \(\sum(x-50)=60\) and \(\sum(x-50)^2=236\). Find an unbiased estimate of the population variance.

Worked solution
  1. \(s^2=\dfrac{1}{29}\Bigl(236-\dfrac{60^2}{30}\Bigr)=\dfrac{1}{29}(236-120)\)

Answer: \(4\)
