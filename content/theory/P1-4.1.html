4.1 Radians

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton), Chapter 4 Circular measure, pp.101–103 (Key points 4.1–4.4, Worked example 4.1, Exercise 4A). Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: radian, arc, subtend, degrees, in terms of π

What is a radian?

Draw a circle with centre \(O\) and radius \(r\). Mark an arc whose length is exactly \(r\). The angle that this arc subtends at \(O\) (the angle it makes at the centre) is 1 radian. It is sometimes written 1 rad, but usually an angle with no degree sign is taken to be in radians.

The whole circumference has length \(2\pi r\), which is \(2\pi\) radius-lengths, so a full turn is \(2\pi\) radians.

$$2\pi\text{ radians}=360^\circ\qquad\pi\text{ radians}=180^\circ$$

When an angle is written in terms of π, such as \(\frac{\pi}{4}\), we normally leave out the word radians. So in angle work, \(\pi\) stands for \(180^\circ\).

Converting

From degrees to radians: multiply by \(\dfrac{\pi}{180}\). From radians to degrees: multiply by \(\dfrac{180}{\pi}\).

$$\theta^\circ=\theta\times\frac{\pi}{180}\text{ rad},\qquad \theta\text{ rad}=\theta\times\frac{180}{\pi}\ ^\circ$$

For simple fractions of \(180^\circ\) it is quicker to scale: \(180^\circ=\pi\), so \(60^\circ=\frac{\pi}{3}\), \(36^\circ=\frac{\pi}{5}\), and so on. A useful check: \(1\text{ rad}=\frac{180}{\pi}\approx57.3^\circ\), a little less than \(60^\circ\).

Calculator: when an angle is in radians, set the calculator to radian mode (R or RAD) before using sin, cos or tan.

Worked example 1: Degrees to radians, in terms of π

Book: Worked example 4.1a, p.102; Exercise 4A Q1 p.102

Change \(75^\circ\) to radians, giving your answer in terms of \(\pi\).

  1. Method 1: \(180^\circ=\pi\), so \(15^\circ=\frac{\pi}{12}\) (divide by 12) M1
  2. \(75^\circ=5\times15^\circ=\frac{5\pi}{12}\) A1
  3. Method 2: \(75\times\frac{\pi}{180}=\frac{75\pi}{180}=\frac{5\pi}{12}\) (same answer)

Answer: \(\frac{5\pi}{12}\)

Worked example 2: Radians to degrees

Book: Worked example 4.1b, p.102; Exercise 4A Q2 p.102

Change \(\frac{7\pi}{10}\) radians to degrees.

  1. Method 1: \(\pi=180^\circ\), so \(\frac{\pi}{10}=18^\circ\) M1
  2. \(\frac{7\pi}{10}=7\times18^\circ=126^\circ\) A1
  3. Method 2: \(\frac{7\pi}{10}\times\frac{180}{\pi}=\frac{7\times180}{10}=126\) (same answer)

Answer: \(126^\circ\)

Worked example 3: Decimal answers

Book: Exercise 4A Q3–4, p.103

(a) Write \(220^\circ\) in radians, correct to 3 significant figures. (b) Write \(0.6\) radians in degrees, correct to 1 decimal place.

  1. (a) \(220\times\frac{\pi}{180}=3.8397\ldots\) M1
  2. \(=3.84\) rad (3 s.f.) A1
  3. (b) \(0.6\times\frac{180}{\pi}=34.377\ldots\) M1
  4. \(=34.4^\circ\) (1 d.p.) A1

Answer: (a) \(3.84\) (b) \(34.4^\circ\)

Worked example 4: Angles in a triangle in radians

Book: Key point 4.2, p.101 (angle facts in radians)

Two angles of a triangle are \(\frac{\pi}{4}\) and \(\frac{5\pi}{12}\). Find the third angle, in terms of \(\pi\) and in degrees.

  1. Angles in a triangle add up to \(180^\circ=\pi\) B1
  2. \(\pi-\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{5\pi}{12}=\frac{12\pi-3\pi-5\pi}{12}\) M1
  3. \(=\frac{4\pi}{12}=\frac{\pi}{3}\) A1
  4. \(\frac{\pi}{3}=\frac{180^\circ}{3}=60^\circ\) B1

Answer: \(\frac{\pi}{3}\), which is \(60^\circ\)

Common mistakes

  • Multiplying by the wrong fraction: degrees \(\to\) radians uses \(\frac{\pi}{180}\), so the answer is the smaller number.
  • Writing \(\pi=3.14^\circ\): in angle work \(\pi\) means \(180^\circ\).
  • Not simplifying: \(\frac{75\pi}{180}\) should be written \(\frac{5\pi}{12}\).
  • Rounding too early: keep the full calculator value until the last step.

Quick checks

Quick check 1

Change \(150^\circ\) to radians, giving your answer in terms of \(\pi\). Work on paper, then reveal the solution and self-mark M1 for a correct conversion method and A1 for the simplified exact answer; no typed numeric answer is used.

Worked solution
  1. \(30^\circ=\frac{\pi}{6}\) M1
  2. \(150^\circ=5\times30^\circ=\frac{5\pi}{6}\) A1

Answer: \(\frac{5\pi}{6}\)

Quick check 2

Change \(\frac{7\pi}{12}\) radians to degrees.

Worked solution
  1. \(\frac{\pi}{12}=\frac{180^\circ}{12}=15^\circ\)
  2. \(7\times15^\circ=105^\circ\)

Answer: \(105^\circ\)

Quick check 3

Write \(47^\circ\) in radians, correct to 3 significant figures.

Worked solution
  1. \(47\times\frac{\pi}{180}=0.82030\ldots\)
  2. \(=0.820\) (3 s.f.; the final zero is significant)

Answer: \(0.820\)

Quick check 4

Write \(1.3\) radians in degrees, correct to 1 decimal place.

Worked solution
  1. \(1.3\times\frac{180}{\pi}=74.484\ldots\)
  2. \(=74.5^\circ\) (1 d.p.)

Answer: \(74.5^\circ\)
