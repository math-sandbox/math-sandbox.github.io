9.3 Integration of expressions of the form (ax + b)^n

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 9 Integration, printed pp.247–248. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook rule for integrating (ax + b)^n as the reverse of the chain rule.

Key words: integration, chain rule, constant of integration

Reversing the chain rule

By the chain rule, \(\dfrac{d}{dx}(ax+b)^{n+1}=a(n+1)(ax+b)^n\). Reversing this gives

\[\int(ax+b)^n\,dx=\frac{(ax+b)^{n+1}}{a(n+1)}+c,\qquad n\ne-1.\]

Add one to the power, divide by the new power, and also divide by \(a\), the coefficient of \(x\). The rule works for negative and fractional powers, so write \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2x+1}}\) as \((2x+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\) first. Do not expand the bracket.

Worked example 1: A positive power

Coursebook, 9.3, integrating (ax + b)^n. New numbers.

Find \(\displaystyle\int(3x-1)^5\,dx\).

  1. \(\dfrac{(3x-1)^6}{6\times3}\) M1
  2. \(=\dfrac{(3x-1)^6}{18}+c\) A1

Answer: \(\dfrac{1}{18}(3x-1)^6+c\)

Worked example 2: A negative power

Coursebook, 9.3, a power in the denominator. New numbers.

Find \(\displaystyle\int\frac{6}{(2x+1)^2}\,dx\).

  1. \(\displaystyle\int6(2x+1)^{-2}\,dx=\dfrac{6(2x+1)^{-1}}{-1\times2}\) M1
  2. \(=-\dfrac{3}{2x+1}+c\) A1

Answer: \(-\dfrac{3}{2x+1}+c\)

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to divide by \(a\).
  • Multiplying by \(a\) instead of dividing (that is the rule for differentiating).
  • Expanding the bracket for a large power instead of using the rule.

A curve is such that \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{8}{\sqrt{(4x+1)}}\). The point \((2, 5)\) lies on the curve. Find the equation of the curve. [4]

Mark scheme
  1. \(y=\dfrac{8(4x+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\frac{1}{2}}\ldots\) B1 Official MS: B1, correct integrand (unsimplified) without ÷4.
  2. \(\ldots\div4\ (+c)\) B1 Official MS: B1, for ÷4; ignore c.
  3. \(5=4\sqrt{9}+c\) M1 Official MS: M1, substitution of correct values into an integrand to find c (uses x = 2 and y = 5).
  4. \(c=-7\), so \(y=4\sqrt{4x+1}-7\) A1 Official MS: A1, c = −7; y = 4√(4x + 1) − 7.

Quick check 1

\(\displaystyle\int(2x+3)^4\,dx=\dfrac{(2x+3)^5}{K}+c\). Find \(K\).

Worked solution
  1. New power 5; the coefficient of \(x\) is 2

Answer: \(K=10\)

Quick check 2

A curve has \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{4x+1}}\) and passes through \((2, 5)\). Find its equation.

Worked solution
  1. \(y=\dfrac{4(4x+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\frac{1}{2}\times4}+C=2\sqrt{4x+1}+C\)
  2. \(5=6+C\)

Answer: \(y=2\sqrt{4x+1}-1\)
