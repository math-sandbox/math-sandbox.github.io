13.2 Time

Based on Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics Core and Extended Coursebook, 3rd edition (Karen Morrison and Nick Hamshaw), Chapter 13 Understanding measurement, printed pp.405–408 (Worked examples 2–4, Exercise 13.2, and reading timetables on p.408). Printed pages were read from rendered scans of part_041 (pp.405–408). Own wording and new numbers.

Key words: 12-hour clock, 24-hour clock, timetable

Working with time

Times may be written on the 12-hour clock (with am or pm) or on the 24-hour clock (as a four-digit time such as \(16\,20\)). To change a pm time after 12 noon into 24-hour form, add 12 hours to the hour. Midnight is \(00\,00\) and 12 noon is \(12\,00\).

Time is not a decimal system: there are 60 minutes in an hour. To find how long something lasts, convert to minutes (or split the interval at a whole hour or at midnight), add or subtract, then convert back to hours and minutes if needed. A timetable uses the 24-hour clock and lists departure and arrival times for a journey.

Worked example 1: A time interval in the afternoon

Book: Worked example 2 style, printed p.405 (Sara left at 2.15 pm and returned at 3.50 pm). New times.

Lina left home at 1.20 pm and returned at 3.05 pm. How long was she away from home?

  1. From 1.20 pm to 2.00 pm is 40 minutes. From 2.00 pm to 3.05 pm is 1 hour and 5 minutes, which is 65 minutes M1
  2. The total is \(40+65=105\) minutes, and \(105=60+45\), so she was away for 1 hour and 45 minutes A1

Answer: 1 hour 45 minutes

Worked example 2: An interval that crosses midnight

Book: Worked example 4 style, printed p.406 (19:35 Monday to 03:55 Tuesday). New times.

How much time passes from \(21\,50\) on Monday to \(07\,15\) on Tuesday?

  1. Split the interval at midnight. From \(21\,50\) to \(24\,00\) is 2 hours 10 minutes M1
  2. From \(00\,00\) to \(07\,15\) is 7 hours 15 minutes. Adding gives \(2\,\mathrm{h}\,10\,\mathrm{min}+7\,\mathrm{h}\,15\,\mathrm{min}=9\,\mathrm{h}\,25\,\mathrm{min}\) A1

Answer: 9 hours 25 minutes

Common mistakes

  • Subtracting times as if they were decimals, for example treating \(18\,20-05\,35\) as \(18.20-5.35\).
  • Forgetting to add 12 hours when changing a pm time to the 24-hour clock, or writing 12 noon as \(00\,00\).
  • Not splitting an interval at midnight when the end time is on the next day.

Quick checks

Quick check 1

Write 4.20 pm in the 24-hour clock as a four-digit time.

Worked solution
  1. Add 12 hours to 4: \(4+12=16\), so 4.20 pm is \(16\,20\) A1

Answer: \(1620\)

Quick check 2

A bus leaves at \(08\,10\) and arrives at \(11\,55\). How many minutes does the journey take?

Worked solution
  1. \(11\,55\) is \(11\times 60+55=715\) minutes after midnight, and \(08\,10\) is \(8\times 60+10=490\) minutes after midnight M1
  2. The difference is \(715-490=225\) minutes A1

Answer: \(225\)

Quick check 3

A train leaves at \(07\,50\) and the journey lasts 2 hours 35 minutes. Write the arrival time as a four-digit 24-hour time.

Worked solution
  1. \(07\,50+2\,\mathrm{h}=09\,50\), then add 35 minutes to obtain \(10\,25\) A1

Answer: \(1025\)

Marks: M = method, A = accurate answer (depends on M), B = independent mark. Time is in hours and minutes, not decimals, unless a question asks for a time in hours as a decimal.
