8.2 The normal distribution

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 1 Coursebook (Dean Chalmers), Chapter 8 The normal distribution, printed pp.193–204. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook use of the standard normal table, standardising, and finding an unknown mean or standard deviation.

Key words: normal distribution, standard normal distribution, standardise

The normal curve

Many continuous variables, such as heights or masses, have a normal distribution. The graph of its probability density is a symmetrical bell-shaped curve with its peak at the mean \(\mu\). We write \(X\sim\mathrm{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\), where \(\sigma^2\) is the variance and \(\sigma\) the standard deviation. About 68% of values lie within one standard deviation of the mean and about 95% within two.

The standard normal distribution

The standard normal distribution is \(Z\sim\mathrm{N}(0, 1)\). Its cumulative probabilities \(\Phi(z)=P(Z\lt z)\) are given in the table in the formula booklet. By symmetry \(P(Z\gt z)=1-\Phi(z)\) and \(\Phi(-z)=1-\Phi(z)\).

Standardising

To find probabilities for \(X\sim\mathrm{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\), we standardise: \(Z=\dfrac{X-\mu}{\sigma}\) has the standard normal distribution, so

\[P(X\lt x)=\Phi\Bigl(\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\Bigr).\]

To work backwards from a probability, read the \(z\)-value from the table and solve \(\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}=z\) for the unknown \(x\), \(\mu\) or \(\sigma\). Always draw a sketch to check whether \(z\) should be positive or negative.

Worked example 1: A probability

Coursebook, 8.2, standardising. New numbers.

\(X\sim\mathrm{N}(50, 16)\). Find \(P(X\gt 56)\).

  1. \(\sigma=4\), \(z=\dfrac{56-50}{4}=1.5\) M1
  2. \(P(X\gt 56)=1-\Phi(1.5)=1-0.9332\) M1
  3. \(=0.0668\) A1

Answer: \(0.0668\)

Worked example 2: An unknown mean

Coursebook, 8.2, finding the mean from a probability. New numbers.

\(X\sim\mathrm{N}(\mu, 4^2)\) and \(P(X\lt 30)=0.1\). Find \(\mu\).

  1. \(\Phi(1.282)=0.9\), so \(P(X\lt 30)=0.1\) gives \(z=-1.282\) B1
  2. \(\dfrac{30-\mu}{4}=-1.282\) M1
  3. \(\mu=30+5.128=35.1\) A1

Answer: \(\mu=35.1\) (3 s.f.)

Common mistakes

  • Dividing by the variance instead of the standard deviation.
  • Using \(+z\) when the value is below the mean. A sketch shows the sign.
  • Forgetting \(1-\Phi(z)\) for an upper-tail probability.

Past paper

9709/61/M/J/14 Q1, official QP and official MS.

The petrol consumption of a certain type of car has a normal distribution with mean 24 kilometres per litre and standard deviation 4.7 kilometres per litre. Find the probability that the petrol consumption of a randomly chosen car of this type is between 21.6 kilometres per litre and 28.7 kilometres per litre. [4]

Mark scheme
  1. \(P(21.6<X<28.7)=P\Bigl(\dfrac{21.6-24}{4.7}<Z<\dfrac{28.7-24}{4.7}\Bigr)\) M1 Official MS: M1, standardising; no continuity correction, no square root.
  2. \(=P(-0.5106<Z<1)\) A1 Official MS: A1, one rounding to Φ (0.841 or 0.695).
  3. \(=\Phi(1)-\Phi(-0.5106)=\Phi(1)+\Phi(0.5106)-1\) M1 Official MS: M1, Φ1 + Φ2 – 1.
  4. \(=0.8413-(1-0.6953)=0.537\) A1 Official MS: A1, 0.537 (0.5366), correct answer.

Past paper

9709/63/O/N/12 Q1, official QP and official MS.

In a normal distribution with mean 9.3, the probability of a randomly chosen value being greater than 5.6 is 0.85. Find the standard deviation. [3]

Mark scheme
  1. \(P(X>5.6)=0.85\), so \(z=-1.036\) B1 Official MS: B1, ±(1.036 to 1.037) seen.
  2. \(\dfrac{5.6-9.3}{\sigma}=-1.036\) M1 Official MS: M1, equation with 5.6 (or 13.0), 9.3, σ and a z-value; no continuity correction.
  3. \(\sigma=3.57\) A1 Official MS: A1, 3.57, correct final answer.

Quick check 1

\(X\sim\mathrm{N}(60, 25)\). Find the value of \(z\) such that \(P(X\lt 68)=\Phi(z)\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\sigma=5\)
  2. \(z=\dfrac{68-60}{5}\)

Answer: \(1.6\)

Quick check 2

\(X\sim\mathrm{N}(50, \sigma^2)\) and \(P(X\lt 60)=0.9\). Given that \(\Phi(1.282)=0.9\), find \(\sigma\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{60-50}{\sigma}=1.282\)
  2. \(\sigma=\dfrac{10}{1.282}\)

Answer: \(7.80\) (3 s.f.)

Quick check 3

\(X\sim\mathrm{N}(40, 36)\). Given that \(\Phi(1.645)=0.95\), find the value of \(x\) such that \(P(X\gt x)=0.05\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{x-40}{6}=1.645\)
  2. \(x=40+9.87\)

Answer: \(49.9\) (3 s.f.)
