8.3 Practical maximum and minimum problems

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 8 Further differentiation, printed pp.221–226. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook steps for forming an expression in one variable from a constraint and finding its greatest or least value.

Key words: stationary point

A method for practical problems

In a practical problem you usually want the greatest or least value of a quantity, such as an area, a volume or a cost.

  • Write the quantity in terms of two variables, then use the given constraint to rewrite it in terms of one variable.
  • Differentiate and solve the derivative equal to zero to find the stationary point.
  • Check whether it is a maximum or a minimum, usually with the second derivative.
  • Answer the question that was asked, with units, and reject values that make no sense, such as a negative length.

Worked example 1: A rectangle with a fixed perimeter

Coursebook, 8.3, practical maximum. New numbers.

A rectangle has perimeter 36 cm. Find the greatest possible area.

  1. Width \(x\), length \(18-x\), so \(A=18x-x^2\) M1
  2. \(\dfrac{dA}{dx}=18-2x=0\), \(x=9\) M1
  3. \(\dfrac{d^2A}{dx^2}=-2\lt 0\), maximum; \(A=81\) cm\(^2\) A1

Answer: \(81\) cm\(^2\), when the rectangle is a square

Worked example 2: An open box

Coursebook, 8.3, maximum volume. New numbers.

Squares of side \(x\) cm are cut from the corners of a 12 cm by 12 cm sheet, and the sides are folded up to make an open box. Find the greatest volume.

  1. \(V=x(12-2x)^2=4x^3-48x^2+144x\) M1
  2. \(\dfrac{dV}{dx}=12x^2-96x+144=12(x-2)(x-6)=0\) M1
  3. \(x=6\) gives \(V=0\), so \(x=2\); \(\dfrac{d^2V}{dx^2}=24x-96=-48\lt 0\), maximum A1
  4. \(V=2\times8^2=128\) cm\(^3\) A1

Answer: \(128\) cm\(^3\)

Common mistakes

  • Differentiating an expression that still has two variables in it.
  • Giving the value of x when the question asks for the greatest area or volume.
  • Not checking that the stationary point is the kind of point (maximum or minimum) the question wants.

Past paper

9709/12/M/J/10 Q8, official QP and official MS.

A solid rectangular block has a square base of side \(x\) cm. The height of the block is \(h\) cm and the total surface area of the block is 96 cm\(^2\).

(i) Express \(h\) in terms of \(x\) and show that the volume, \(V\) cm\(^3\), of the block is given by

\(V=24x-\tfrac{1}{2}x^3.\) [3]

Given that \(x\) can vary,

(ii) find the stationary value of \(V\), [3]

(iii) determine whether this stationary value is a maximum or a minimum. [2]

Mark scheme
  1. (i) \(4xh+2x^2=96\) M1 Official MS: M1, needs to consider at least 5 areas.
  2. (i) \(h=\dfrac{24}{x}-\dfrac{x}{2}\) A1 Official MS: A1 co.
  3. (i) \(V=x^2h=24x-\tfrac{1}{2}x^3\) M1 Official MS: M1, for V = x²h with h as f(x).
  4. (ii) \(\dfrac{dV}{dx}=24-\dfrac{3x^2}{2}\) B1 Official MS: B1 co.
  5. (ii) \(=0\) when \(x=4\) M1 Official MS: M1, sets differential to 0 and solves.
  6. (ii) \(V=64\) A1 Official MS: A1 co.
  7. (iii) \(\dfrac{d^2V}{dx^2}=-3x\) M1 Official MS: M1, any valid method.
  8. (iii) \(-12<0\), so maximum A1 Official MS: A1√ (follow through), co; maximum.

9709/12/F/M/16 Q6, official QP and official MS.

A vacuum flask (for keeping drinks hot) is modelled as a closed cylinder in which the internal radius is \(r\) cm and the internal height is \(h\) cm. The volume of the flask is 1000 cm\(^3\). A flask is most efficient when the total internal surface area, \(A\) cm\(^2\), is a minimum.

(i) Show that \(A=2\pi r^2+\dfrac{2000}{r}\). [3]

(ii) Given that \(r\) can vary, find the value of \(r\), correct to 1 decimal place, for which \(A\) has a stationary value and verify that the flask is most efficient when \(r\) takes this value. [5]

Mark scheme
  1. (i) \(A=2\pi r^2+2\pi rh\) B1 Official MS: B1, A = 2πr² + 2πrh.
  2. (i) \(\pi r^2h=1000\), so \(h=\dfrac{1000}{\pi r^2}\) M1 Official MS: M1, πr²h = 1000 → h = 1000/(πr²).
  3. (i) \(A=2\pi r^2+\dfrac{2000}{r}\) A1 Official MS: A1 AG, sub for h into A.
  4. (ii) \(\dfrac{dA}{dr}=4\pi r-\dfrac{2000}{r^2}\), set \(=0\) M1 Official MS: M1, attempt differentiation and set = 0.
  5. (ii) \(4\pi r-\dfrac{2000}{r^2}=0\) A1 Official MS: A1, correct equation.
  6. (ii) \(r^3=\dfrac{500}{\pi}\) M1 Official MS: DM1 (dependent), reasonable attempt to solve to r³ = …; stored as M1.
  7. (ii) \(r=5.4\) A1 Official MS: A1, r = 5.4.
  8. (ii) \(\dfrac{d^2A}{dr^2}=4\pi+\dfrac{4000}{r^3}>0\), hence minimum, hence most efficient B1 Official MS: B1 AG, or convincing alternative method.

Quick check 1

A rectangle has perimeter 40 cm. Its width is \(x\) cm. Show that its area is \(x(20-x)\) cm\(^2\) and use differentiation to find the greatest possible area.

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{dA}{dx}=20-2x=0\), \(x=10\)

Answer: \(100\) cm\(^2\)

Quick check 2

A square sheet of card of side 12 cm has a square of side \(x\) cm cut from each corner, and the sides are folded up to make an open box. Find the greatest possible volume of the box.

Worked solution
  1. \(x=2\) (see Worked example 2)
  2. \(V=2\times8^2\)

Answer: \(128\) cm\(^3\)

Quick check 3

A farmer has 20 m of fencing to make a rectangular pen against a long straight wall, so that only three sides need fencing. The two sides at right angles to the wall are each \(x\) m long. Find the greatest possible area of the pen.

Worked solution
  1. \(A=x(20-2x)\), \(\dfrac{dA}{dx}=20-4x=0\), \(x=5\)

Answer: \(50\) m\(^2\)
