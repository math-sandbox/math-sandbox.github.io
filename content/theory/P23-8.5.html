8.5 The use of partial fractions in integration

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 8 Further calculus, printed pp.194–196. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook use of partial fractions to integrate rational functions.

Key words: partial fractions, laws of logarithms

Why partial fractions help

A rational function such as \(\dfrac{1}{(x+1)(x+3)}\) cannot be integrated directly, but once it is split into partial fractions each term is a standard integral:

  • \(\displaystyle\int\frac{A}{ax+b}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{A}{a}\ln\lvert ax+b\rvert+c\)
  • \(\displaystyle\int\frac{A}{(ax+b)^2}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\frac{A}{a(ax+b)}+c\)
  • \(\dfrac{Bx+C}{x^2+a^2}\): split into \(\dfrac{Bx}{x^2+a^2}\), which gives a logarithm (8.3), and \(\dfrac{C}{x^2+a^2}\), which gives an inverse tangent (8.2).

If the degree of the numerator is not less than the degree of the denominator, divide first. Combine logarithms with the laws of logarithms to give an exact answer.

Worked example 1: Two linear factors

Coursebook, 8.5, integrating partial fractions. New numbers.

Find \(\displaystyle\int\frac{2}{(x+1)(x+3)}\,\mathrm{d}x\).

  1. \(2=A(x+3)+B(x+1)\) gives \(A=1\) and \(B=-1\). M1
  2. \(\dfrac{2}{(x+1)(x+3)}=\dfrac{1}{x+1}-\dfrac{1}{x+3}\) A1
  3. \(\ln\lvert x+1\rvert-\ln\lvert x+3\rvert+c=\ln\left\lvert\dfrac{x+1}{x+3}\right\rvert+c\) A1

Answer: \(\ln\left\lvert\dfrac{x+1}{x+3}\right\rvert+c\)

Worked example 2: An exact definite integral

Coursebook, 8.5, definite integrals with partial fractions. New numbers.

Find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^1\frac{5x+3}{(x+1)(2x+1)}\,\mathrm{d}x\).

  1. \(5x+3=A(2x+1)+B(x+1)\); \(x=-1\) gives \(A=2\) and \(x=-\frac{1}{2}\) gives \(B=1\). M1
  2. \(\displaystyle\int\Bigl(\frac{2}{x+1}+\frac{1}{2x+1}\Bigr)\mathrm{d}x=2\ln(x+1)+\frac{1}{2}\ln(2x+1)\) A1
  3. \(2\ln2+\frac{1}{2}\ln3\) A1

Answer: \(2\ln2+\frac{1}{2}\ln3\)

Common mistakes

  • Integrating \(\dfrac{1}{2x+1}\) as \(\ln(2x+1)\) instead of \(\frac{1}{2}\ln(2x+1)\).
  • Making a sign error when integrating \(\dfrac{1}{3-x}\): the answer is \(-\ln\lvert3-x\rvert\).
  • Leaving the answer as a sum of logarithms when the question asks for a single logarithm.

Quick check 1

Find the value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^1\frac{1}{(x+1)(x+2)}\,\mathrm{d}x\), correct to 3 significant figures.

Worked solution
  1. \(\ln(x+1)-\ln(x+2)\)
  2. \(\ln2-\ln3+\ln2=\ln\frac{4}{3}\)

Answer: \(0.288\)

Quick check 2

Find the value of \(\displaystyle\int_1^4\frac{2}{x(x+2)}\,\mathrm{d}x\), correct to 3 significant figures.

Worked solution
  1. \(\ln x-\ln(x+2)\)
  2. \(\ln4-\ln6+\ln3=\ln2\)

Answer: \(0.693\)

Quick check 3

Find the value of \(\displaystyle\int_2^3\frac{2}{x^2-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\), correct to 3 significant figures.

Worked solution
  1. \(\ln(x-1)-\ln(x+1)\)
  2. \(\ln\frac{2}{4}-\ln\frac{1}{3}=\ln\frac{3}{2}\)

Answer: \(0.405\)
