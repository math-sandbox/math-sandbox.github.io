5.2 The distribution of sample means

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Probability & Statistics 2 Coursebook (Jayne Kranat), Chapter 5 Sampling, printed pp.106–117. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook results for the mean and variance of the sample mean and the central limit theorem.

Key words: sample mean, central limit theorem, standard error

The sample mean as a random variable

Take a random sample \(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n\) from a population with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\). The sample mean \(\bar{X}=\dfrac{X_1+X_2+\dots+X_n}{n}\) changes from sample to sample, so it is a random variable with

\[E(\bar{X})=\mu,\qquad \mathrm{Var}(\bar{X})=\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}.\]

The standard deviation of \(\bar{X}\), \(\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\), is called the standard error of the mean. Larger samples give sample means that are less spread out.

Exact or approximate?

  • If the population is normal, \(\bar{X}\sim\mathrm{N}\Bigl(\mu, \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\Bigr)\) exactly, for any sample size.
  • If the population is not normal but \(n\) is large (as a guide, \(n\ge 30\)), the central limit theorem says \(\bar{X}\) is approximately \(\mathrm{N}\Bigl(\mu, \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\Bigr)\).

To find a probability, standardise using the standard error: \(z=\dfrac{\bar{x}-\mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}}\).

Worked example 1: Describing the distribution

Coursebook, 5.2, distribution of the sample mean. New numbers.

\(X\) has mean 40 and standard deviation 15. Describe the distribution of the mean of a random sample of 50 values of \(X\).

  1. \(n=50\) is large, so by the central limit theorem \(\bar{X}\) is approximately normal. B1
  2. Mean 40 B1
  3. Variance \(\dfrac{15^2}{50}=4.5\) B1

Answer: \(\bar{X}\sim\mathrm{N}(40, 4.5)\) approximately

Worked example 2: A probability for the sample mean

Coursebook, 5.2, normal population. New numbers.

\(X\sim\mathrm{N}(80, 100)\). A random sample of 25 values is taken. Find \(P(\bar{X}\gt 83)\).

  1. \(\bar{X}\sim\mathrm{N}(80, 4)\), standard deviation 2 B1
  2. \(z=\dfrac{83-80}{2}=1.5\) M1
  3. \(P(\bar{X}\gt 83)=1-\Phi(1.5)=1-0.9332=0.0668\) A1

Answer: \(0.0668\)

Common mistakes

  • Dividing by \(n\) instead of \(\sqrt{n}\) for the standard deviation of \(\bar{X}\).
  • Saying the population must be normal for the central limit theorem to apply. The theorem is needed when the population is not normal.
  • Using \(\sigma\) instead of \(\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\) when standardising a sample mean.

Quick check 1

\(X\) has standard deviation 12. Find the standard deviation of the mean of a random sample of 36 values of \(X\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{12}{\sqrt{36}}=\dfrac{12}{6}\)

Answer: \(2\)

Quick check 2

\(X\) has variance 12. The mean of a random sample of \(n\) values has variance 0.6. Find \(n\).

Worked solution
  1. \(\dfrac{12}{n}=0.6\)
  2. \(n=\dfrac{12}{0.6}\)

Answer: \(n=20\)

Quick check 3

\(X\) has mean 50 and standard deviation 6. For a random sample of 16 values, find the \(z\)-value used for \(P(\bar{X}\gt 52)\).

Worked solution
  1. Standard deviation of \(\bar{X}\) is \(\dfrac{6}{4}=1.5\)
  2. \(z=\dfrac{52-50}{1.5}\)

Answer: \(1.33\) (3 s.f.)
