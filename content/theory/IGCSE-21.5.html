21.5 Proportion

Based on Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics Core and Extended Coursebook (Karen Morrison and Nick Hamshaw), Chapter 21 Ratio, rate and proportion. The wording is original and follows the published section titles and the standard Cambridge IGCSE treatment of the topic. Printed pp.739–744.

Key words: direct proportion, inverse proportion, unitary method

Direct proportion

Two quantities are in direct proportion if they increase or decrease at the same rate: doubling one doubles the other, and their ratio stays the same. The cost of petrol and the number of litres bought are in direct proportion.

The unitary method finds the value for one unit first and then multiplies. If 8 pens cost 12 dollars, one pen costs \(12\div8=1.5\) dollars, so 5 pens cost \(7.5\) dollars.

Inverse proportion

Two quantities are in inverse proportion if one increases as the other decreases at the same rate: doubling one halves the other, and their product stays the same. The number of workers and the time to finish a job are in inverse proportion, if everyone works at the same rate.

Best buys

To compare value for money, work out the cost of one unit (for example, the cost per 100 g) for each option, or the amount you get for one dollar. The option with the lower cost per unit is the better buy.

Worked example 1: Direct proportion

Coursebook, 21.5, the unitary method. New numbers.

A car uses 18 litres of fuel to travel 240 km. How much fuel does it use to travel 400 km?

  1. Fuel per km \(=\dfrac{18}{240}=0.075\) litres M1
  2. \(0.075\times400=30\) litres A1

Answer: 30 litres

Worked example 2: Inverse proportion

Coursebook, 21.5, inverse proportion. New numbers.

A coach trip costs each of 30 passengers 24 dollars. The total cost is shared equally. How much would each person pay if 40 passengers went?

  1. Total cost \(=30\times24=720\) dollars M1
  2. \(720\div40=18\) dollars each A1

Answer: 18 dollars

Common mistakes

  • Treating an inverse proportion as direct: more workers means less time, not more.
  • Rounding the cost of one unit too early. Keep the full value until the last step.
  • Comparing best buys with different units, such as grams and kilograms.

Past paper

0580/23/O/N/12 Q3, official QP and official MS.

Jamie needs 300 g of flour to make 20 cakes.

How much flour does he need to make 12 cakes? [2]

Mark scheme
  1. \(\dfrac{300\times12}{20}\) M1 Official MS: M1 for (300 × 12)/20 oe.
  2. \(=180\) g A1 Official MS: 2 marks for 180.

Past paper

0580/22/O/N/22 Q5, official QP and official MS.

Jenna buys 2.4 m of ribbon and 4.8 m of fabric.

The total cost is $33.48 .

Ribbon costs $0.85 per metre.

Find the cost of 1 m of fabric. [3]

Mark scheme
  1. Ribbon costs \(2.4\times0.85=2.04\) dollars. M1 Official MS: M1 for 2.4 × 0.85.
  2. Fabric costs \(33.48-2.04=31.44\) dollars for 4.8 m, so 1 m costs \(31.44\div4.8\). M1 Official MS: M2 for (33.48 − 2.4 × 0.85) oe, as printed; the division by 4.8 leads to the answer.
  3. \(=6.55\) dollars A1 Official MS: 3 marks for 6.55.

Quick check 1

Jamie needs 300 g of flour to make 20 cakes. How much flour does he need to make 12 cakes?

Worked solution
  1. One cake needs \(300\div20=15\) g.
  2. \(15\times12\)

Answer: 180 g

Quick check 2

It takes 6 builders 10 days to build a wall. How many days would it take 4 builders, working at the same rate?

Worked solution
  1. Total work \(=6\times10=60\) builder-days
  2. \(60\div4\)

Answer: 15 days

Quick check 3

Jo buys 2 m of ribbon and 5 m of fabric for a total of 3420 cents. Ribbon costs 85 cents per metre. Find the cost, in dollars, of 1 m of fabric.

Worked solution
  1. Ribbon costs \(2\times85=170\) cents.
  2. Fabric: \((3420-170)\div5=650\) cents

Answer: 6.50 dollars
