5.6 Trigonometric equations

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 5 Trigonometry, printed pp.140–145 (physical PDF page = printed page + 12). Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook method of solving equations such as \(\sin\theta=k\) in a given interval, and equations that are quadratic in a trigonometric ratio.

Key words: related angle, CAST

Solving \(\sin\theta=k\), \(\cos\theta=k\) and \(\tan\theta=k\)

To solve an equation such as \(\sin\theta=k\) in a given interval, first find a related acute angle \(\alpha\) with \(\sin\alpha=|k|\). Then use CAST (or a sketch of the graph) to find all the angles in the interval that have the same sine, with the correct sign. The same method applies to cosine and tangent, with the signs and the period of each function kept in mind: sine and cosine have period \(360^\circ\) (or \(2\pi\)), and tangent has period \(180^\circ\) (or \(\pi\)).

Equations that are quadratic in a ratio, such as \(8\sin^2\theta+6\cos\theta+1=0\), are reduced to a quadratic in one ratio by using \(\sin^2\theta=1-\cos^2\theta\) (or a similar identity), and then solved as above. A root of the quadratic that lies outside \([-1,1]\) is discarded when the unknown is sine or cosine.

Worked example 1: A sine equation in a given interval

Coursebook, 5.6, related-angle style. New equation \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{1}{2}\) for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le 360^\circ\).

Solve \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{1}{2}\) for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le 360^\circ\).

  1. The related acute angle is \(30^\circ\). Sine is positive in the first and second quadrants, so \(\theta=30^\circ\) or \(\theta=180^\circ-30^\circ=150^\circ\). M1
  2. Both values lie in the given interval, and there are no further solutions in that interval. A1

Answer: \(30^\circ\), \(150^\circ\)

Worked example 2: A quadratic in cosine

Coursebook, 5.6, quadratic-in-a-ratio style. New equation in the same family as the official past paper on this page, with a different constant term so that the numbers are not those of the official question.

Solve \(2\sin^2\theta-3\cos\theta=0\) for \(0^\circ<\theta<180^\circ\).

  1. Replace \(\sin^2\theta\) by \(1-\cos^2\theta\): \(2(1-\cos^2\theta)-3\cos\theta=0\), so \(2\cos^2\theta+3\cos\theta-2=0\). M1
  2. Factorise: \((2\cos\theta-1)(\cos\theta+2)=0\), so \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{1}{2}\) or \(\cos\theta=-2\). The second root is impossible. A1
  3. Hence \(\theta=60^\circ\) in the given interval. A1

Answer: \(60^\circ\)

Common mistakes

  • Finding only the related acute angle, and missing the second solution in the interval.
  • Keeping a cosine or sine value that is greater than \(1\) or less than \(-1\).
  • Giving a solution outside the given interval, or in radians when the question asked for degrees.

Solve the equation \(8\sin^2\theta+6\cos\theta+1=0\) for \(0^\circ<\theta<180^\circ\). [3]

Mark scheme
  1. The identity \(\sin^2\theta=1-\cos^2\theta\) is used, to obtain \(8(1-\cos^2\theta)+6\cos\theta+1=0\), which rearranges to \(8\cos^2\theta-6\cos\theta-9=0\). M1 Official MS: M1, expect \(8\cos^2\theta-6\cos\theta-9=0\).
  2. The quadratic is solved by factors, the formula, or completing the square, and the method is shown: \((4\cos\theta+3)(2\cos\theta-3)=0\). A1
  3. \(\cos\theta=-0.75\), so \(\theta=138.6^\circ\) only. A1 Official MS: AWRT, ignore solutions outside the given range; answer in radians scores A0.

Quick check 1

How many solutions of \(\sin 2\theta=\dfrac{1}{2}\) are there for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le 360^\circ\)?

Worked solution
  1. The equation \(\sin\phi=\dfrac{1}{2}\) has two solutions in each interval of \(360^\circ\). Here \(\phi=2\theta\) runs through two such intervals, so there are \(4\) solutions.

Answer: \(4\)

Quick check 2

How many solutions of \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{1}{2}\) are there for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le 720^\circ\)?

Worked solution
  1. \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{1}{2}\) has two solutions in each interval of \(360^\circ\), and \(720^\circ\) is two such intervals, so there are \(4\) solutions.

Answer: \(4\)

Quick check 3

How many solutions of \(\tan\theta=1\) are there for \(0^\circ\le\theta\le 360^\circ\)?

Worked solution
  1. Tangent has period \(180^\circ\) and one solution in each period, so there are \(2\) solutions.

Answer: \(2\)
