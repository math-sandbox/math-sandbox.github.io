9.2 Finding the constant of integration

Based on Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 1 Coursebook (Sue Pemberton, series editor Julian Gilbey), Chapter 9 Integration, printed pp.244–247. Own wording and new numbers, following the coursebook method of using a point on a curve to find the constant of integration.

Key words: constant of integration, integration, gradient function

Using a point on the curve

Integrating the gradient function \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) gives a family of curves, \(y=\mathrm{F}(x)+c\). If one point on the curve is known, substitute its coordinates to find the constant of integration \(c\). This picks out the one curve that passes through the point.

  • Rewrite the gradient as powers of \(x\).
  • Integrate term by term and include \(+c\).
  • Substitute the given \(x\) and \(y\) and solve for \(c\).
  • Write the final equation \(y=\ldots\) in full.

Worked example 1: A polynomial gradient

Coursebook, 9.2, finding the equation of a curve. New numbers.

A curve has \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=6x^2-4\) and passes through \((1, 3)\). Find its equation.

  1. \(y=2x^3-4x+c\) B1
  2. \(3=2-4+c\) M1
  3. \(c=5\), so \(y=2x^3-4x+5\) A1

Answer: \(y=2x^3-4x+5\)

Worked example 2: A root in the gradient

Coursebook, 9.2, using powers of x. New numbers.

A curve has \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{x}}\) and passes through \((9, 10)\). Find its equation.

  1. \(y=\displaystyle\int2x^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,dx=4x^{\frac{1}{2}}+c\) B1
  2. \(10=4\times3+c\) M1
  3. \(c=-2\), so \(y=4\sqrt{x}-2\) A1

Answer: \(y=4\sqrt{x}-2\)

Common mistakes

  • Substituting the point into \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) instead of into the integrated expression.
  • Leaving out \(+c\) before substituting, so the curve cannot pass through the point.
  • Giving only the value of \(c\) when the question asks for the equation of the curve.

Quick check 1

A curve has \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=6x^2+6x\) and passes through \((1, 10)\). Find the constant term in its equation.

Worked solution
  1. \(y=2x^3+3x^2+c\)
  2. \(10=2+3+c\)

Answer: \(c=5\), \(y=2x^3+3x^2+5\)

Quick check 2

A curve has \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{6}{x^2}\) and passes through \((2, 4)\). Find \(c\) in \(y=-\dfrac{6}{x}+c\).

Worked solution
  1. \(4=-3+c\)

Answer: \(c=7\)

Quick check 3

A curve has \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{x}}\) and passes through \((4, 10)\). Find its equation.

Worked solution
  1. \(y=6\sqrt{x}+c\)
  2. \(10=12+c\)

Answer: \(y=6\sqrt{x}-2\)
